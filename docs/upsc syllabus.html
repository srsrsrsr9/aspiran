Understanding the UPSC Civil Services Examination

The Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) conducts the Civil Services Examination (CSE) to recruit candidates for prestigious services such as the Indian Administrative Service (IAS), Indian Police Service (IPS), Indian Foreign Service (IFS) and Indian Revenue Service (IRS), among others.

The examination evaluates much more than textbook knowledge. It tests a candidate's understanding of society, governance, economics, history, current affairs, analytical thinking, ethical judgement and ability to communicate effectively.

The selection process has three stages. Each stage serves a different purpose and requires a distinct preparation strategy.

STAGE 01

Preliminary Examination

An objective screening examination that tests conceptual clarity, awareness of current affairs, reasoning and aptitude.

STAGE 02

Main Examination

A descriptive examination that evaluates in-depth knowledge, structured writing, critical analysis and subject expertise.

STAGE 03

Personality Test

An interview designed to assess personality, judgement, communication, awareness and suitability for public service.

Stage 1: Preliminary Examination (Prelims)

Prelims is the first stage of the UPSC selection process. It consists of two objective-type papers conducted on the same day. Candidates must qualify in both papers to be considered for the Main Examination.

2 Objective Papers
400 Total Marks
2 hrs Per Paper
33% Minimum CSAT Score

Paper I: General Studies (GS)

General Studies Paper I

Merit-based screening
Maximum Marks: 200 Duration: 2 Hours Questions: 100 Format: MCQ

General Studies Paper I assesses a candidate's understanding of India's history, geography, political system, economy, environment, science and technology, along with important current affairs.

Key Subjects and Topics

  • History: Ancient and Medieval Indian History, Modern Indian History, Indian National Movement and Freedom Struggle.
  • Geography: Physical Geography, Indian Geography, World Geography, climate, natural resources and economic geography.
  • Indian Polity and Governance: Constitution, Parliament, Judiciary, Fundamental Rights, Directive Principles, constitutional bodies and public institutions.
  • Indian Economy: Economic development, banking, inflation, taxation, government budgeting, employment and economic reforms.
  • Environment and Ecology: Biodiversity, ecosystems, conservation, climate change, environmental conventions and sustainable development.
  • Science and Technology: Recent developments in science, space technology, biotechnology, IT and emerging technologies.
  • Current Affairs: Important national and international events, government initiatives and developments relevant to the above subjects.
Our preparation approach: Build strong conceptual foundations, connect static subjects with current affairs, practise topic-wise MCQs and develop effective revision techniques. Regular mock tests help students improve accuracy, speed and examination temperament.

Paper II: Civil Services Aptitude Test (CSAT)

CSAT Paper II

Qualifying Paper
Maximum Marks: 200 Duration: 2 Hours Questions: 80 Minimum Qualifying Marks: 66

CSAT evaluates a candidate's aptitude, reasoning, comprehension and problem-solving abilities. It is designed to test how effectively candidates interpret information and arrive at logical conclusions.

Key Subjects and Topics

  • Reading Comprehension: Understanding passages, identifying central ideas, interpreting arguments and drawing conclusions.
  • Logical Reasoning: Statements, assumptions, conclusions, syllogisms and logical relationships.
  • Analytical Ability: Puzzles, patterns, arrangements and analytical problem-solving.
  • Basic Numeracy: Number systems, percentages, ratios, averages, profit and loss, time and work, and other Class 10-level mathematics.
  • Data Interpretation: Tables, graphs, charts and numerical information.
  • Decision-Making: Problem-solving and decision-making scenarios.
Our preparation approach: Strengthen mathematical fundamentals, practise comprehension passages, develop logical reasoning skills and take timed CSAT practice tests. Students should aim comfortably above the qualifying threshold rather than treating CSAT as an afterthought.
Important examination rule: Both Prelims papers carry 200 marks each. CSAT is qualifying in nature. Candidates must secure at least 33% in CSAT, while the General Studies Paper I score determines the Prelims merit cutoff. One-third of the marks assigned to a question is deducted for an incorrect answer.

Stage 2: Main Examination (Mains)

Mains is the most extensive and academically demanding stage of the Civil Services Examination. Unlike Prelims, it requires candidates to write detailed, structured answers that demonstrate conceptual understanding, critical thinking and the ability to present balanced arguments.

The written examination consists of nine papers. Two are qualifying language papers, while the remaining seven contribute to the Mains merit score.

9 Written Papers
7 Merit Papers
1750 Merit Marks
3 hrs Per Paper

Part A: Qualifying Language Papers

Paper A: Indian Language

Qualifying
Maximum Marks: 300 Duration: 3 Hours

Candidates must select one language from the languages included in the Eighth Schedule of the Constitution of India, subject to the exemptions specified in the UPSC notification.

Key Topics

  • Reading comprehension.
  • Precis writing.
  • Grammar and vocabulary.
  • Short essay writing.
  • Translation between English and the selected Indian language.
Our preparation approach: Improve reading comprehension, written expression, grammar and translation skills through regular language exercises and practice.

Paper B: English

Qualifying
Maximum Marks: 300 Duration: 3 Hours

This paper evaluates a candidate's ability to understand and communicate effectively in English. It focuses on comprehension, vocabulary, grammar and written expression.

Key Topics

  • Comprehension of passages.
  • Precis writing.
  • Grammar and vocabulary.
  • Short essay writing.
Our preparation approach: Develop clarity and accuracy in written communication through comprehension exercises, grammar practice and structured writing.
Note: The two language papers are qualifying in nature. Their marks are not included in the final ranking. Candidates must satisfy the prescribed qualifying standards in both papers.

Part B: Papers Counted for Merit

These seven papers carry a combined total of 1,750 marks and play a decisive role in determining a candidate's Mains performance.

Paper I: Essay

250 Marks
Duration: 3 Hours Descriptive Paper

The Essay paper assesses a candidate's ability to develop ideas, construct logical arguments and communicate complex thoughts in a clear, coherent and persuasive manner.

Common Areas of Essay Preparation

  • Society, education and social justice.
  • Politics, democracy and governance.
  • Economy, development and inclusive growth.
  • Science, technology and innovation.
  • Environment and sustainable development.
  • Philosophy, ethics and contemporary issues.
Our preparation approach: Develop a structured approach to brainstorming, outlining, argument building, use of examples and effective conclusions. Regular timed essay practice helps improve clarity and coherence.

Paper II: General Studies I

250 Marks
Duration: 3 Hours History, Culture, Geography and Society

GS I focuses on India's heritage and culture, historical developments, world geography and the structure and evolution of Indian society.

Key Topics

  • Indian Heritage and Culture: Art forms, architecture, literature and cultural traditions.
  • Modern Indian History: Important events, personalities and developments from the mid-18th century onwards.
  • Freedom Struggle: Different phases of the national movement and contributions from across India.
  • Post-Independence India: Consolidation, reorganisation and important developments.
  • World History: Industrial Revolution, world wars, colonisation, decolonisation and political ideologies.
  • Indian Society: Diversity, women, population, urbanisation, poverty and social empowerment.
  • Geography: Physical geography, natural resources, geographical phenomena and their impact on human activity.
Our preparation approach: Use timelines, maps, case studies, thematic notes and analytical answer-writing exercises to develop a connected understanding of historical and contemporary developments.

Paper III: General Studies II

250 Marks
Duration: 3 Hours Polity, Governance, Social Justice and International Relations

GS II evaluates a candidate's understanding of India's constitutional framework, political institutions, public administration, governance mechanisms and international relations.

Key Topics

  • Indian Constitution: Constitutional framework, amendments, important provisions and basic structure.
  • Political System: Parliament, state legislatures, executive, judiciary and federal structure.
  • Governance: Transparency, accountability, e-governance, citizen charters and public service delivery.
  • Social Justice: Welfare schemes, health, education, poverty, vulnerable sections and social development.
  • Constitutional and Statutory Bodies: Election Commission, CAG, commissions and regulatory institutions.
  • International Relations: India's foreign policy, bilateral relations, regional groupings and international organisations.
Our preparation approach: Connect constitutional concepts with current government policies, landmark judgements and contemporary governance challenges. Practise balanced answers supported by constitutional provisions and relevant examples.

Paper IV: General Studies III

250 Marks
Duration: 3 Hours Economy, Science, Environment, Security and Disaster Management

GS III examines the candidate's understanding of India's economic development, technological progress, environmental challenges, internal security and disaster management.

Key Topics

  • Indian Economy: Growth, development, employment, inclusive growth, budgeting, infrastructure and investment.
  • Agriculture: Cropping patterns, irrigation, agricultural marketing, food security and farm subsidies.
  • Science and Technology: Developments in biotechnology, space, IT, robotics, artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies.
  • Environment: Conservation, pollution, environmental impact assessment, climate change and biodiversity.
  • Internal Security: Cybersecurity, terrorism, border management, organised crime and security challenges.
  • Disaster Management: Disaster preparedness, mitigation, response and recovery mechanisms.
Our preparation approach: Combine conceptual learning with economic data, government reports, current affairs and real-world case studies. Practise using diagrams, flowcharts and structured analytical answers.

Paper V: General Studies IV

250 Marks
Duration: 3 Hours Ethics, Integrity and Aptitude

GS IV focuses on ethical and moral reasoning, integrity, accountability and the values expected of individuals working in public administration.

Key Topics

  • Ethics and Human Values: Ethical principles, human values, attitude and moral reasoning.
  • Emotional Intelligence: Understanding emotions, empathy, self-awareness and decision-making.
  • Integrity and Impartiality: Public service values, objectivity, dedication and accountability.
  • Ethics in Public Administration: Transparency, probity, citizen-centric governance and ethical leadership.
  • Case Studies: Practical ethical dilemmas involving administrative decisions and competing interests.
Our preparation approach: Build a strong understanding of ethical concepts and practise case studies involving real-world administrative dilemmas. Focus on balanced, practical and ethically defensible solutions.

Stage 3: Personality Test (UPSC Interview)

The final stage of the Civil Services Examination is the Personality Test. Candidates who qualify in Mains are invited for an interview conducted by a UPSC board.

Beyond Knowledge: Assessing the Individual

The Personality Test carries 275 marks. It is not simply another academic examination. The objective is to assess a candidate's suitability for a career in public service.

  • Clarity of thought and expression.
  • Critical thinking and analytical ability.
  • Awareness of national and international developments.
  • Leadership, initiative and decision-making.
  • Integrity, honesty and balanced judgement.
  • Interest in public affairs and understanding of society.
  • Ability to respond calmly to challenging questions.

How We Prepare Students for the Interview

Detailed Application Form (DAF) Analysis

Prepare students to discuss their educational background, hometown, work experience, hobbies and other details mentioned in their application.

Current Affairs and General Awareness

Develop the ability to discuss important national and international developments with clarity, perspective and balanced judgement.

Mock Interview Sessions

Simulate the interview environment and provide structured feedback on communication, confidence, body language and response quality.

Personality Development

Strengthen communication, self-awareness, decision-making and the ability to articulate informed opinions.

How Is the Final UPSC Rank Determined?

The final merit list is prepared using the marks obtained in the Main Examination (seven merit papers) and the Personality Test. Prelims marks are not included in the final ranking.

Examination Stage Marks Counted for Final Merit
Preliminary Examination Not counted
Main Examination 1,750
Personality Test 275
Total Final Merit 2,025

Candidates are ranked based on their combined Mains and Interview scores. Service allocation depends on final rank, service preferences, vacancies and applicable eligibility requirements.
