# UPSC Civil Services: Consolidated Facts Picture

Compiled 31 Oct 2026 from our discussion so far. Source of all facts: my knowledge through mid-2026, not a live check. Verify against the current UPSC notification (upsc.gov.in) before publishing anything.

---

## Part 1: Exam structure

### Three stages

| Stage | Nature | Counts toward final rank? |
|---|---|---|
| Prelims | Objective screening, 2 papers, same day | No (cutoff-based gate only) |
| Mains | Descriptive, 9 papers (2 qualifying + 7 merit) | Yes, 1,750 marks |
| Personality Test (Interview) | Board interview | Yes, 275 marks |

Final merit = 1,750 + 275 = **2,025 marks**.

### Prelims

| Paper | Marks | Questions | Duration | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GS Paper I | 200 | 100 MCQ | 2 hrs | Determines the Prelims cutoff |
| CSAT (Paper II) | 200 | 80 MCQ | 2 hrs | Qualifying, minimum 33% (66 marks) |

- Negative marking: one-third of the question's marks deducted per wrong answer.
- Cutoffs have swung noticeably between years, so a safety margin matters.

### Mains

| Paper | Subject | Marks | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Indian Language (Eighth Schedule) | 300 | Qualifying |
| B | English | 300 | Qualifying |
| I | Essay | 250 | Merit |
| II | GS I (Heritage, History, Geography, Society) | 250 | Merit |
| III | GS II (Polity, Governance, Social Justice, IR) | 250 | Merit |
| IV | GS III (Economy, S&T, Environment, Security, Disaster Mgmt) | 250 | Merit |
| V | GS IV (Ethics, Integrity, Aptitude) | 250 | Merit |
| VI | Optional Paper 1 | 250 | Merit |
| VII | Optional Paper 2 | 250 | Merit |

- Each paper is 3 hours.
- Language papers: the other papers are evaluated only if the candidate clears the qualifying marks (25%) in both. Their marks do not count in ranking.
- Essay: two essays, 125 marks each.
- GS I to III: 20 questions each, 10 questions of 10 marks (about 150 words) and 10 questions of 15 marks (about 250 words).
- GS IV leans on case studies.

### Relative weight in the final 2,025

| Component | Marks | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Optional (2 papers) | 500 | about 25% |
| Interview | 275 | about 14% |
| Essay | 250 | about 12% |
| Each GS paper | 250 | about 12% each |
| Prelims | 0 | 0%, but gates everything |

### Four distinct skills the exam tests

```
Prelims GS       -> recall + discrimination (elimination, statement-based MCQs)
CSAT             -> speed and accuracy under a pass mark
Mains / Essay    -> structured argument under word limits
Ethics/Interview -> judgement and self-presentation
```

## Part 2: PYQs (Previous Year Questions)

### What they are
Actual past UPSC papers: Prelims GS and CSAT; Mains Essay, GS 1-4, Optional; language papers.

### Why they matter
- They define the syllabus in practice (the syllabus text is vague; PYQs show real depth).
- They show question style. UPSC rarely repeats questions verbatim but often repeats concepts and framings.
- They calibrate difficulty and time.
- They train technique: elimination and "how many statements are correct" formats for Prelims; directive words (discuss, critically examine, comment) and word limits for Mains.

### Where they fit in the prep cycle
```
Orientation -> skim 1-2 years of papers to see the exam's shape
Foundation  -> topic-wise PYQs right after finishing each topic
Revision    -> re-solve PYQs as a retention check
Mock phase  -> full papers year-wise, timed
Mains       -> PYQs are the core of answer-writing practice
```
Saving PYQs for the end wastes most of their value.

### How many years

| Paper | Suggested coverage |
|---|---|
| Prelims GS | Minimum 10 years, ideally 2011 onward (CSAT introduced) |
| CSAT | 8-10 years, mainly to calibrate the 33% bar |
| Mains GS | 2013 onward (current pattern), about 12 years |
| Essay | All years since 2013 |
| Optional | 10+ years; Optional repeats heavily |

### Sources
- **Authoritative for questions:** upsc.gov.in, Examinations > Previous Question Papers (the only official source for papers).
- **Answers and explanations: no single authoritative source.** UPSC publishes Prelims answer keys only after the cycle concludes, and publishes no model answers for Mains. Everything else is third-party interpretation:
  - Coaching institutes (Vision IAS, Forum IAS, InsightsIAS, Drishti, ClearIAS and others); keys can differ on disputed questions.
  - Publisher compilations (topic-wise, reasonably reliable, often dated).
  - Topper answer copies for Mains (benchmarks, not "correct" answers).

### Sound collection method
1. Download papers from UPSC.
2. Use the official key where available; cross-check unofficial keys on disputed questions.
3. Tag each question by subject, topic and syllabus node.
4. Anchor each answer to a source (NCERT chapter, standard book, government document).

### What aspirants actually need beyond raw questions
Raw questions are free and everywhere, so the value is in:
- Topic-wise classification mapped to syllabus nodes.
- Explanations that say why each wrong option is wrong (especially Prelims).
- Source linkage (concept origin and what to read).
- Cross-year patterns on a topic.
- Current-affairs linkage (many "static" questions have a news hook).
- For Mains: what the directive word demands, plus a model structure.

### Trends and patterns
- Prelims has moved from facts to concepts and application.
- Harder formats: multi-statement, "how many are correct", matching.
- Static meets current: a current event as entry point, an underlying concept tested.
- Subject weights shift: Environment, ecology and economy heavy recently; Polity steady; Ancient and Medieval history generally shrinking, with year-to-year fluctuation.
- Prelims cutoffs are volatile.
- Mains questions are increasingly integrated across governance, economy, technology and security. GS IV rewards structured reasoning over memorisation.
- Treat coaching claims like "X% of the paper came from our test series" as marketing; overlaps are often generic concept matches.

## Part 3: Review of the attached exam page (preview.html)

**Verdict:** facts and skeleton are accurate; framing is generic for a preparation offering.

### Verified correct
- Prelims 2 x 200 marks, 100 and 80 questions, CSAT 33% (66 marks), one-third negative marking.
- Mains arithmetic (1,750 merit marks; language papers 300 each, qualifying), interview 275, total 2,025, Prelims not counted.
- Paper numbering matches UPSC's own.

### Imprecise or incomplete
1. "Merit" is used for both Prelims and Mains; "cutoff-based screening" is cleaner for Prelims.
2. Language papers are understated: failing the qualifying marks (25% in both) means the other papers are not evaluated.
3. "Popular Optional Subjects" includes Economics and Mathematics, which are not commonly chosen. Drop "popular" or list only the common ones.
4. Syllabus paraphrase gaps:
   - GS IV: moral thinkers and philosophers.
   - GS II: role of civil services, pressure groups.
   - GS III: media and social networks, development-extremism linkage.
   - GS I: globalisation, communalism, regionalism, secularism.
   - CSAT: communication and interpersonal skills.
5. Title says "Complete Course Guide" but the page is an exam guide.

### Missing content
- Eligibility, age limits, attempts, annual calendar.
- The funnel: roughly 10+ lakh applicants for about 1,000 posts, with a few thousand reaching the interview.
- Question formats and word limits.
- Relative weights (see Part 1).
- PYQs, answer-writing cycles, revision.

### Page and UX issues
- CTA button links to `#admissions`, the section it sits in (dead link).
- Duplicate heading: "Paper I: General Studies (GS)" directly above a card titled "General Studies Paper I".
- No navigation or table of contents on a very long page.
- Inter font declared but never loaded.

### Bigger issue
Ten near-identical "Our preparation approach" boxes say essentially "build foundations, practise, take mocks." They are not wrong but are indistinguishable from competitors, and some claims (individual feedback, body-language feedback, mentorship) are only credible if the product delivers them. The page is organised by exam structure, while learning happens by skill.
