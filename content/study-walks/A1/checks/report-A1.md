# Report: a1-economy-inflation-targeting

- **What it covers:** Flexible inflation targeting in India. The 2016 amendment to the RBI Act. The Government sets the target in consultation with the RBI (Section 45ZA), and in 2026 it kept 4% with a band of 2 to 6% through March 2031. How the six-member MPC is made up. CPI vs WPI. Headline vs core. Repo, SDF and MSF, and how a rate change passes through to loans. What counts as failure (three consecutive quarters) and the Section 45ZN report, with the 2022 case.
- **Past question:** UPSC Prelims 2023, GS Paper 1, Q22 (csp-2023-gs1 n22). It is about central banks raising rates to fight consumer prices. UPSC key: both statements true, and II explains I.
- **Recent Indian example:** On 5 to 7 October 2026 the MPC raised the repo rate by 25 bps to 5.50% (6-0, stance "calibrated tightening"). It cited a deficient monsoon, El Niño, and onion and sugar spikes. October 2026 CPI was 6.09%, with food at 8.94% and onion up 116% (MoSPI release of 12 November 2026). The script uses this to show that one month above 6% is not a failure.
- **Contested:** Whether to target headline or core inflation, and whether 4% is the right number. Both sides are given. The outcome (headline kept, 4% kept) is stated as the decision. None of it is used in recall.
- **Left out (could not open the source):** the exact date and Gazette number of the 2026 notification (the press says 25 March 2026); the Governor's casting vote and the MPC quorum; the WPI publisher and its new base year; the full statutory text of 45ZA, 45ZB and 45ZN. The PDFs on India Code and rbidocs did not load, so the report contents and MPC structure come from the RBI discussion paper and the NCAER paper hosted by NITI Aayog.
- **TRAGIC skips:** none. Tone: NEUTRAL.
- **Existing notes:** economy.json eco-syl-inflation matches what was opened (target retained to March 2031, CPI base 2024). Nothing stale was found. The casting-vote line could not be re-verified today.
- **Sources** were opened on 20 to 21 November 2026 (the date changed during the work) and are stamped 2026-11-21.

# Report: a1-polity-basic-structure

- **What it covers:** the basic structure doctrine told as a story. It starts with the text and procedure of Article 368, then goes case by case: Shankari Prasad (1951), Sajjan Singh (1964), Golaknath (1967, 6:5, prospective overruling), the 24th Amendment (1971), Kesavananda Bharati (1973), Indira Gandhi v Raj Narain (1975, Art 329A(4), free and fair elections), the 42nd Amendment and Minerva Mills (1980, Art 368(4) and (5) void), and NJAC (2015). It closes with the procedural versus substantive limits.
- **Past question:** csm-2025-gs2 question 12, quoted word for word in English. The Hindi script gives its gist and says so.
- **Recent example:** Dr Balram Singh v Union of India, 25 Nov 2024 (2024 INSC 893). The Court dismissed the challenge to "socialist" and "secular" in the Preamble and held that the amending power extends to the Preamble. It also said amendments can still be tested against the basic structure.
- **Trap:** mixing up which case did what (Golaknath vs Kesavananda), and treating the basic features as a complete list. Shelat and Grover JJ wrote: "These cannot be catalogued but can only be illustrated." This is quoted from the opened judgment.
- **Contested:** whether the doctrine is legitimate. The script gives both sides (critics: the phrase is not in the text, judges define it, and it is open-ended; supporters: a limited amending power needs a guard). No person or party is named. The recall questions test only settled holdings.
- **Left out because no source could be opened:** the 7:6 split in Kesavananda (the script says "majority view of seven judges", as the Raj Narain judgment puts it); that Kesavananda was the largest bench ever; the name of the petitioner's math; where Minerva Mills was located. The Legislative Department's own Constitution PDF would not load (the page needs scripts to render), so the Article 368 text was taken from Indian Kanoon.
