N: Here is a question to carry with you on this walk. If a reformer wants to change society, where should the change begin? At the top, by persuading the powerful? Or at the bottom, with the people who carry the heaviest load?

F: That sounds like a trick question. Can't it be both?

N: It can. But in nineteenth-century India, two very different answers were given. Today we will follow both. One answer came from Calcutta, with Raja Rammohun Roy. The other came from Pune, with Jotirao Phule and Savitribai Phule.

F: Let's start with Pune, then. Why Pune?

N: Because of a school. The year is eighteen forty-eight. In Pune, at a place called Bhide Wada, the Phules opened a school for girls. Savitribai Phule became its headmistress. The NCERT Class Twelve sociology book calls her the first headmistress of the country's first school for girls in Pune.

F: So she was the teacher, not just the founder's wife.

N: Exactly. That is the first thing to fix in your notes. Savitribai is not a side character in Jotirao's story. She taught. She ran the school. The same NCERT book says she gave her life to educating Shudras and Ati-Shudras, and she started a night school for farmers and labourers.

F: Wait, Shudras and Ati-Shudras. Can you explain those words?

N: Sure. Shudras were the labouring castes. Ati-Shudras were those treated as untouchable. Phule used these two words again and again. He wanted both groups to unite against caste discrimination. That idea is the key to everything that follows.

F: Okay. So who was Jotirao Phule, before the school?

N: He was born in eighteen twenty-seven. The NCERT Class Eight history book, Our Pasts Part Three, says he studied in schools set up by Christian missionaries. As he grew up, he formed his own views on the injustice of caste.

F: What were those views?

N: He attacked the claim of Brahmans that they were superior because they were Aryans. Phule turned the argument around. He said the Aryans had come from outside and defeated the people who already lived here. Those defeated people, in his telling, were the so-called low castes. So the land, he argued, really belonged to them.

F: That's a strong claim. Is it history or is it argument?

N: Good question. Treat it as his argument, a way of fighting back. The point for the exam is not whether his history of the Aryans was correct. The point is what he did with it. He gave the labouring castes a story in which they were the true owners of the land, not people born to serve.

F: And this is where Gulamgiri comes in?

N: Yes. In eighteen seventy-three, Phule wrote a book called Gulamgiri. The word means slavery. About ten years earlier, the American Civil War had ended slavery in America. Phule dedicated his book to the Americans who had fought to free slaves.

F: Why would an Indian reformer dedicate a book to Americans?

N: Because he saw a link. He compared the condition of the lower castes in India with the condition of enslaved Black people in America. Caste, for him, was a form of slavery. That was a bold way to say it.

F: And the same year, he started a Samaj, right?

N: Right. Also in eighteen seventy-three, he founded the Satyashodhak Samaj. Satyashodhak means seeker of truth. The NCERT sociology book calls it the Truth Seekers Society, devoted to human rights and social justice for low-caste people. The history book says it spread the message of caste equality.

F: So one year, two big things. A book and a Samaj.

N: Yes. Eighteen seventy-three is a year worth keeping. Gulamgiri and the Satyashodhak Samaj, both in eighteen seventy-three.

F: Now what about Rammohun Roy? Wasn't he doing the same work, just in Bengal?

N: That is exactly the trap we will reach in a minute. First, the facts. Rammohun Roy lived from seventeen seventy-two to eighteen thirty-three. He founded a reform association in Calcutta called the Brahmo Sabha, later known as the Brahmo Samaj. NCERT's Class Eight book, in a box on reform groups, says the Brahmo Samaj was formed in eighteen thirty. It rejected idol worship and drew on the Upanishads.

F: And he's the sati man.

N: He campaigned against sati, the burning of widows. He knew Sanskrit, Persian and several other languages. He used the ancient texts to show that widow burning had no sanction in them. British officials were ready to listen. In eighteen twenty-nine, sati was banned.

F: So his method was to argue from the scriptures.

N: Yes, and later reformers copied that method. Ishwarchandra Vidyasagar used the ancient texts to argue that widows could remarry. A law permitting widow remarriage was passed in eighteen fifty-six.

F: So what's actually different between Rammohun and Phule? Both cared about women. Both wanted change.

N: Here is the difference, in three parts. First, where they stood. NCERT notes that many reformers and members of reform associations came from the upper castes. Rammohun worked from within that world, persuading the educated and the officials. Phule called himself one of the low-caste leaders and spoke for those below.

F: And second?

N: Second, the method. Rammohun and Vidyasagar looked for support inside the sacred texts. Phule did not try to win the argument inside the texts. He questioned the authority behind caste itself.

F: And third?

N: Third, how much they joined together. Phule did not treat women and caste as two separate files. The NCERT book says he extended his criticism of caste to argue against all forms of inequality. He cared about the plight of upper-caste women, the miseries of the labourer, and the humiliation of the low castes. And Savitribai's school put girls and the lower castes in the same classroom of reform.

F: So reform from below, with caste and gender together.

N: That is the line to remember. Reform from below, caste and gender together.

F: Did anyone continue this?

N: Yes. NCERT says this movement was carried into the twentieth century by Doctor B. R. Ambedkar in western India and by E. V. Ramaswamy Naicker in the south.

F: Has this actually come up in the exam?

N: It has, and recently. UPSC Mains twenty twenty-five, General Studies Paper One, question eleven, reads: Mahatma Jotirao Phule's writings and efforts of social reforms touched issues of almost all subaltern classes. Discuss. It carried fifteen marks, with a limit of two hundred and fifty words.

F: Subaltern classes. That's a heavy word.

N: It just means groups kept at the bottom of power. Lower castes, untouchables, women, peasants, labourers. So the question is really asking: show the range. Do not stop at the girls' school. Talk about caste, the labourer, the peasant, women of every caste. And give one or two dated facts: eighteen forty-eight for the school, eighteen seventy-three for Gulamgiri and the Satyashodhak Samaj.

F: Is there anything recent on this? Something from the last couple of years?

N: There is. On eleven April twenty twenty-six, the Government of India began a nationwide commemoration of the two hundredth birth anniversary of Mahatma Jyotiba Phule. A Ministry of Culture release on the Press Information Bureau, the PIB, says it runs for two years, from eleven April twenty twenty-six to eleven April twenty twenty-eight. A High-Level Committee chaired by the Prime Minister guides it.

F: Anything else from that day?

N: Two more things. A release from the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment says that, for the first time, floral tributes to Phule were offered inside the Parliament premises, at Prerna Sthal. And the same day, All India Radio reported that Maharashtra's School Education Department began a year-long plan in all schools, with libraries turned into knowledge centres and books on Phule's life for students.

F: Wait, one release said two years and I've seen one year somewhere.

N: Good catch. The Social Justice ministry's release speaks of year-long celebrations. The Ministry of Culture's release gives the full two-year window. If you write about it, the safe line is: a national commemoration began on eleven April twenty twenty-six.

F: Okay. Now the trap you promised.

N: Here's the trap. Many answers put every nineteenth-century reformer in one basket called the renaissance. Rammohun, Vidyasagar, Dayanand, Phule, all as one wave of enlightened change. That hides the anti-caste strand. Phule was not just one more name in the list. He questioned the very people many other reformers came from. If your answer misses that, it misses the point of the question.

F: And the second trap?

N: Dates of the Samajs. They get mixed up. So keep a simple order. The Brahmo Samaj in Calcutta comes first, in Rammohun's time. NCERT gives eighteen thirty for its formation. The Satyashodhak Samaj comes in eighteen seventy-three. The Arya Samaj, founded by Swami Dayanand Saraswati, comes in eighteen seventy-five. Brahmo, then Satyashodhak, then Arya.

F: So Satyashodhak is just two years before Arya Samaj.

N: Right. That's an easy pair to confuse in a Prelims option. Keep them apart.

N: Let's gather it up. Three things to keep. First, the Phules' work began with education. In eighteen forty-eight they opened a girls' school at Bhide Wada in Pune, and Savitribai Phule was its headmistress. Second, eighteen seventy-three is the Phule year. Jotirao wrote Gulamgiri and founded the Satyashodhak Samaj, and he linked caste to slavery. Third, the Phules' reform was different. It came from below, it questioned caste itself rather than arguing inside the texts, and it joined caste and gender together. Rammohun's Brahmo Samaj worked mostly from the top, through persuasion and scripture.

F: Can we try some questions?

N: Yes. Three quick ones. Question one. In which year did Jotirao Phule write Gulamgiri? Was it eighteen twenty-nine, or eighteen fifty-six, or eighteen seventy-three?

[pause 4s]

N: Eighteen seventy-three. The same year he founded the Satyashodhak Samaj. Eighteen twenty-nine was the sati ban, and eighteen fifty-six the widow remarriage law.

N: Question two. Savitribai Phule was the first headmistress of the country's first school for girls in which city? Was it Calcutta, or Pune, or Madras?

[pause 4s]

N: Pune. The school opened in eighteen forty-eight at Bhide Wada.

N: Question three. To whom did Phule dedicate Gulamgiri? Was it to the Americans who fought to free slaves, or to the British Governor-General, or to the Brahmo Samaj?

[pause 4s]

N: To the Americans who fought to free slaves. He was linking the condition of the lower castes in India to slavery in America.

F: That helps. The order of the Samajs feels clearer now.

N: Good. So, back to the question we began with. Where should reform begin? Rammohun began by persuading the powerful. The Phules began in a classroom in Pune, with girls and with the castes kept at the bottom. Both mattered. But only one of them asked who had built the ladder in the first place. Take your time with it. That is all for this walk.
