Many notes still call the reverse repo the floor for short-term rates. Since twenty twenty-two, it isn't. The R B I steers overnight rates with a corridor around its policy repo rate. The ceiling is the Marginal Standing Facility rate, a quarter point above repo. In April twenty twenty-two, a new floor arrived: the Standing Deposit Facility, a quarter point below, with no collateral needed. The fixed-rate reverse repo stayed in the toolkit, but outside the corridor. As of October twenty twenty-six: repo five point five, deposit facility five point two five, M S F five point seven five percent. Learn the corridor, not just the rate. Your turn. Which facility is now the floor of the R B I's corridor?
