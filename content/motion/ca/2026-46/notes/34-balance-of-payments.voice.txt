From April to June twenty twenty-six, India imported eighty-six billion dollars more goods than it exported. Yet its current account deficit was just four point two billion: half a percent of G D P. Why? The current account also counts services and transfers. Net services brought in fifty-one point six billion; remittance receipts, forty-two point nine billion. The other side records investment: textbooks call it the capital account; R B I data calls it the financial account. That quarter, direct investment came in, while portfolio money flowed out. Goods tell half the story. Services and remittances tell the rest. Your turn. Which account records remittances from abroad?
