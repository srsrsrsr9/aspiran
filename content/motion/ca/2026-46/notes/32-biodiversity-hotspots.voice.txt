A biodiversity hotspot isn't just rich in species. It's defined by what it has lost. To qualify, a region needs at least fifteen hundred plant species found nowhere else, and must have lost at least seventy percent of its original vegetation. There are thirty-six worldwide. Four reach into India: the Himalaya, Indo-Burma, the Western Ghats, and Sundaland, through the Nicobar Islands. But a hotspot is a conservation label, not a law. Legal protection comes from the Wild Life Protection Act: national parks, sanctuaries, conservation reserves and community reserves. In a national park, grazing is not allowed. In a sanctuary, the Chief Wildlife Warden can permit some rights. Rich, and threatened, by definition. Your turn. Which hotspot covers the Nicobar Islands?
