=== When Union and State laws clash, the Union usually wins. Usually. (NEUTRAL) ===
When a Union law and a State law clash, the Union usually wins. Usually.
Article two forty-six splits law-making into three lists: Union, State, and Concurrent.
A subject on no list belongs to Parliament, under Article two forty-eight.
If a State law clashes with Parliament's law on a Concurrent subject, the State law is void to that extent.
Unless the President assents to it. Then it prevails in that State, until Parliament legislates again.
And Article three fifty-six, President's Rule? In Bommai, in nineteen ninety-four, nine judges made it open to judicial review, with majority tested on the floor.
The lists divide power. The courts guard the balance.
