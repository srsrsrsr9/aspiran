Parliament keeps writing rules for tribunals. The Supreme Court keeps striking them down. In nineteen ninety-seven, in L. Chandra Kumar, seven judges held that High Court review is part of the basic structure. Tribunal rulings stay open to it. In twenty twenty-one, an ordinance set a four-year term, and a minimum age of fifty. In July, the Court struck both down. Then the Tribunals Reforms Act brought them back, and abolished several appellate tribunals. In November twenty twenty-five, the Court struck them down again: Parliament had re-enacted what it had already invalidated. It ordered a National Tribunals Commission within four months. The fight is about independence. Your turn. What did the Tribunals Reforms Act do?
