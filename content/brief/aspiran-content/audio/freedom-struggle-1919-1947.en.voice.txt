--- part 1 ---
India's freedom struggle, part two: from nineteen nineteen to nineteen forty-seven.

Non-Cooperation and Khilafat.

Welcome back. This is the second of two talks on India's freedom struggle. Part one ended in nineteen nineteen, with Jallianwala Bagh and the dyarchy reforms. Now we follow the story to independence in nineteen forty-seven.

After the First World War, many Indian Muslims were troubled by the treatment of the Ottoman Sultan, who was regarded by many as the Khalifa, a religious head. The Khilafat movement grew around this concern, led by figures such as the Ali brothers, Mohammad Ali and Shaukat Ali. Gandhi saw a chance to bring Hindus and Muslims together in one struggle.

In nineteen twenty, the Congress accepted Gandhi's programme of non-cooperation. The special session at Calcutta in September approved it, and the Nagpur session in December confirmed it. The idea was simple: British rule in India rested on Indian cooperation, so if Indians withdrew that cooperation, the rule would weaken.

People returned titles. Students left government schools and colleges, and national institutions such as the Jamia Millia Islamia and the Kashi Vidyapith were set up. Lawyers gave up practice. Foreign cloth was boycotted, and khadi spread. Councils were boycotted. The movement reached towns and villages, and many peasants and workers joined in their own ways.

--- part 2 ---
In February nineteen twenty-two, at Chauri Chaura in the United Provinces, a clash between a crowd and the police ended with a police station set on fire and policemen killed. Gandhi, who held non-violence as central, called off the movement. Some leaders were disappointed, but Gandhi felt the people needed more preparation in non-violent methods. Soon after, he was arrested and imprisoned.

The years between, and the call for Purna Swaraj.

We are now in the second part, the mid and late nineteen twenties.

With the movement withdrawn, some leaders, led by Chittaranjan Das and Motilal Nehru, formed the Swaraj Party in nineteen twenty-three. They chose to enter the councils and oppose the government from within. Others, called the no-changers, focused on Gandhi's constructive programme: khadi, village work, Hindu-Muslim unity and the removal of untouchability.

These years also saw new currents. Revolutionary groups such as the Hindustan Republican Association, later the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association, became active. Bhagat Singh and his comrades are remembered from this period. Left-wing ideas spread among workers and youth. Peasant movements grew in several regions.

In nineteen twenty-seven, the British government appointed the Simon Commission to review the reforms. It had no Indian member. The Commission arrived in nineteen twenty-eight and was met everywhere with black flags and the slogan Simon, go back.

--- part 3 ---
In Lahore, Lala Lajpat Rai was injured in a police lathi charge during a protest, and he died soon after.

Indians answered the charge that they could not agree on a constitution. A committee led by Motilal Nehru drafted the Nehru Report in nineteen twenty-eight, proposing dominion status and a list of rights. It did not satisfy all groups, and debates over representation continued.

In December nineteen twenty-nine, at the Lahore session, with Jawaharlal Nehru as president, the Congress declared complete independence, Purna Swaraj, as its goal. The twenty-sixth of January, nineteen thirty, was celebrated as Independence Day across the country. That is why, years later, the Constitution came into force on the twenty-sixth of January.

Salt, talks and the nineteen thirty-five Act.

Let us move to the third part, the nineteen thirties.

On the twelfth of March, nineteen thirty, Gandhi set out from Sabarmati Ashram with a group of followers. They walked to Dandi on the Gujarat coast, reaching it in early April. There, Gandhi picked up a handful of salt, breaking the law that gave the government a monopoly over salt. Salt was a clever choice. Every household used it, rich or poor, so the protest made sense to everyone.

This began the Civil Disobedience movement. Unlike Non-Cooperation, it asked people not only to refuse cooperation but to break specific laws. Salt laws were broken along the coasts, forest laws in some regions, and people refused to pay certain taxes.

--- part 4 ---
Women took part in large numbers. In the northwest, Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan led the Khudai Khidmatgars, a non-violent movement.

The government arrested tens of thousands. Meanwhile, a series of Round Table Conferences met in London to discuss constitutional reform. The Congress stayed away from the first. In March nineteen thirty-one, the Gandhi-Irwin Pact was signed. Gandhi agreed to suspend the movement and attend the second conference, and the government agreed to release many prisoners. The second conference, later in nineteen thirty-one, ended without agreement, especially on the question of representation for minorities.

In nineteen thirty-two, the British announced the Communal Award, which extended separate electorates to the depressed classes. Gandhi, in prison at Yerwada, began a fast against this part of the award. After talks, Gandhi and Dr B. R. Ambedkar agreed to the Poona Pact in September nineteen thirty-two. It replaced separate electorates for the depressed classes with reserved seats in joint electorates, and in fact increased the number of seats reserved for them.

Then came the Government of India Act of nineteen thirty-five. It was long and detailed. It ended dyarchy in the provinces and gave them provincial autonomy. It proposed an all-India federation including the princely states, though that federation never came into being. It set up a Federal Court. Many features of this Act later shaped our Constitution.

--- part 5 ---
Elections under the Act were held in early nineteen thirty-seven. The Congress formed ministries in most provinces. They worked for about two years.

War, Quit India and the INA.

We are now in the fourth part, the Second World War years.

In September nineteen thirty-nine, the Viceroy declared India at war without consulting Indian leaders. In protest, the Congress ministries resigned. In March nineteen forty, at its Lahore session, the Muslim League passed a resolution calling for independent states in the Muslim-majority areas of the northwest and east. This is often called the Lahore Resolution.

The British made offers. The August Offer of nineteen forty was not accepted by the Congress. Gandhi then began individual satyagraha, in which chosen individuals made anti-war speeches and courted arrest. Vinoba Bhave was the first.

In March nineteen forty-two, the Cripps Mission came with a promise of dominion status after the war, and a constitution-making body. But it allowed provinces to stay out, and it gave little real power during the war. The Congress rejected it.

On the eighth of August, nineteen forty-two, in Bombay, the Congress passed the Quit India resolution. Gandhi gave the call Do or Die. Within hours, the top leaders were arrested. Yet the movement spread, largely without central direction. Students, workers and peasants joined.

--- part 6 ---
Parallel governments were set up for a time in places such as Ballia in the United Provinces, Tamluk in Bengal and Satara in Maharashtra. Leaders like Aruna Asaf Ali, Jayaprakash Narayan and Usha Mehta, who ran an underground radio, became well known. The movement was put down by force, but it showed that British rule could not last long.

Outside India, Subhas Chandra Bose took a different path. He had been Congress president in nineteen thirty-eight and nineteen thirty-nine, but later left the Congress. He reached Southeast Asia and led the Indian National Army, also called the Azad Hind Fauj. In October nineteen forty-three, he announced a Provisional Government of Free India in Singapore. The army took part in campaigns near the northeast frontier. After the war, the trials of its officers at the Red Fort in nineteen forty-five stirred huge public sympathy across the country. In February nineteen forty-six, ratings of the Royal Indian Navy in Bombay also rose in revolt. These events showed that the loyalty of the armed forces could no longer be taken for granted.

The road to independence, and a recap.

We are in the last part.

In nineteen forty-five and forty-six, elections were held. The Congress won most general seats, and the Muslim League won most Muslim seats. In March nineteen forty-six, the British Cabinet Mission arrived. It proposed a united India with a weak centre and groups of provinces, and a Constituent Assembly.

--- part 7 ---
There was early acceptance, but disagreements followed over how the plan would work.

On the sixteenth of August, nineteen forty-six, the Muslim League called for Direct Action Day. It was followed by terrible communal violence in Calcutta, and later in other places. An interim government led by Jawaharlal Nehru took office in September nineteen forty-six. The Constituent Assembly first met on the ninth of December, nineteen forty-six.

In February nineteen forty-seven, the British Prime Minister, Clement Attlee, announced that Britain would leave India by June nineteen forty-eight. Lord Mountbatten became the Viceroy. On the third of June, nineteen forty-seven, he announced a plan for partition and transfer of power. The British Parliament passed the Indian Independence Act in July. On the fifteenth of August, nineteen forty-seven, India became independent. Pakistan had become a separate dominion the day before.

Independence came with great human suffering. Partition displaced many millions of people, and large numbers lost their lives in violence on both sides. Remembering that cost is part of understanding this history honestly.

Now a recap. Non-Cooperation, from nineteen twenty, brought the masses into the movement, and ended after Chauri Chaura. The Simon Commission was boycotted, and the Lahore session of nineteen twenty-nine declared Purna Swaraj. The Salt March of nineteen thirty began Civil Disobedience.

--- part 8 ---
The Poona Pact of nineteen thirty-two settled the question of reserved seats. The Act of nineteen thirty-five gave provincial autonomy. Quit India in nineteen forty-two, the Indian National Army trials and the naval revolt showed that British rule was ending. And the Cabinet Mission, the Mountbatten Plan and the Independence Act led to freedom on the fifteenth of August, nineteen forty-seven.

Across both talks, notice one pattern: periods of mass struggle alternated with periods of talks and building. Each phase prepared the ground for the next.
