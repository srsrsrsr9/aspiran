The Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers.
Article 74: there is a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the President. The 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 made this advice binding on the President. The 44th Amendment Act, 1978 allowed the President to send the advice back once for reconsideration; the President must act on the advice given after reconsideration.
Article 75(1): the Prime Minister is appointed by the President, and other ministers are appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister.
Article 75(1A), added by the 91st Amendment Act, 2003: the total number of ministers, including the Prime Minister, cannot exceed 15 percent of the total number of members of the Lok Sabha.
Article 75(3): the Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha. It is not collectively responsible to the Rajya Sabha.
Article 75(5): a minister who is not a member of either House of Parliament for six consecutive months ceases to be a minister at the end of that period.
Article 78: the Prime Minister must communicate to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers about the administration of the Union and proposals for legislation, and give information the President asks for.
In the states, Article 164(1A) caps the Council of Ministers at 15 percent of the total members of the Legislative Assembly, with a minimum of 12 ministers including the Chief Minister.
