Acts that shaped the Constitution, before 1947.
Regulating Act, 1773: the first step by the British Parliament to control the East India Company in India. The Governor of Bengal became the Governor-General of Bengal; Warren Hastings was the first. It provided for a Supreme Court at Calcutta, set up in 1774.
Charter Act, 1833: the Governor-General of Bengal became the Governor-General of India; Lord William Bentinck was the first. It ended the Company's commercial activities.
Government of India Act, 1858: after the revolt of 1857, the Crown took over the government of India from the Company. It created the post of Secretary of State for India, and the Governor-General also became the Viceroy.
Indian Councils Act, 1909 (the Morley-Minto Reforms): introduced separate electorates for Muslims.
Government of India Act, 1919 (the Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms): introduced dyarchy in the provinces, and a bicameral legislature at the Centre.
Government of India Act, 1935: provided for an All-India Federation (which never came into being), provincial autonomy in place of dyarchy in the provinces, dyarchy at the Centre, and a Federal Court, set up in 1937. Many provisions of the Constitution draw on this Act.
