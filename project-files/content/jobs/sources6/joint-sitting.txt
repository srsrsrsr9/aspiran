Joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament (Article 108).
The President may summon a joint sitting of the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha when a bill passed by one House is rejected by the other; or the Houses disagree on amendments to the bill; or more than six months pass from the date the other House receives the bill without it being passed.
A joint sitting is not available for a Money Bill. On a Money Bill the Lok Sabha prevails: the Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations, within 14 days (Article 109).
A joint sitting is also not available for a Constitution Amendment Bill. Under Article 368, each House must pass such a bill separately by a special majority.
The Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides over a joint sitting (Article 118(4)). In the Speaker's absence, the Deputy Speaker presides, and in the Deputy Speaker's absence, the Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
At a joint sitting, the bill is passed by a majority of the total number of members of both Houses present and voting. Because the Lok Sabha has more members, it usually prevails.
Only three bills have gone to a joint sitting: the Dowry Prohibition Bill (1961), the Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill (1978), and the Prevention of Terrorism Bill (2002).
