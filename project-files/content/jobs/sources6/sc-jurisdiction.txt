The Supreme Court's jurisdiction, article by article.
Original jurisdiction (Article 131): disputes between the Government of India and one or more states, or between states. Such a dispute can begin only in the Supreme Court.
Writ jurisdiction (Article 32): any person can move the Supreme Court directly for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights. The Court can issue writs such as habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo warranto.
Appellate jurisdiction: appeals from High Courts in constitutional cases (Article 132), civil cases (Article 133) and criminal cases (Article 134).
Special leave to appeal (Article 136): the Supreme Court may, in its discretion, grant special leave to appeal from any judgment or order of any court or tribunal in India, except a court or tribunal of the armed forces.
Advisory jurisdiction (Article 143): the President may refer a question of law or fact of public importance to the Supreme Court for its opinion. The opinion is not binding on the President.
Review of its own judgments (Article 137).
Court of record (Article 129): its judgments are records of lasting value, and it can punish for contempt of itself.
Law declared by the Supreme Court is binding on all courts in India (Article 141).
Complete justice (Article 142): the Supreme Court may pass any decree or order necessary for doing complete justice in any case before it.
Transfer of cases (Article 139A): it can transfer cases between High Courts, or to itself.
Judges of the Supreme Court retire at 65 (Article 124(2)). Since the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Act, 2019, the sanctioned strength is 34, including the Chief Justice of India.
