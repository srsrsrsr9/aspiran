The President of India and the Governor of a state, compared.
Office: The President is provided for in Article 52. Every state has a Governor under Article 153. The 7th Amendment Act, 1956 allows one person to be Governor of two or more states.
How they get the post: The President is elected indirectly by an electoral college of the elected members of both Houses of Parliament and the elected members of the state Legislative Assemblies, including the Legislative Assemblies of Delhi and Puducherry (added by the 70th Amendment Act, 1992). The Governor is not elected. The President appoints the Governor by warrant under his hand and seal (Article 155).
Minimum age: 35 years for both.
Term: The President holds office for five years from the date of entering office (Article 56). The Governor's term is also five years, but the Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President (Article 156), so can be removed earlier.
Removal: The President can be removed only by impeachment by Parliament for violation of the Constitution (Article 61). There is no impeachment for a Governor; the Governor holds office at the President's pleasure.
Pardoning power: The President's power under Article 72 covers sentences by court martial and the death sentence. The Governor's power under Article 161 covers offences against state laws. A Governor cannot pardon a death sentence, though the Governor can suspend, remit or commute it, and has no power over court-martial sentences.
Ordinances: The President can issue ordinances under Article 123 when either House of Parliament is not in session. The Governor can issue ordinances under Article 213 when the state legislature is not in session. An ordinance must be laid before the legislature and ceases to operate six weeks after it reassembles, unless approved earlier.
Bills: Under Article 111 the President can give assent, withhold assent, or return a bill (other than a Money Bill) for reconsideration. Under Article 200 the Governor can give assent, withhold assent, return a bill (other than a Money Bill), or reserve it for the consideration of the President.
Discretion: The President acts on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers (Article 74). The Governor also acts on ministerial advice, but Article 163 refers to functions the Governor exercises "in his discretion".
