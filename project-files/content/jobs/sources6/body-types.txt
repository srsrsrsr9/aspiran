Constitutional, statutory and executive bodies.
A constitutional body is created by the Constitution itself. A statutory body is created by an Act of Parliament. An executive body is created by a government resolution or order, with no Act behind it.
Constitutional bodies: the Election Commission of India (Article 324); the Union Public Service Commission (Article 315); the Finance Commission (Article 280); the Comptroller and Auditor General (Article 148); the National Commission for Scheduled Castes (Article 338); the National Commission for Scheduled Tribes (Article 338A, added by the 89th Amendment Act, 2003); the National Commission for Backward Classes (Article 338B, added by the 102nd Amendment Act, 2018); the GST Council (Article 279A, added by the 101st Amendment Act, 2016).
Statutory bodies: the National Human Rights Commission (Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993); the Central Information Commission (Right to Information Act, 2005); the Central Vigilance Commission (Central Vigilance Commission Act, 2003; it was first set up in 1964 by a government resolution); the Lokpal (Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013); the National Green Tribunal (National Green Tribunal Act, 2010); the National Disaster Management Authority (Disaster Management Act, 2005); the National Commission for Women (National Commission for Women Act, 1990).
Executive bodies: NITI Aayog, set up by a Union Cabinet resolution on 1 January 2015, replacing the Planning Commission, which was also set up by a resolution, in 1950.
The National Commission for Backward Classes was first a statutory body under a 1993 Act; the 102nd Amendment made it a constitutional body.
