The Preamble of the Constitution of India.
It opens with "We, the people of India", which shows that the Constitution draws its authority from the people.
It describes India as a Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic.
It sets out four objectives: Justice (social, economic and political); Liberty of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship; Equality of status and of opportunity; and Fraternity, assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation.
The Preamble is based on the Objectives Resolution, which Jawaharlal Nehru moved in the Constituent Assembly on 13 December 1946. The Assembly adopted the resolution on 22 January 1947.
The Constitution, with its Preamble, was adopted on 26 November 1949.
The Preamble has been amended only once, by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976. It added the words "Socialist", "Secular" and "Integrity".
In the Berubari Union case (1960), the Supreme Court said the Preamble is not a part of the Constitution. In the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973), the Supreme Court held that the Preamble is a part of the Constitution.
The Preamble is not enforceable in a court of law. Courts use it to interpret the Constitution where the text is unclear.
In November 2024, the Supreme Court dismissed petitions that challenged the addition of "Socialist" and "Secular" by the 42nd Amendment.
