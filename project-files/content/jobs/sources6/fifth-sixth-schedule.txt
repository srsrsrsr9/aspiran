The Fifth and Sixth Schedules: two ways the Constitution protects tribal areas.
The Fifth Schedule (Article 244(1)) deals with the administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in any state other than Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram. The President declares an area to be a Scheduled Area. Each state with Scheduled Areas has a Tribes Advisory Council of not more than 20 members, of whom about three-fourths are Scheduled Tribe members of the state Legislative Assembly. The Governor can direct that an Act of Parliament or the state legislature shall not apply to a Scheduled Area, or shall apply with changes, and can make regulations for peace and good government there.
Ten states have Fifth Schedule areas: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha and Rajasthan.
The Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA) extends Part IX on panchayats to Fifth Schedule areas, with powers for the gram sabha.
The Sixth Schedule (Article 244(2)) applies to tribal areas in four states: Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram. These areas are run by Autonomous District Councils, which can make laws on subjects such as land, forests, inheritance and marriage, and can run courts for disputes between tribal people. A district council has up to 30 members, of whom up to four are nominated by the Governor and the rest are elected. There are ten Autonomous District Councils in the four states.
In short: the Fifth Schedule relies on the Governor and a Tribes Advisory Council; the Sixth Schedule gives elected councils their own law-making powers.
