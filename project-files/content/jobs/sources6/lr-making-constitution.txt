How India's Constitution was made, 1946 to 1950.
The Constituent Assembly was set up under the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946. Its members were not directly elected by the people. They were elected indirectly by the members of the provincial legislative assemblies in July and August 1946, and the princely states were to nominate their representatives.
The planned total strength was 389: 296 seats for British India and 93 for the princely states. After Partition, members from the areas that went to Pakistan left, and the strength fell to 299.
The Assembly first met on 9 December 1946. Dr Sachchidananda Sinha, the oldest member, was the temporary president. On 11 December 1946 Dr Rajendra Prasad was elected its president.
On 13 December 1946 Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution, which set out the aims of the Constitution. The Assembly adopted it on 22 January 1947. Its ideas later became the Preamble.
The Assembly worked through many committees. The Drafting Committee was set up on 29 August 1947, with Dr B.R. Ambedkar as its chairman and seven members in all. Sir B.N. Rau was the constitutional adviser to the Assembly and prepared an early draft.
The Assembly also adopted the national flag on 22 July 1947. From 1947 it also worked as the country's legislature (the Dominion Parliament) when it met in that role, alongside its work of making the Constitution.
The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949. The Assembly took almost three years: 2 years, 11 months and 18 days, over 11 sessions. Since 2015, 26 November is observed as Constitution Day.
Some provisions, such as those on citizenship, elections and the provisional Parliament, took effect on 26 November 1949 itself. The rest came into force on 26 January 1950, the day chosen because on 26 January 1930 the Congress had celebrated Purna Swaraj (complete independence) Day after the Lahore session resolution of 1929.
The Assembly held its final session on 24 January 1950. On that day members signed the Constitution, adopted Jana Gana Mana as the national anthem and Vande Mataram as the national song, and elected Dr Rajendra Prasad as the first President of India.
As adopted, the Constitution had a Preamble, 395 articles in 22 parts, and 8 schedules.
Criticisms and responses. Critics said the Assembly was not a representative body because it was not elected by universal adult franchise; that one party, the Congress, dominated it; that lawyers dominated it, making the text long and legalistic; and that it borrowed heavily from the Government of India Act, 1935 and from foreign constitutions. Those who defend it say the Assembly included members from many communities, regions and views, including members outside the Congress; that it worked by long debate and consensus, and its debates are published; and that borrowed ideas were adapted to Indian conditions rather than copied.
