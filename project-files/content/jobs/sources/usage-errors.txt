Common errors in English usage, with corrections, for Paper B practice:
1. Wrong: One of my friends are preparing for the examination. Right: One of my friends is preparing for the examination. (The subject is "one", which is singular.)
2. Wrong: He is senior than me in service. Right: He is senior to me in service. ("Senior", "junior", "superior" and "inferior" take "to", not "than".)
3. Wrong: Each of the students have submitted the form. Right: Each of the students has submitted the form. ("Each" takes a singular verb.)
4. Wrong: I am knowing the answer. Right: I know the answer. (Verbs of knowing and believing are not usually used in the continuous form.)
5. Wrong: Despite of the rain, the meeting was held. Right: Despite the rain, the meeting was held. ("Despite" is not followed by "of"; "in spite of" is.)
6. Wrong: We discussed about the new policy. Right: We discussed the new policy. ("Discuss" takes a direct object without "about".)
