Editor's source notes: three simple calming exercises.

Slow breathing. Breathing slowly, at about six breaths a minute, is a common relaxation exercise. One way is to breathe in through the nose for about 4 seconds and out for about 6 seconds, without forcing. Some people like a short pause of 1 or 2 seconds at the top. A few minutes is enough; there is no need to breathe deeply or hold the breath long. A 2023 study by Balban and colleagues at Stanford found that a few minutes a day of breathing exercises was linked to better mood and lower stress over a month, with the slow-exhale exercise doing especially well.

The double-inhale sigh (sometimes called the physiological sigh). Breathe in through the nose, then take a second, shorter breath in on top of the first, then breathe out slowly and fully through the mouth. Repeat two or three times, then breathe normally. In the Stanford study, this "cyclic sighing" was the exercise linked to the largest improvement in mood.

Grounding (5-4-3-2-1). Notice five things you can see, four things you can feel or touch, three things you can hear, two things you can smell, and one thing you can taste. Go slowly; there are no wrong answers. It brings attention to the present moment and the room around you. It needs no equipment and can be done with eyes open in a hostel room or a library.

These are simple exercises, not treatments. Anyone who feels unsafe or overwhelmed should talk to someone: in India, Tele-MANAS (14416) is a free helpline open at all hours.
