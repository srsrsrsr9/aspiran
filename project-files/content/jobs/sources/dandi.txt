On 12 March 1930, Mahatma Gandhi set out from the Sabarmati Ashram in Ahmedabad with 78 followers.
They walked about 385 kilometres over 24 days, through villages where crowds gathered to hear him.
They reached Dandi, on the coast of Gujarat, on 5 April 1930.
On the morning of 6 April, Gandhi picked up a lump of natural salt, breaking the British salt law, which taxed salt and made its production a government monopoly.
The march launched the Civil Disobedience Movement. Salt laws were broken along the coast, and thousands were arrested.
It drew wide attention in India and abroad.
