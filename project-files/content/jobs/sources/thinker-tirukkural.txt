Editor's source notes: Tirukkural 391.

The Tirukkural is a Tamil classic of 1,330 couplets, called kurals, attributed to Thiruvalluvar. Scholars date it variously, often to somewhere between about 300 BCE and 500 CE. It is arranged in three books, on virtue (aram), wealth and statecraft (porul), and love (inbam). The book on porul covers kingship, ministers, learning and the qualities of good administration, which is why it is often compared with Kautilya's Arthashastra.

Kural 391 opens the chapter on learning (chapter 40). In plain words it says: learn well, and without flaw, whatever is worth learning, and after learning it, live by it. The point is that learning is complete only when it shows in conduct.

Why it matters for the exam: GS Paper IV asks for the contributions of moral thinkers from India, and the Tirukkural gives short, exact lines on learning, self-discipline and the ethics of public office. The couplet fits answers on integrity (the match between what one knows and what one does), on the gap between knowing and doing in public life, and on lifelong learning for civil servants. In an essay on education, it makes a clear, non-partisan closing line.
