Editor's source notes: Charles Darwin's working day at Down House, from Francis Darwin's reminiscences in The Life and Letters of Charles Darwin (1887, public domain).

Charles Darwin (1809 to 1882) was an English naturalist whose On the Origin of Species was published in 1859. He lived and worked at Down House in Kent for about forty years, and his health was often poor. His son Francis described his father's day.

He rose early and took a short walk before breakfast.
After breakfast, from about eight to half past nine, he worked; Francis calls this one of his best times of the day for work.
At about half past nine he went to the drawing-room and read his letters, then lay on the sofa while family letters, and then part of a novel, were read aloud to him.
From about half past ten until twelve or a quarter past, he worked again; Francis says he then considered his day's work over.
At noon he went for a walk, including a round of the Sandwalk, a gravel path at the edge of his grounds.
After lunch he read the newspaper and wrote his letters.
In the afternoon he rested, and a novel was read aloud to him.
Around four he walked again, and then worked for about an hour.
In the evening, after dinner, he played backgammon with his wife Emma, read scientific books, and listened to music or reading aloud, going to bed at about half past ten.

Francis notes that his father did very little work after the morning and early afternoon, and that his poor health made long hours impossible.
