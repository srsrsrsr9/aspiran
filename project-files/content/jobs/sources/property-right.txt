Before 1978, the right to property was a Fundamental Right, under Article 19(1)(f) (to acquire, hold and dispose of property) and Article 31 (compulsory acquisition of property).
The 44th Amendment Act, 1978 removed the right to property from the list of Fundamental Rights. It is now a constitutional (legal) right under Article 300A, in Part XII, which says that no person shall be deprived of property save by authority of law.
The change followed years of litigation over land reforms and the nationalisation of banks, in which property claims were used to challenge laws.
Effect: a person can no longer go directly to the Supreme Court under Article 32 for the right to property; they can approach a High Court under Article 226 or go to an ordinary court.
