Sun Tzu, The Art of War, Chapter 3, "Attack by Stratagem", verses 17 and 18, in the translation by Lionel Giles (1910), which is in the public domain. The editor has to check the wording against the 1910 text before recording.

17. Thus we may know that there are five essentials for victory: (1) He will win who knows when to fight and when not to fight. (2) He will win who knows how to handle both superior and inferior forces. (3) He will win whose army is animated by the same spirit throughout all its ranks. (4) He will win who, prepared himself, waits to take the enemy unprepared. (5) He will win who has military capacity and is not interfered with by the sovereign.

18. Hence the saying: If you know the enemy and know yourself, you need not fear the result of a hundred battles. If you know yourself but not the enemy, for every victory gained you will also suffer a defeat. If you know neither the enemy nor yourself, you will succumb in every battle.

About the text: The Art of War is an ancient Chinese text on strategy traditionally attributed to Sun Tzu. Lionel Giles, a British sinologist, published his English translation in 1910.
For an aspirant, the passage is worth hearing for its idea of knowing yourself and knowing the task, and of choosing when to act. It is not about treating other people as enemies.
