Editor's source notes: Benjamin Franklin's daily scheme, from his Autobiography (written 1771 to 1790, public domain).

Benjamin Franklin (1706 to 1790) was an American printer, writer, scientist and statesman. In his Autobiography he describes a plan he drew up in his twenties for organising each day, as part of a larger project to build thirteen virtues, including order. He set out a daily scheme as a table of hours.

The morning question: "What good shall I do this day?"
5 to 7: rise, wash, address Powerful Goodness; contrive the day's business and take the resolution of the day; prosecute the present study; and breakfast.
8 to 11: work.
12 to 1: read, or overlook my accounts, and dine.
2 to 5: work.
6 to 9: put things in their places; supper; music or diversion, or conversation; examination of the day.
The evening question: "What good have I done to-day?"
10 to 4: sleep.

Franklin admits in the same book that he found order the hardest of his virtues to keep, that his business often pulled him away from the plan, and that he never reached the perfection he had aimed at, though he believed the attempt made him better and happier.
