Article 32 lets a person move the Supreme Court directly to enforce Fundamental Rights. Dr B.R. Ambedkar called it the heart and soul of the Constitution.
The Supreme Court can issue five kinds of writs:
- Habeas corpus ("to have the body"): orders a detained person to be produced before the court, to test whether the detention is lawful. It can be issued against public authorities and private persons.
- Mandamus ("we command"): orders a public official or body to perform a public duty. It cannot be issued against a private individual, or against the President or a state Governor.
- Prohibition: issued by a higher court to a lower court or tribunal to stop it from acting beyond its jurisdiction, while the case is still going on.
- Certiorari: issued by a higher court to quash an order a lower court or tribunal has already passed without jurisdiction.
- Quo warranto ("by what authority"): asks a person holding a public office to show by what right they hold it.
High Courts can issue writs under Article 226. Their power is wider, because they can issue writs for Fundamental Rights and "for any other purpose".
