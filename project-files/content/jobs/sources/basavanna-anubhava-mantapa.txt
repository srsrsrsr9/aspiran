Editor's source notes: Basavanna and the Anubhava Mantapa.

Basavanna lived in the 12th century in Karnataka. As a young man he left his home town and later served as a minister at the court of the Kalachuri king Bijjala at Kalyana.
At Kalyana, he and other poet-saints, known as the Sharanas, began meeting at the Anubhava Mantapa, often described as a hall of spiritual experience. People of many castes and trades came, among them weavers, cobblers and farmers, and women as well as men; the woman poet Akka Mahadevi is remembered as one of those associated with it.
They expressed their ideas in vachanas, short sayings in everyday Kannada rather than in Sanskrit, so that ordinary people could understand them. The vachanas rejected caste distinctions and empty ritual, and praised honest work; the phrase "Kayakave Kailasa", work is worship, is associated with the movement.
Accounts say that a marriage arranged between families of different castes among the Sharanas led to a violent backlash at Kalyana, and the community scattered.
The vachanas survived, copied and sung, and today they are among the best-known works of Kannada literature.
