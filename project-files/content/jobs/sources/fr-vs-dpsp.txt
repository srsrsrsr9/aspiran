Fundamental Rights are in Part III of the Constitution and are justiciable: a person can go to court to enforce them. Examples: equality before the law (Article 14); protection of six freedoms including speech and expression (Article 19); abolition of untouchability (Article 17); the right to constitutional remedies (Article 32).
Directive Principles of State Policy are in Part IV and are not justiciable: courts cannot enforce them, but they are fundamental in the governance of the country. Examples: equal pay for equal work for men and women (Article 39(d)); a uniform civil code for citizens (Article 44); organising village panchayats (Article 40); separating the judiciary from the executive (Article 50).
