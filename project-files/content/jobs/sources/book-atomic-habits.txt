Editor's source notes: James Clear, Atomic Habits (Avery, 2018).

One idea from the book: habit stacking. Clear suggests linking a new habit to one you already do every day, using the formula "After [current habit], I will [new habit]." The current habit acts as the cue, so you don't have to remember the new one or wait for motivation. He pairs it with making the new habit very small at first: the two-minute rule says a new habit should take less than two minutes to start, such as "read one page" instead of "read a chapter".

How it applies to preparation: an aspirant who already makes tea every morning could decide, "After I pour my morning tea, I will open today's current-affairs notes." It works best when the new habit is small and the cue happens at the same time and place every day.

Where to read it: most city and university libraries stock it, and many public libraries lend it as an e-book. The author's website has free articles that explain habit stacking.
