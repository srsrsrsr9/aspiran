The repo rate is the interest rate at which the Reserve Bank of India lends money to commercial banks for a short period, against government securities.
It is decided by the Monetary Policy Committee, which has six members: three from the RBI, including the Governor, and three appointed by the Government of India.
When the RBI raises the repo rate, borrowing from the RBI becomes costlier for banks. Banks tend to raise their own lending rates, people and businesses borrow and spend less, and this helps cool inflation. Cutting the repo rate works the other way and supports growth.
Under the flexible inflation targeting framework, the RBI aims to keep CPI inflation at 4 percent, within a band of 2 to 6 percent.
