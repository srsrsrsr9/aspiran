Kautilya's Arthashastra, Book 1, Chapter 19 (R. Shamasastry translation, 1915) says of the king: "In the happiness of his subjects lies his happiness; in their welfare his welfare." (Quote id: kautilya-arthashastra-1-19. The editor has yet to verify the exact wording.)
The idea links the ruler's own good to the welfare of the people, rather than to the ruler's personal wishes.
Syllabus links: GS1 (Mauryan polity and the Arthashastra), GS2 (the idea of a welfare state; Article 38 of the Constitution, a Directive Principle, asks the State to secure a social order for the promotion of the welfare of the people), and GS4 (ethics in public administration, and the duty of public servants towards citizens).
Use in answers: an ancient Indian source for the idea that legitimacy comes from serving the people, for example in questions on good governance, welfare schemes or the ethics of public office.
