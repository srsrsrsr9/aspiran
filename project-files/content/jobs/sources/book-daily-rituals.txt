Editor's source notes: Mason Currey, Daily Rituals: How Artists Work (Knopf, 2013).

One idea from the book: there is no one right routine. Currey collects short accounts of the daily routines of 161 writers, composers, painters, scientists and philosophers. Some worked early in the morning, some late at night; some worked for hours at a stretch, others in short bursts around jobs and families. What most had in common was not a particular timetable but some regular structure that suited their own lives, which they kept to most days.

How it applies to preparation: there is no single "topper's timetable" that suits everyone. An aspirant with a job, or with caring duties at home, can build a routine around fixed points that fit their own day, rather than copying someone else's hours.

Where to read it: many public libraries stock it. The book began as a blog, and short accounts in the same style are easy to find in library magazines and newspapers.
