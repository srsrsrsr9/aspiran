Editor's source notes: Savitribai Phule and the first girls' schools in Pune.

Savitribai Phule was born in 1831 in Naigaon, in present-day Maharashtra. She was married to Jyotirao Phule as a child, and he taught her to read and write at home.
In 1848, the Phules started a school for girls at Bhide Wada in Pune. Savitribai became one of its teachers, which made her one of the first Indian women to teach in a school.
Many people in Pune opposed girls' education. Accounts of her life say that on her way to the school, people threw mud and dung at her, and that she carried a second sari to change into once she reached the school.
The Phules went on to open more schools for girls and for children of lower castes in Pune over the following years. Fatima Sheikh, who taught alongside Savitribai, is remembered as one of the first Muslim women teachers in India.
Savitribai also wrote poetry; her collection Kavya Phule was published in 1854.
She died in 1897 in Pune, after catching plague while caring for patients during an epidemic.
Her birthday, 3 January, is observed in Maharashtra as a day for girls' education.
