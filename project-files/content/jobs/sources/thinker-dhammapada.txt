Editor's source notes: the first verse of the Dhammapada.

The Dhammapada is a collection of 423 verses of the Buddha's teaching in Pali, part of the Pali Canon. It is among the most widely read early Buddhist texts, and F. Max Müller's English translation appeared in 1881 in the Sacred Books of the East series.

The first verse says, in Müller's words, that all that we are is the result of what we have thought: it is founded on our thoughts and made up of our thoughts. The verse goes on to say that if a person speaks or acts with an impure mind, pain follows, as the wheel follows the foot of the ox that draws the cart. The second verse says the same of a pure mind, which happiness follows like a shadow.

Why it matters for the exam: GS Paper IV covers attitude (its content, structure and influence on thought and behaviour) and the contributions of moral thinkers from India. The verse is a compact Indian statement that attitude and intention come before action, useful in answers on attitude formation, on the role of intention in ethics, and on why officials need self-awareness. In an essay on the mind or on character, it makes a clear opening.
