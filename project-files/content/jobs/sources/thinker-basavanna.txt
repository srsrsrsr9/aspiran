Editor's source notes: Basavanna and "Kayakave Kailasa".

Basavanna was a 12th-century poet and social reformer in Karnataka. He served as a minister at the court of the Kalachuri king Bijjala at Kalyana. With other poet-saints called the Sharanas, he wrote vachanas, short free-verse sayings in Kannada, in the everyday language of the people rather than in Sanskrit.

The Sharanas met at the Anubhava Mantapa, often described as a hall of spiritual experience, where people of different castes and occupations, and women as well as men, discussed ideas as equals.

The phrase "Kayakave Kailasa", usually rendered as "work is worship", is associated with Basavanna and the Sharana movement. It holds that honest work of any kind, whether by a weaver, a cobbler or a farmer, has dignity, and that no occupation is lower than another.

Why it matters for the exam: GS Paper I covers the Bhakti movement and its regional saints; GS Paper IV asks for the contributions of Indian moral thinkers and covers the dignity of labour and equality. The idea suits answers on work ethic in public service, on equality of opportunity, and on the Bhakti and reform traditions. In an essay on work or on equality, it gives a short Indian line from a regional language.
