Editor's source notes: Charles Duhigg, The Power of Habit (Random House, 2012).

One idea from the book: the habit loop. Duhigg describes a habit as a loop of three parts: a cue that triggers it, a routine (the behaviour itself), and a reward that makes the brain want to repeat it. To change a habit, he suggests keeping the same cue and reward and swapping in a different routine. He also describes keystone habits: single habits, such as regular exercise, that tend to pull other good habits along with them.

How it applies to preparation: an aspirant who reaches for the phone (routine) whenever a hard chapter feels boring (cue), to get a break (reward), could keep the cue and the reward but change the routine, for example a two-minute walk away from the desk, and then return.

Where to read it: most public and college libraries stock it, and many lend it as an e-book.
