The Coromandel coast of Tamil Nadu receives a large share of its annual rainfall between October and December.
During this season the northeast monsoon winds blow from land to sea over most of India, but over the Bay of Bengal they pick up moisture before reaching the Tamil Nadu coast, and so bring rain there.
Cyclones that form over the Bay of Bengal in October and November also bring heavy rain to the east coast.
During the southwest monsoon (June to September), the Tamil Nadu coast lies in the rain shadow of the Western Ghats and gets relatively little rain.
The Western Ghats force the moist southwest monsoon winds to rise, giving heavy orographic rain on the western slopes.
The Thar desert is dry partly because the Aravalli range lies parallel to the Arabian Sea branch of the southwest monsoon and does not block it.
