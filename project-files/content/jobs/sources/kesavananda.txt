Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) was heard by a bench of 13 judges of the Supreme Court, the largest ever. It was decided by a majority of 7 to 6.
The question: does Parliament's power to amend the Constitution under Article 368 have any limit?
The court held that Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution, including Fundamental Rights, but it cannot alter or destroy its basic structure.
The basic structure is not defined in one list. Later cases have counted features such as the supremacy of the Constitution, judicial review, federalism, secularism and free and fair elections as part of it.
The case is central to questions on the amending power, judicial review and the separation of powers.
