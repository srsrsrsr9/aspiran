Editor's source notes: Patanjali, Yoga Sutras 1.12.

The Yoga Sutras attributed to Patanjali are a short text of about 196 sutras, or aphorisms, in Sanskrit. Scholars date them variously, often to somewhere in the early centuries CE. The second sutra defines yoga as the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind.

Sutra 1.12 says, in one common rendering, that these fluctuations are controlled by practice and non-attachment (abhyasa and vairagya). The sutras that follow explain that practice means steady effort kept up over a long time, without breaks and with care, and that non-attachment means not being pulled about by what one craves.

Why it matters for the exam: GS Paper IV asks for the contributions of Indian thinkers and covers emotional intelligence and self-regulation. The pairing of steady practice with letting go of results fits answers on self-control in public life and on handling pressure. For an aspirant, it is also a plain description of how preparation works: regular revision, without hanging every day on one mock score.
