In Punjab and Haryana, paddy is harvested in October and wheat must be sown by November, leaving a short window to clear the fields.
Many farmers burn the leftover stubble because it is the fastest and cheapest option.
The smoke adds to pollution across north India. In early winter, calm winds and cooler air near the ground often trap pollutants, so Delhi's air quality can fall to "severe".
Poor air raises the risk of respiratory illness.
Options include the Happy Seeder, which sows wheat without removing the stubble, the Pusa bio-decomposer, which breaks stubble down in the field, and using stubble as fuel in biomass power plants.
