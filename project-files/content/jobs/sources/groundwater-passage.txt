Passage (written by the editor for practice):

For decades, the answer to a dry field was a deeper well. Cheap or free electricity for farm pumps made it possible to lift water from ever greater depths, and in many districts the water table has fallen year after year. Each farmer acts sensibly on his own: if he does not pump, his neighbour will, and the water will be gone anyway. The result is a race to the bottom that no single farmer can stop. Groundwater is a shared resource that behaves like a private one, because the person who pumps it pays only for the electricity, not for the water he takes from others. Some states have tried metering farm power, others have paid farmers to use less, and a few villages have agreed among themselves on what to grow and when to pump. Where such agreements have held, they have rested less on rules from outside than on trust, regular meetings and simple, visible measurements of the water level that everyone can check. The lesson is not that farmers are careless, but that a common resource needs a common way of deciding.
