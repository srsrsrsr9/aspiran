Article 368 of the Constitution sets out how Parliament amends the Constitution.
An amendment bill can be introduced in either House of Parliament, by a minister or by a private member. It cannot be introduced in a state legislature.
Each House must pass the bill separately by a special majority: a majority of the total membership of the House and a two-thirds majority of the members present and voting.
There is no joint sitting for an amendment bill. If the two Houses disagree, the bill fails.
If the amendment changes federal provisions, such as the representation of states in Parliament or the distribution of powers, it must also be ratified by the legislatures of half of the states, by a simple majority.
The bill then goes to the President, who must give assent. The 24th Amendment of 1971 made this assent compulsory.
