Editor's source notes: lines widely shared as quotes of the Buddha, Swami Vivekananda and A. P. J. Abdul Kalam.

The Buddha. The early record of the Buddha's teaching is the Pali Canon, which includes the Dhammapada, a collection of 423 verses. Many lines shared online as Buddha quotes are not in it. "Holding on to anger is like grasping a hot coal with the intent of throwing it at someone else; you are the one who gets burned" is not found in the Pali Canon; it is a modern rewording, loosely based on an image in a later commentary. The Dhammapada does open with the idea that the mind comes first and shapes experience.

Swami Vivekananda. "Arise, awake, and stop not till the goal is reached" is genuinely associated with Vivekananda. It is his adaptation of a verse of the Katha Upanishad (1.3.14), and it appears in his lectures, collected in The Complete Works of Swami Vivekananda, published by Advaita Ashrama. National Youth Day, 12 January, marks his birthday.

A. P. J. Abdul Kalam. Kalam's books include Wings of Fire (1999), written with Arun Tiwari, and Ignited Minds (2002). Many lines on posters credited to him cannot be traced to his books or speeches, so each one has to be checked. Kalam, who led the SLV-3 project, often told the story of the failed first SLV-3 launch in 1979: Satish Dhawan, then chairman of ISRO, faced the press himself and took responsibility, and after the successful launch in 1980 he asked Kalam to speak to the press.

Why it matters: in GS4 and Essay answers, an untraceable quote is a risk. A true story told in your own words, such as the SLV-3 episode, is safer and more convincing.
