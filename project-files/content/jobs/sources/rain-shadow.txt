The Western Ghats stand in the path of the southwest monsoon winds. As moist air rises over the windward western slopes, it cools and drops heavy rain on the Konkan and Malabar coasts. This is orographic rainfall.
On the leeward eastern side, the descending air is drier, so large parts of the interior Deccan, such as parts of Marathwada and north interior Karnataka, lie in a rain shadow and get much less rain.
The Coromandel coast of Tamil Nadu gets much of its annual rain from the northeast monsoon between October and December, because during the southwest monsoon it lies in the rain shadow of the Western Ghats.
Mangroves grow in the intertidal zones of tropical coasts and deltas. They can tolerate salt water, and many have breathing roots (pneumatophores) that help them survive in waterlogged soil.
Cherrapunji and Mawsynram in Meghalaya receive very heavy rain because moist winds from the Bay of Bengal are funnelled up the Khasi hills.
