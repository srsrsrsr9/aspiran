Fundamental Duties are in Article 51A, in Part IVA of the Constitution.
They were added by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee. The original Constitution had no Fundamental Duties.
The 42nd Amendment added ten duties. The 86th Amendment Act, 2002 added an eleventh: a parent or guardian is to provide opportunities for education to their child between six and fourteen years of age.
Fundamental Duties apply to citizens only, not to foreigners.
They are not directly enforceable in a court of law; the Constitution provides no direct legal sanction for their violation. Parliament can enforce them through suitable laws.
Among the duties: to abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem; to protect and improve the natural environment; to develop the scientific temper; to safeguard public property and abjure violence.
The idea of Fundamental Duties was inspired by the Constitution of the former USSR.
