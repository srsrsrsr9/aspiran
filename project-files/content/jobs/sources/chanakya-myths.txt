Facts about Kautilya (Chanakya) and his texts, for a Myth vs Fact pack. The editor should check each against R.P. Kangle's edition of the Arthashastra.
The Arthashastra is a treatise on statecraft, economics and administration traditionally attributed to Kautilya, also known as Chanakya or Vishnugupta, linked with the court of Chandragupta Maurya.
A manuscript of the Arthashastra was found in 1905 and R. Shamasastry published an English translation in 1915. Before that, the text was known mainly through references in other works.
The Arthashastra and the "Chanakya Niti" are different works. The Chanakya Niti is a collection of moral sayings; many lines circulated online as "Chanakya quotes" do not appear in the Arthashastra, and many cannot be traced to either text.
Scholars debate whether the whole Arthashastra was written by one person at one time; many think it was compiled and revised over a period.
The saptanga theory in the Arthashastra describes seven elements of the state: the king (swami), ministers (amatya), territory and people (janapada), fort (durga), treasury (kosha), army (danda) and ally (mitra).
The Arthashastra describes the king's welfare as lying in the welfare of his subjects (Book 1, Chapter 19).
