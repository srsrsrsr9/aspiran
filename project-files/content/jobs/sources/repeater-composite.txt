Editor's note: this is an illustrative composite written by the editor from common experiences. It is not one real person, and it uses no name.
A woman in her late twenties from a small town in Madhya Pradesh. She had a government school teaching job and studied for the civil services in the evenings and on weekends.
The call: after a friend cleared the state service exam, she decided to try the UPSC CSE seriously.
The ordeal: she took three attempts. She cleared Prelims once but did not clear Mains. In the third year she was studying 10 hours a day on top of her job, and her health and sleep suffered.
The decision: after the third attempt, she decided to stop the UPSC attempts and focus on the state civil services exam, while keeping her teaching job. What she knew then: her Mains answers were improving slowly, her savings were low, and her parents needed her nearby. What happened: she cleared the state service exam two years later. What she would keep: the daily habit of answer writing, and her study group. What she would change: she would have set a clear limit on attempts at the start, and talked to her family about it earlier.
What she brought back: she now runs a free weekend answer-writing circle for aspirants at the district library.
