Editor's source notes: social reform movements in modern India.

In 1848, Jyotirao Phule and Savitribai Phule started a school for girls in Pune, at Bhide Wada; Savitribai taught there.
In 1873, Jyotirao Phule founded the Satyashodhak Samaj (Society of Truth-Seekers) to work for the rights of lower castes. The same year he published Gulamgiri (Slavery).
In 1888, Sree Narayana Guru consecrated a Shiva idol at Aruvippuram in Kerala, challenging the rule that only Brahmins could consecrate temple idols.
In 1903, the Sree Narayana Dharma Paripalana Yogam (SNDP Yogam) was founded in Kerala, with Sree Narayana Guru as its president.
In 1924, the Vaikom Satyagraha began in Travancore, for the right of all castes to use the roads around the Vaikom temple.
In 1927, B. R. Ambedkar led the Mahad Satyagraha, asserting the right of Dalits to draw water from the Chavdar tank at Mahad.

Why it matters: GS Paper I covers social reform movements and their leaders. Questions often ask which came first, or match a leader with an organisation.
