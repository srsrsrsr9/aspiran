# Long read (one topic in depth)

Schema: `upsc-pods/long-read@1` · Folder: `content/long-read/` · Family: Learn · Engine: steps

**Exam:** revision, mains · papers: prelims-gs1, mains-gs2 · formats: mains-answer, mains-both-sides, pre-current-static

**Sound:** A page turn between sections; nothing else.

**Motion:** Sections fade in as they scroll into view (none with reduced motion).

## How to fill it

A five-to-ten-minute read on one syllabus topic, written like a good senior's notes: the idea first, then the articles and years, then the debate and how the exam uses it. It fills the gap between quick formats (flashcards, MCQs) and a textbook chapter.
- sections: 4 to 7, in a teaching order (what it is, how it came about, how it works, where it has been tested in court or in practice, what is debated now). Each body 200 words or fewer, in short paragraphs separated by a blank line.
- Every fact, article number, year, case and name comes from the source. Where the source hedges ("about", "usually"), keep the hedge.
- keyFacts: the lines an aspirant would put on a revision card, each one checkable against the source.
- examAngle: what kinds of questions the topic invites (statement questions on articles, a 15-mark "critically examine" on its working). Never quote or invent a previous-year question.
- debate: both sides, fairly and in neutral words. Never say which side is right, and never praise or blame a party or a living politician.
- selfCheck: questions, not answers. Each must be answerable from the long read itself.
- minutes: total words in the English sections divided by 150, rounded.
