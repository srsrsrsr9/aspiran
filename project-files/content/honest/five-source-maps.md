> UPSC Pods earns nothing from any source on these maps. There are no affiliate links and no paid placements. How we pick: free and official sources first (NCERT, the Constitution, government reports, UPSC's own papers), then at most one or two standard books that many aspirants already use. A book is listed for what it does well, not for who sells it. We never list a coaching institute's material. Where a detail is still being checked, it is marked for review. If you think a source is wrong or missing, tell us in Resource Court.

For each subject: at most five sources, each with why, what to read, what to skip and how many times to revise. Then the common trap, one rule, and a Keep / Skip / Maybe call on three things people often hoard.

## Polity

**Serves:** Prelims GS I, Mains GS2

### 1. Indian Constitution at Work (NCERT, Class 11 Political Science)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** Explains why the Constitution looks the way it does. With this base, the standard book reads faster and sticks better.
- **Read:** The whole book. It is short. Read it once like a story before you open anything else.
- **Skip:** The companion book, Political Theory, except the chapters on rights, equality and secularism if you have time.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 2. The Constitution of India (bare text)

Legislative Department, Ministry of Law and Justice. Free. https://legislative.gov.in/

- **Why:** The exam quotes the text itself. Reading the actual Article settles doubts that notes and summaries create.
- **Read:** The Preamble, Parts III, IV and IVA, then each Article your main book cites, as you meet it.
- **Skip:** Reading it cover to cover, and the long lists in the Schedules.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 3. Indian Polity

M. Laxmikanth, McGraw Hill. Paid.

- **Why:** Covers nearly all of Prelims polity in one place, in a clear order. Most aspirants use it, so your pod can revise it together.
- **Read:** Core chapters: making of the Constitution, rights, Union and State governments, judiciary, federalism, emergency, local government, constitutional bodies.
- **Skip:** Appendix tables on the first reading. Come back to them in the last Prelims revision.
- **Revisions:** 3

### 4. UPSC previous year question papers

Union Public Service Commission. Free. https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers

- **Why:** The exam's own record of what it asks. It tells you which parts of your other sources deserve depth and which deserve a skim.
- **Read:** Polity questions from Prelims GS Paper I and Mains GS2, the last ten years, sorted by topic.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 5. PRS Legislative Research: bill summaries and Monthly Policy Review

PRS Legislative Research. Free. https://prsindia.org/

- **Why:** Neutral, short summaries of bills, Acts and Parliament's work. This is how static polity meets the news for Mains.
- **Read:** The summary page of each major bill in the news, and the Monthly Policy Review.
- **Skip:** Full bill texts and committee reports, unless a topic is clearly exam-heavy.
- **Revisions:** 1

**The trap.** Buying three polity books and two sets of notes, then finishing none of them properly.

**The one rule.** One standard book, read three times, with the bare Article open beside it.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| D.D. Basu's Introduction to the Constitution of India | Maybe | Good for depth in Mains and for law-minded readers. Not needed for Prelims if your main book is done well. |
| Forwarded polity notes PDFs from many channels | Skip | They repeat your book with more errors and no page numbers. Your own margin notes are better. |
| A big polity MCQ practice book | Maybe | Useful after your second revision, to find weak chapters. Before that, it only tests what you have not read. |

## Modern History

**Serves:** Prelims GS I, Mains GS1

### 1. Themes in Indian History, Part III (NCERT, Class 12)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** A calm start on colonial rule, 1857, Gandhi and the making of the Constitution, built on real sources. Good for Mains thinking.
- **Read:** All chapters once, lightly. Note the sources each chapter quotes; Mains likes them.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 2. A Brief History of Modern India

Rajiv Ahir, Spectrum Books. Paid.

- **Why:** Exam-shaped, in date order, and close to Prelims depth. Easy to revise in the last month.
- **Read:** From the mid-1700s onward. The national movement chapters in full, plus the boxes on Acts, sessions and organisations.
- **Skip:** The arrival of the Europeans in detail, and the long appendices on your first reading.
- **Revisions:** 3

### 3. India's Struggle for Independence

Bipan Chandra, Mridula Mukherjee, Aditya Mukherjee, K.N. Panikkar, Sucheta Mahajan, Penguin. Paid.

- **Why:** Explains why movements rose and fell. This builds the analysis a fact book cannot give you.
- **Read:** The chapters on phases you find thin. Read it like a story, once, without making notes.
- **Skip:** Making notes on every chapter. Your fact notes already live in the main book.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 4. Politics in India since Independence (NCERT, Class 12 Political Science)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** GS1 asks about India after 1947. This short book covers nation-building, planning and the big political turns.
- **Read:** Chapters on nation-building, planned development, the Emergency and regional aspirations.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 5. UPSC previous year question papers

Union Public Service Commission. Free. https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers

- **Why:** The exam's own record of what it asks. It tells you which parts of your other sources deserve depth and which deserve a skim.
- **Read:** Modern history questions from Prelims and Mains GS1, last ten years. Mark the topics that keep coming back.
- **Revisions:** 2

**The trap.** Collecting five modern history books and every topper's notes, then reading the same 1857 chapter six times.

**The one rule.** Facts from one book. The why from one story-book. Nothing else.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The old NCERT Modern India | Maybe | A fine book, but it overlaps your main book heavily. Read it only if you have not started another. |
| A self-made timeline of sessions, Acts and movements | Keep | Two or three pages you made yourself are the best revision tool for Prelims dates. |
| Toppers' handwritten notes PDFs | Skip | They were made for someone else's memory. You will spend weeks decoding them. |

## Ancient and Medieval History

**Serves:** Prelims GS I, Mains GS1

### 1. Themes in Indian History, Part I (NCERT, Class 12)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** From Harappa to early states and religions, told through evidence. A free, official base.
- **Read:** All chapters, once carefully, then once quickly before Prelims.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 2. Themes in Indian History, Part II (NCERT, Class 12)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** Covers travellers' accounts, Bhakti and Sufi traditions, Vijayanagara and agrarian life. UPSC asks these more than battles.
- **Read:** All chapters in your copy. Spend most time on Bhakti, Sufi and Vijayanagara.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 3. India's Ancient Past **[verify]**

R.S. Sharma, Oxford University Press. Paid.

- **Why:** One readable narrative of ancient India by a respected historian. Fills gaps the Class 12 book leaves.
- **Read:** Harappan, Vedic, Mauryan, post-Mauryan and Gupta chapters, and those on Buddhism and Jainism.
- **Skip:** Long debates on chronology and fine archaeological detail.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 4. History of Medieval India **[verify]**

Satish Chandra, Orient BlackSwan. Paid.

- **Why:** The Sultanate, the Mughals and regional kingdoms in one narrative, with a focus on administration and society.
- **Read:** Chapters on administration, economy, culture, and Bhakti and Sufi movements.
- **Skip:** Battle sequences and succession quarrels.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 5. UPSC previous year question papers

Union Public Service Commission. Free. https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers

- **Why:** The exam's own record of what it asks. It tells you which parts of your other sources deserve depth and which deserve a skim.
- **Read:** Ancient and medieval questions in Prelims, last ten years. Notice how many are about culture, religion and terms.
- **Revisions:** 2

**The trap.** Memorising every dynasty, king and battle date. UPSC mostly asks culture, religion, administration and economy.

**The one rule.** Read for society and culture, not for kings.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Upinder Singh's large history of ancient and early medieval India | Maybe | Excellent, but very long. Keep it for reference if you love the subject or take history optional. |
| A wall chart of all dynasties with dates | Skip | It rewards the kind of question UPSC rarely asks. Your time is better spent on culture. |
| Old NCERTs on ancient and medieval India | Maybe | Good, but the books above grew out of them. Pick one line, not both. |

## Art and Culture

**Serves:** Prelims GS I, Mains GS1

### 1. An Introduction to Indian Art, Part I (NCERT, Class 11 Fine Arts)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** Official and illustrated. Sculpture, architecture and painting make far more sense with pictures in front of you.
- **Read:** The whole book. Spend extra time on temple architecture, Buddhist art and murals.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 2. Living Craft Traditions of India (NCERT, Class 11 Heritage Crafts) **[verify]**

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** A short official book on crafts and the people who make them. Useful for Prelims craft and textile questions.
- **Read:** The chapters on textiles, metal and stone crafts, and regional traditions.
- **Skip:** Activity sections meant for school students.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 3. Indian Art and Culture

Nitin Singhania, McGraw Hill. Paid.

- **Why:** Brings most of the Prelims art and culture syllabus into one book. Saves you from collecting scattered notes.
- **Read:** Architecture, sculpture, painting, music, dance, theatre, and religion and philosophy chapters.
- **Skip:** Long lists of festivals, awards and minor crafts on your first reading.
- **Revisions:** 3

### 4. Centre for Cultural Resources and Training (CCRT) website **[verify]**

CCRT, Ministry of Culture. Free. https://ccrtindia.gov.in/

- **Why:** Official notes on dance, music and crafts. A good check when two books disagree.
- **Read:** The pages on classical dance and music forms, and on India's entries in the UNESCO heritage lists.
- **Skip:** Training programme pages meant for teachers.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 5. UPSC previous year question papers

Union Public Service Commission. Free. https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers

- **Why:** The exam's own record of what it asks. It tells you which parts of your other sources deserve depth and which deserve a skim.
- **Read:** Art and culture questions in Prelims, last ten years, and culture questions in Mains GS1.
- **Revisions:** 2

**The trap.** Making separate notes for every craft, festival and instrument. The list never ends.

**The one rule.** Learn through pictures and PYQs. One book for facts.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A second art and culture book | Skip | Two books on a list-heavy subject double the lists, not the marks. |
| Virtual or real museum visits | Maybe | If you enjoy them, seeing a Gandhara Buddha once fixes it in memory. Not required. |
| A full list of all GI tags | Skip | Learn the ones that come up in the news, with their state. The full list is too long to be worth it. |

## Geography (physical, Indian, world)

**Serves:** Prelims GS I, Mains GS1

### 1. Fundamentals of Physical Geography (NCERT, Class 11)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** The cleanest base for landforms, climate, oceans and the atmosphere. Most physical geography questions start here.
- **Read:** The whole book. Redraw each diagram once by hand.
- **Revisions:** 3

### 2. India: Physical Environment (NCERT, Class 11)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** India's relief, drainage, climate, soils and vegetation in one short official book.
- **Read:** The whole book, with an atlas open.
- **Revisions:** 3

### 3. Fundamentals of Human Geography and India: People and Economy (NCERT, Class 12)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** Population, settlement, resources and industry, for the world and for India. Feeds both Prelims and GS1.
- **Read:** Chapters on population, resources, agriculture, industry and transport.
- **Skip:** Practical-work sections on statistics and map projection drills.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 4. Certificate Physical and Human Geography

Goh Cheng Leong, Oxford University Press. Paid.

- **Why:** Explains world climate types and landforms with clear diagrams. Fills the world geography gap NCERT leaves.
- **Read:** The physical geography part and the chapters on world climate types.
- **Skip:** The human and economic geography part. Its facts are dated.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 5. Oxford Student Atlas for India

Oxford University Press. Paid.

- **Why:** Geography without a map is memory work. With one, it becomes sight. Map questions appear in Prelims every year.
- **Read:** India physical and political maps, then world regions in the news. Use it daily, a few minutes at a time.
- **Skip:** Statistical pages and thematic maps you will never be asked about.
- **Revisions:** 3

**The trap.** Reading geography like a novel, with no map open, then forgetting where anything is.

**The one rule.** Every place you read, find it on the map. Same day.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Majid Husain's Geography of India | Maybe | Deep and useful for Mains or the geography optional. The NCERTs are enough for most GS needs. |
| Optional-level physical geography textbooks | Skip | Written for a different paper. For GS they add depth you will not be asked for. |
| A blank outline map you fill in yourself | Keep | Cheap and powerful. Filling rivers, passes and parks yourself beats any printed list. |

## Economy

**Serves:** Prelims GS I, Mains GS3

### 1. Introductory Macroeconomics (NCERT, Class 12) and Indian Economic Development (NCERT, Class 11)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** The basic ideas in plain language: national income, money, banking, budget, balance of payments and India's growth story.
- **Read:** Macroeconomics in full. In Indian Economic Development, the chapters on reforms since 1991, poverty, employment and rural development.
- **Skip:** The statistics-for-economics companion book.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 2. Economic Survey

Ministry of Finance, Government of India. Free. https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/

- **Why:** The government's own account of the economy each year. Mains arguments and many Prelims terms come from here.
- **Read:** The current year's overview chapter, the chapters on themes in the news, and the boxes defining new terms.
- **Skip:** The statistical appendix tables.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 3. Union Budget: Budget at a Glance and the Budget Speech

Ministry of Finance, Government of India. Free. https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/

- **Why:** Shows where the government's money comes from and where it goes. Two short documents, not a whole library.
- **Read:** Budget at a Glance in full, and Part A of the Budget Speech.
- **Skip:** Demands for Grants and the detailed expenditure documents.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 4. Indian Economy

Ramesh Singh, McGraw Hill. Paid.

- **Why:** One reference that covers most of the syllabus. Useful when an NCERT idea needs more detail.
- **Read:** Use it like a dictionary: inflation, banking, fiscal policy, external sector, agriculture, as topics come up.
- **Skip:** Reading it cover to cover in one go.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 5. Reserve Bank of India: Monetary Policy Statements

Reserve Bank of India. Free. https://www.rbi.org.in/

- **Why:** Interest rates, inflation and banking, first-hand. Short, and written to be read by the public.
- **Read:** The resolution after each Monetary Policy Committee meeting.
- **Skip:** Research papers and detailed bulletins.
- **Revisions:** 1

**The trap.** Treating economy as numbers to memorise. GDP figures change; the ideas stay.

**The one rule.** Concept first, news second, numbers last.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Two economy reference books side by side | Skip | They cover the same ground in different words. Pick one and stay with it. |
| Summaries of the Survey and Budget made by others | Maybe | Fine for revision once you have seen the original. As a replacement, they lose the reasoning. |
| A notebook of 20 core economy arguments for Mains | Keep | Short, yours, and reusable across many questions on growth, jobs, farming and inflation. |

## Environment and Ecology

**Serves:** Prelims GS I, Mains GS3

### 1. Biology (NCERT, Class 12): the ecology chapters **[verify]**

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** The science of ecosystems, populations and biodiversity, explained properly. Many Prelims questions test exactly these basics.
- **Read:** The ecology chapters at the end: organisms and populations, ecosystem, biodiversity and conservation. Recent printings dropped some parts; an older printing has them.
- **Skip:** The rest of the biology book, unless it is also in your science plan.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 2. Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change: Annual Report

MoEFCC, Government of India. Free. https://moef.gov.in/

- **Why:** The official list of laws, missions, programmes and protected-area work, in one document.
- **Read:** Chapters on conservation, wetlands, climate change, pollution control and international cooperation.
- **Skip:** Administrative and budget chapters.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 3. India State of Forest Report

Forest Survey of India. Free. https://fsi.nic.in/

- **Why:** Forest and tree cover questions come from this report. Its key findings are short.
- **Read:** The key findings section of the current report only.
- **Skip:** State-by-state chapters and methodology.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 4. Economic Survey: the climate and environment chapter

Ministry of Finance, Government of India. Free. https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/

- **Why:** Shows how India argues its climate position. This is the framing Mains answers need.
- **Read:** That one chapter in the current Survey.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 5. UPSC previous year question papers

Union Public Service Commission. Free. https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers

- **Why:** The exam's own record of what it asks. It tells you which parts of your other sources deserve depth and which deserve a skim.
- **Read:** Environment questions in Prelims, last ten years, and Mains GS3 environment questions. Note which conventions and species recur.
- **Revisions:** 2

**The trap.** Memorising every species, national park and convention date from giant lists.

**The one rule.** Learn the concept, then hang the news on it.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| One thick environment guidebook | Maybe | Only if NCERT and the official reports leave real gaps for you. Many manage without one. |
| A list of every national park with its area | Skip | Learn the parks in the news, on the map. Areas are rarely asked. |
| A one-page sheet of major conventions and what each one does | Keep | Short, made by you, and revised often. It covers a lot of Prelims ground. |

## Science and Technology

**Serves:** Prelims GS I, Mains GS3

### 1. Science (NCERT, Class 9 and Class 10)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** The basic science that makes the news readable. Without it, every new technology feels like a new subject.
- **Read:** Chapters on cells, life processes, heredity, light, electricity and magnetism.
- **Skip:** Numerical exercises and lab activities.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 2. Biology (NCERT, Class 12): biotechnology and human health chapters

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** Biotech, vaccines and disease show up often. These chapters explain them at exactly the right depth.
- **Read:** The two biotechnology chapters and the chapter on human health and disease.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 3. Press Information Bureau: science releases

Press Information Bureau, Government of India. Free. https://pib.gov.in/

- **Why:** S&T in Prelims is mostly current. PIB gives the official version of missions, launches and schemes.
- **Read:** Releases from science ministries and departments, once a week.
- **Skip:** Event and award releases.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 4. ISRO website: missions pages

Indian Space Research Organisation. Free. https://www.isro.gov.in/

- **Why:** Space questions return almost every year. The mission pages state the facts plainly.
- **Read:** Pages for current and recent missions, and launch vehicles.
- **Skip:** Technical papers and archived older missions.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 5. UPSC previous year question papers

Union Public Service Commission. Free. https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers

- **Why:** The exam's own record of what it asks. It tells you which parts of your other sources deserve depth and which deserve a skim.
- **Read:** S&T questions in Prelims and Mains GS3, last ten years. See how often they ask what a technology does, not how it is built.
- **Revisions:** 2

**The trap.** Reading every science article like a scientist, then running out of time for the rest.

**The one rule.** For each new thing: what it is, how it works in one line, why India cares.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A dedicated science and technology book | Maybe | Can help organise topics, but goes out of date fast. The news and NCERT do most of the work. |
| Science magazine subscriptions | Skip | Enjoyable, but far deeper than the exam. Read them for fun after the exam. |
| Short explainers for hard topics, from official or university sources | Keep | Ten minutes on a topic like gene editing can save an hour of confused reading. |

## International Relations

**Serves:** Prelims GS I, Mains GS2

### 1. Contemporary World Politics (NCERT, Class 12 Political Science)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** A short base on the world after the Cold War, the UN and regional groupings. Gives the vocabulary for GS2.
- **Read:** The whole book, once, lightly.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 2. Ministry of External Affairs: bilateral briefs and joint statements

Ministry of External Affairs, Government of India. Free. https://www.mea.gov.in/

- **Why:** India's official position, in its own words. Bilateral briefs are short and kept up to date.
- **Read:** Bilateral briefs for neighbours and major partners, and joint statements from visits in the news.
- **Skip:** Speeches and briefings on minor events.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 3. Does the Elephant Dance? Contemporary Indian Foreign Policy

David M. Malone, Oxford University Press. Paid.

- **Why:** A thoughtful overview of how India deals with the world. Builds the depth that Mains answers need.
- **Read:** Chapters on the neighbourhood, China, the United States and multilateral diplomacy.
- **Skip:** Event details. It is an older book, so check facts against current MEA briefs.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 4. UPSC previous year question papers

Union Public Service Commission. Free. https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers

- **Why:** The exam's own record of what it asks. It tells you which parts of your other sources deserve depth and which deserve a skim.
- **Read:** IR questions in Mains GS2 and Prelims, last ten years. Note how often they ask about India's interests, not just events.
- **Revisions:** 2

**The trap.** Making notes on every summit and every country. The world is too big for that.

**The one rule.** For each key partner: the history, ties now, the friction, and a way forward.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A second Indian foreign policy book | Maybe | Only if you enjoy the subject. One good book plus MEA briefs covers most of what is asked. |
| Country-wise notes on fifty or more countries | Skip | Most will never be asked. Focus on neighbours, major powers and groupings India belongs to. |
| A world map you mark with each visit and agreement | Keep | Links IR to geography and makes Prelims location questions easier. |

## Indian Society and Social Justice

**Serves:** Mains GS1, Mains GS2

### 1. Indian Society (NCERT, Class 12 Sociology)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** Caste, tribe, family, demography and inequality, explained with care. The concepts GS1 society questions are built on.
- **Read:** The whole book.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 2. Social Change and Development in India (NCERT, Class 12 Sociology)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** How Indian society is changing: urbanisation, globalisation, media and social movements.
- **Read:** Chapters on structural change, globalisation and social movements.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 3. Census of India: data highlights

Office of the Registrar General and Census Commissioner, India. Free. https://censusindia.gov.in/

- **Why:** Official data for population, literacy, sex ratio and urbanisation. A real number makes a society answer credible.
- **Read:** The highlights and key tables only, for the most recent census released.
- **Skip:** District-level tables.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 4. Press Information Bureau: releases on welfare schemes

Press Information Bureau, Government of India. Free. https://pib.gov.in/

- **Why:** GS2 social justice asks about schemes for vulnerable groups. PIB gives their official aims and design.
- **Read:** Releases from ministries for social justice, women and child development, tribal affairs, minority affairs, health and education.
- **Skip:** Event and inauguration releases.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 5. UPSC previous year question papers

Union Public Service Commission. Free. https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers

- **Why:** The exam's own record of what it asks. It tells you which parts of your other sources deserve depth and which deserve a skim.
- **Read:** Society questions in Mains GS1 and social justice questions in GS2, since the current syllabus began.
- **Revisions:** 2

**The trap.** Writing opinions with no concept, example or data behind them.

**The one rule.** Every point: one concept, one real example, one scheme or data point.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| An optional-level sociology textbook | Skip | Written for a different paper. GS society needs clear concepts, not theory debates. |
| Data on every welfare scheme | Skip | Numbers change and blur. Know the aim and design of the big schemes instead. |
| A personal bank of ten real examples from your own district or state | Keep | Examples you know first-hand are easy to remember and stand out in answers. |

<a id="governance"></a>
## Governance

**Serves:** Mains GS2

### 1. Second Administrative Reforms Commission reports

Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances. Free. https://darpg.gov.in/

- **Why:** Still the most quoted official source on administrative reform. A recommendation from here strengthens almost any governance answer.
- **Read:** The summaries of recommendations of: Right to Information, Ethics in Governance, Local Governance, e-Governance, and Citizen Centric Administration.
- **Skip:** The full text of all fifteen reports.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 2. Governance in India

M. Laxmikanth, McGraw Hill. Paid.

- **Why:** Arranged around the GS2 governance syllabus. Saves you building the structure yourself.
- **Read:** Chapters on e-governance, citizen charters, transparency, civil services, NGOs and self-help groups.
- **Skip:** Practice question sections, if you already use PYQs.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 3. NITI Aayog reports and indices

NITI Aayog. Free. https://www.niti.gov.in/

- **Why:** Official indices and reports give data and best practices you can cite.
- **Read:** Executive summaries of indices and reports that are in the news.
- **Skip:** Full reports and annexures.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 4. Press Information Bureau: governance releases

Press Information Bureau, Government of India. Free. https://pib.gov.in/

- **Why:** New portals, schemes and reforms are announced here first, in the government's own words.
- **Read:** Releases on administrative reforms, digital governance and public grievance systems.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 5. UPSC previous year question papers

Union Public Service Commission. Free. https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers

- **Why:** The exam's own record of what it asks. It tells you which parts of your other sources deserve depth and which deserve a skim.
- **Read:** Governance questions in Mains GS2 since the current syllabus began. Group them by theme.
- **Revisions:** 2

**The trap.** Reading governance as theory and writing answers with no example or reform in them.

**The one rule.** Every answer: the problem, one example, one reform from ARC or a committee.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The full text of all fifteen ARC reports | Skip | Thousands of pages. The recommendation summaries give you what answers need. |
| A one-page list of key committees and what they recommended | Keep | Short, made by you, and useful across GS2, GS3 and GS4. |
| Public administration optional textbooks | Skip | Written for the optional paper. For GS they add theory you will not be asked for. |

## Ethics (GS4)

**Serves:** Mains GS4

### 1. UPSC GS Paper IV previous papers

Union Public Service Commission. Free. https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers

- **Why:** The paper's pattern has been steady since it began. Past papers teach you its language better than any book.
- **Read:** Every GS4 paper so far. Answer at least the case studies yourself, timed.
- **Revisions:** 3

### 2. Second ARC Fourth Report: Ethics in Governance

Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances. Free. https://darpg.gov.in/

- **Why:** The official Indian source on integrity, codes of conduct and corruption. Gives GS4 answers a strong reference.
- **Read:** The chapters on the ethical framework, codes of ethics for civil servants, and the summary of recommendations.
- **Skip:** Chapters on legal procedure and specific anti-corruption agencies in detail.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 3. Psychology (NCERT, Class 11 and Class 12): chapters on attitude, emotions and intelligence **[verify]**

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** The syllabus names attitude, emotional intelligence and aptitude. These chapters explain them simply and correctly.
- **Read:** The chapters on attitude and social cognition, intelligence, and motivation and emotion.
- **Skip:** The rest of both books.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 4. The Seven Principles of Public Life (Nolan Principles)

UK Committee on Standards in Public Life. Free. https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-7-principles-of-public-life

- **Why:** Seven short principles that answers on public service values often use. One page, easy to remember.
- **Read:** The one-page list of the seven principles and their meaning.

## Essay

**Serves:** Essay

### 1. UPSC Essay paper: previous papers

Union Public Service Commission. Free. https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers

- **Why:** Shows the two kinds of topics UPSC sets: philosophical and issue-based. Practise on the real ones.
- **Read:** All essay papers of the last ten years. Pick one topic a week and write it in full.
- **Revisions:** 2

### 2. Yojana and Kurukshetra magazines **[verify]**

Publications Division, Ministry of Information and Broadcasting. Free. https://www.publicationsdivision.nic.in/

- **Why:** Theme-based issues give balanced arguments and real examples. Good raw material for issue-based essays.
- **Read:** The issues whose theme matches a broad essay area: education, women, villages, technology, environment.
- **Skip:** Every issue cover to cover.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 3. One national newspaper's editorial page (your choice)

Paid.

- **Why:** Shows how good writers build an argument in a few hundred words. Reading for form, not just facts.
- **Read:** Two editorials a week, read slowly. Note how each one opens, balances and closes.
- **Revisions:** none; revise your own notes instead

**The trap.** Reading essay books and quote collections instead of writing essays.

**The one rule.** One full essay a week, timed, then a calm read-through the next day.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Essay compilation books | Maybe | Read two or three essays to see structure. Never copy their lines; examiners have read them too. |
| Memorised quote lists | Skip | One or two quotes you truly understand do more than twenty you squeeze in. |
| A pod-mate who reads your essay and tells you one thing | Keep | Honest, regular feedback on your real writing is the best essay resource there is. |

## CSAT

**Serves:** Prelims CSAT (GS II)

### 1. UPSC CSAT (Prelims GS Paper II) previous papers

Union Public Service Commission. Free. https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers

- **Why:** The fairest test of where you stand. CSAT's style is specific, and its own papers teach it best.
- **Read:** Take one recent paper early, timed, to see your level. Then work through the rest topic by topic.
- **Revisions:** 3

### 2. Mathematics (NCERT, Class 9 and Class 10)

NCERT. Free. https://ncert.nic.in/textbook.php

- **Why:** Rebuilds school maths calmly, for those who left it years ago.
- **Read:** Only chapters where PYQs show you are weak: number systems, linear equations, statistics, probability.
- **Skip:** Geometry proofs and constructions.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 3. Quantitative Aptitude for Competitive Examinations

R.S. Aggarwal, S. Chand. Paid.

- **Why:** A large bank of solved practice questions, chapter by chapter. Good for drilling the topics CSAT repeats.
- **Read:** Only chapters matching PYQ topics: percentages, ratio, averages, time and work, speed and distance, basic counting.
- **Skip:** Everything else. The book serves many exams; CSAT needs a small part.
- **Revisions:** 1

**The trap.** Ignoring CSAT because 'it's only qualifying', then missing the cut by a few marks.

**The one rule.** Take one timed past paper this month. Let the score decide how much CSAT you need.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A verbal and non-verbal reasoning book | Maybe | Helpful if reasoning PYQs are your weak area. Otherwise PYQs are enough. |
| A full CSAT test series | Maybe | Worth it only if your PYQ scores sit close to the qualifying line. |
| Shortcut-trick videos | Skip | Tricks without understanding fail under exam pressure. Basic methods, practised, hold up. |

## Current affairs

**Serves:** Prelims GS I, Mains GS1, Mains GS2, Mains GS3, Mains GS4, Essay, Interview

### 1. One national newspaper (your choice)

Paid.

- **Why:** The daily habit that links your static subjects to the real world. One paper is enough.
- **Read:** National news, the editorial and opinion pages on syllabus topics, and the economy page. Keep it to under an hour.
- **Skip:** City news, crime, sports, entertainment and day-to-day political sparring.
- **Revisions:** none; revise your own notes instead

### 2. Press Information Bureau

Press Information Bureau, Government of India. Free. https://pib.gov.in/

- **Why:** The government's own version of schemes, decisions and reports. Settles what a newspaper summary leaves unclear.
- **Read:** Releases on topics you met in the paper that week. Skim once a week, not daily.
- **Skip:** Every release on every ministry.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 3. PRS Legislative Research: Monthly Policy Review

PRS Legislative Research. Free. https://prsindia.org/

- **Why:** One neutral monthly summary of bills, policies and key decisions. A calm way to catch what you missed.
- **Read:** The monthly review, once a month.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 4. Yojana **[verify]**

Publications Division, Ministry of Information and Broadcasting. Free. https://www.publicationsdivision.nic.in/

- **Why:** A monthly, theme-based government magazine. Good for Mains depth on one theme at a time.
- **Read:** The articles closest to the syllabus in each issue, usually three or four.
- **Skip:** Articles far from the syllabus.
- **Revisions:** 1

### 5. India Year Book (Bharat: A Reference Annual) **[verify]**

Publications Division, Ministry of Information and Broadcasting. Paid. https://www.publicationsdivision.nic.in/

- **Why:** The official yearly reference on schemes, institutions and national facts. Useful for Prelims fact checks.
- **Read:** Chapters on polity, the economy, environment, welfare, and science.
- **Skip:** State-by-state profiles and long lists of awards and events.
- **Revisions:** 1

**The trap.** Three newspapers, two monthly magazines, four quiz channels, and no time left for the syllabus.

**The one rule.** One paper, one monthly source, and your own short notes. Revise the notes, not the paper.

| Commonly hoarded | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A monthly current affairs compilation | Maybe | One, used for revision, can help. Two or three of them just repeat each other. |
| A second newspaper | Skip | Doubles the reading time and adds very little that the first one missed. |
| Daily current affairs quiz channels | Maybe | A short weekly quiz helps recall. Daily alerts from many channels mostly add anxiety. |
