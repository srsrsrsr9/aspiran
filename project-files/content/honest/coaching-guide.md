## Do you need coaching at all?

Honest answer: some people do, many don't. Both are normal.

**Coaching can help if:**
- you need a fixed routine and people around you to keep going,
- you are new to the humanities and want someone to explain the basics,
- you learn better by listening than by reading,
- you want regular answer-writing feedback and can't find it elsewhere.

**You may not need it if:**
- you can follow a reading plan on your own,
- you can get honest feedback from a pod or a senior,
- the fee would put real strain on you or your family.

Many people clear this exam with coaching. Many clear it without. The syllabus, NCERTs, official reports and past papers are free. What you pay for is structure, explanation and feedback. Ask yourself which of those you actually lack. Then pay only for that, if you pay at all.

## What the consumer regulator has said

India's Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) works under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. On 13 November 2024 it issued the **Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisement in Coaching Sector, 2024**. In plain words:

- No false claims about selection rates, ranks, or "guaranteed" selection.
- An ad that shows a successful candidate must state their name, rank and the course they took, and whether they paid for it.
- Using a student's name or photo needs their written consent, taken after the result.
- Disclaimers must be in the same font size as the other key details.
- No false urgency, like "only a few seats left".

The CCPA has acted on this. In a May 2026 release it said it had issued more than 60 notices to coaching institutes and penalised 31, over ₹1.39 crore in all. A common finding: ads showed toppers without saying which course they had actually taken.

Under the Act, a misleading ad can draw a penalty of up to ₹10 lakh, and up to ₹50 lakh for a repeat.

## 10 questions to ask before you pay

Ask these calmly. A good institute will not mind. Write the answers down.

1. **Refund policy:** can I see it in writing, before I pay? What do I get back if I leave after one month?
2. **Batch size:** how many students sit in one class?
3. **Who teaches:** which teachers, by name, will teach my batch, and for how many sessions each?
4. **"Selections":** for each name you advertise, which course did they take, and did they pay for it?
5. **Trial classes:** can I sit in two or three regular classes, not a special demo?
6. **Full fee:** what is the total, broken up, including GST? Is anything charged later?
7. **Payment:** can I pay in instalments, without a penalty?
8. **What's included:** printed material, tests, answer evaluation, doubt sessions, library?
9. **If things change:** what happens if a teacher leaves or classes are cancelled?
10. **Feedback:** who checks my answers, how fast, and is it written or just a mark?

If an answer is vague, ask again. If it stays vague, that is your answer.

## Red flags

None of these alone proves an institute is bad. Together, they tell you to slow down.

- **"Guaranteed selection"** or "100% result". No one can promise this, and the coaching guidelines forbid false guarantees.
- **Countdown discounts**: "offer ends tonight", "last 5 seats". Real seats and real prices don't vanish at midnight.
- **Topper photo walls with no course details.** Ask which course each person took. Some joined only for an interview programme after clearing the earlier stages on their own.
- **Pressure to pay the full fee upfront**, especially before you have seen a regular class.
- **No written refund policy**, or one you are shown only after paying.
- **Big promises about teachers** who turn out to teach only a few sessions.
- **Rank talk everywhere**: how many in the top 10, the top 100. Your question is simpler: will this help *me* learn?

If you feel rushed, that feeling is useful. Step out, sleep on it, and talk to someone who has nothing to sell you.

## How to judge a test series

Before you buy, ask for one free sample test with its solutions. Then check four things.

**1. Do the questions feel like UPSC?** Put the sample next to a real past paper from upsc.gov.in. Same kinds of statements? Same level of trickiness? Or are they full of obscure facts no PYQ has ever asked?

**2. Are the explanations good?** A good explanation tells you why each wrong option is wrong, and where to read more. One line saying "Option B is correct" teaches you nothing.

**3. Is the analysis useful?** Look for a breakdown by subject and by type of mistake: didn't know, misread, guessed wrong. That shows you what to fix.

**4. For Mains: who evaluates, and how?** Ask for a sample evaluated answer. Are the comments specific? Or just a mark and "good attempt"?

What not to judge by: the all-India rank. It depends on who else took that test, not on whether the test is any good.

**Sources:**
- [UPSC previous year question papers](https://upsc.gov.in/examinations/previous-question-papers)

## How to use a test series well

A mock score is a reading on one day, not a verdict on you.

**Read your scores as a range.** Take your last five mock scores. Look at the lowest and the highest. That spread is your honest range. On exam day you could land anywhere in it. One good score doesn't mean you're safe. One bad score doesn't mean you're done.

**Spend more time on review than on the test.** For every wrong answer, write one line: what went wrong, and where you will fix it. That line is worth more than the score.

**Take fewer tests, reviewed well.** Writing tests back to back with no review is busy work. It feels productive and teaches little.

**Keep your rank to yourself if it hurts.** You don't owe anyone your mock rank, including your pod.

**Mix in real past papers.** They are free on upsc.gov.in and are the truest mock there is.

## The cost of a year

The coaching fee is usually the number people talk about. It is rarely the biggest one.

A year of preparation can include rent, food, a library seat, books, test series, travel home, phone and data, and coaching. There is also the cost no receipt shows: the salary or studies you are putting on hold.

We won't give you a "typical" total. There isn't one. A year in a big coaching hub and a year at home in a small town are very different, and your number is the only one that matters.

**Use the Cost of a Year calculator in My Path.** Put in your own numbers, line by line. Seeing the full total in one place helps you decide calmly: whether to take coaching, which parts of it, and for how long.

## If you were misled **[verify numbers]**

It happens to careful people too. It is not your fault, and you have options.

**1. Collect what you have.** Fee receipts, the ad or brochure, screenshots, chats, the refund policy, emails. Note dates.

**2. Write to the institute first.** Say what was promised, what happened, and what you want (a refund, for example). Keep a copy.

**3. Contact the National Consumer Helpline (NCH).** It is free.
- Call **1915** or **1800-11-4000** (8 am to 8 pm, except national holidays)
- WhatsApp or SMS: **8800001915**
- The **NCH app** or the **UMANG** app
- Online: **consumerhelpline.gov.in**

**4. If that doesn't settle it**, you can file a case with the consumer commission online through **e-Jagriti**, the government's consumer case portal.

You can also tell your pod. You don't have to name anyone in public. Talking it through helps, and it may protect the next person.

## A note for parents

If you are paying for your child's coaching, thank you. That is a real sacrifice. Here is one honest thing to keep in mind. A bigger fee, a famous name or a wall of topper photos does not raise your child's chances by itself. What helps is steady study, honest feedback, enough sleep, and a home where a bad mock or a missed attempt can be talked about without fear. Before you pay, sit with your child and go through the ten questions above together. Ask for the refund policy in writing. Don't let a "today only" offer rush either of you. And whatever the result, your child is more than this exam. Telling them that, out loud, may be the most useful thing you do all year.
