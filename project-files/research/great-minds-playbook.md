# Great minds in UPSC Pods: what to borrow, what to leave out

Written 10 Oct 2026 (second round added the same day as section D, and quoting and crediting as section E) for the "Ideas from great minds" thread. Nothing here is built yet. Each change names the thing it touches and the thread that owns it.

## Short answer

It is not too late. The app is pre-launch, and almost everything below is copy, content rules or small flow changes to screens that already exist. Several ideas are already in the app without being named: three tasks a day (fewer decisions), no public streak, a finished screen at the end of every game, Close the Day and Lamp Off. This document names the science behind those so they don't get diluted, and adds what is missing.

The filter used throughout: our audience is often lonely, under family pressure, distrustful of hype and low at night. A technique is in only if it helps the aspirant study or feel better. Anything that works by pressure (loss framing, scarcity, urgency, variable rewards) is out, and the last section says what was dropped and why.

## A. App changes (owner: "App brainstorm and build")

**A1. Today's tasks are time, not output.** Touches: Today's 3 tasks.
Oakley: procrastination is avoiding the small pain of starting, and the pain fades within minutes of starting. "Finish Polity chapter 4" triggers it; "25 minutes on Polity" doesn't. Fogg: make the start tiny.
- Each task shows minutes and a subject, e.g. "25 min · Polity (Laxmikanth ch. 4)", not "finish chapter 4".
- Each task has a "Just 5 minutes" start button. If they stop at 5, the task counts as started, not failed.
- Ticking a task gives a small, quiet acknowledgement (Fogg's "shine"), never confetti or points.

**A2. Close the Day: three new lines.** Touches: Close the Day, Lamp Off.
- Line 1, "What I did today" (not "what I missed"). The default chip list is things done.
- Line 2, "One thing I can say from memory": they type one fact or idea without looking. This is retrieval practice (Roediger and Karpicke), the strongest cheap learning technique there is, and it beats the illusion of competence that rereading gives (Oakley).
- Line 3, "Tomorrow's first step": an if-then plan (Gollwitzer). The field is pre-shaped: "After ____, I'll ____ for 5 minutes." Next morning, this line is task 1 on Today. Leaving tomorrow's start half-open also uses the Zeigarnik pull.
- After a day marked "slipped", the reply is self-compassionate, not cheerful: "Slipped today. Most people do some days. Tomorrow's first step is ready." Wohl and colleagues found students who forgave themselves for procrastinating procrastinated less on the next exam.
- Close the Day always ends on Lamp Off or a calm finished screen (peak-end rule: people remember an experience by its best or worst moment and its end).

**A3. A private planning mirror in the weekly rhythm.** Touches: the private weekly rhythm, Today.
Kahneman's planning fallacy: we plan from the best case, not from what usually happens. The cure is the "outside view" (reference class).
- Close the Day already knows planned vs done minutes. Once a week, privately: "You usually finish about 7 of every 10 planned minutes. Next week's plan is sized to that." With one tap to accept or ignore.
- Wording is calibration, never a grade. No red. Nobody else sees it.

**A4. Monday WOOP, as a fresh start.** Touches: weekly rhythm, Pod weekly goal.
Oettingen found positive fantasies alone lower effort; adding the obstacle and a plan raises it. Milkman found people start new behaviour more easily at "fresh starts" like Mondays.
- Monday's first open shows four short private fields: Wish for the week, best Outcome, main Obstacle ("my phone after 10 pm"), Plan ("If it's 10 pm, phone goes to the other room").
- Optional: share only the wish to the pod. The pod's weekly goal stays chosen by members and phrased as process ("everyone writes 4 answers"), which keeps Cialdini's commitment effect without public scoring.
- Skippable, and skipping is silent.

**A5. Spaced recall built into Chai.** Touches: Chai cards, Quiz night, Practice "Learn and play".
Spacing and testing are the two best-replicated findings in learning science.
- 1 day: top of today's Chai shows one question from yesterday's card ("Yesterday's chai: which body grants GI tags?"). One tap, then it's gone.
- 7 days: Friday's quiz already revisits the week. Make it pull first from cards the person got wrong or skipped.
- 30 days: a monthly "Remember these?" pack, built from what they missed.
- Store the per-card result on the phone only; no server needed, works offline and on low-end phones.

**A6. Rooms: focus and break, with a real break.** Touches: Pod study room, soundscapes.
Oakley's focused and diffuse modes: insight often arrives when you step away.
- An optional, self-started focus block (25 on, 5 off) for the person, not the room. This is not a countdown on content; the "no timers" rule for games stays.
- The break screen suggests standing, water, looking out of a window. It never suggests opening the feed.

**A7. Peak-end on every finish.** Touches: Practice answer feedback, Quiz night, Learn and play finished screens.
- Answer feedback order: one fix first, then one specific strength last ("Your example from Kerala's floods was well chosen").
- Finished screens name one thing they got right, specifically, not "Great job!".

**A8. Mocks shown as a range, never one number.** Touches: Practice, My Path analytics (when mocks arrive).
Kahneman: narrow framing (judging each gamble alone) makes losses sting more; broad framing calms it. Single mock scores are also noisy.
- Show the last five as a band ("Your range: 82 to 104"), with the newest as a dot inside it.
- A bad mock inside the usual range gets the line "Inside your normal range."

**A9. Chotu at night.** Touches: Chotu prompts.
The distress check always runs first and wins; nothing here replaces it or a human reply.
- Rumination after a bad day: Chotu offers distanced self-talk (Kross): "If a podmate told you this, what would you say to them?" or uses their name in the third person.
- Catastrophising about one result: Kahneman's focusing illusion ("Nothing in life is as important as you think it is while you are thinking about it") in plain words: "Tonight this feels like everything. Let's look at it again tomorrow, after Lamp Off."
- After 11 pm, Chotu nudges toward Lamp Off rather than more study (sleep is when memory consolidates; Oakley cites this often).

**A10. My Path and Plan B: honest numbers, both frames.** Touches: My Path calculators, Plan B.
- Use natural frequencies (Gigerenzer): "Of every 1,000 people who sat Prelims in 2026, about 24 reached Mains and about 2 were finally selected." Derived from the brief's 1.1 numbers; verify against upsc.gov.in before launch.
- People in a loss frame take bigger risks (prospect theory). A repeater deciding on another attempt is often in that frame. So the Plan B screen shows the same choice both ways: what another year gives and costs, and what a Plan B gives and costs. It never recommends either.
- A sunk-cost line: "The years you've already put in count either way. This choice is only about the next one."

**A11. Notifications hang on a routine, not a clock.** Touches: onboarding, notifications.
Fogg: behaviour follows a prompt anchored to an existing habit.
- Onboarding asks "When do you usually have your morning chai?" and sends at most one daily nudge then: "Chai's ready." No loss copy ("Don't lose your progress").
- Easy to switch off, and silence is respected after two ignored nudges.

**A12. Plug cognitive leaks.** Touches: Today, navigation, Pod.
Sierra: every unnecessary decision, badge or guilt cue drains the willpower the user needs for the real work.
- One decision per screen at most; remember last choices (language, room, soundscape).
- No unread counts on Pod; show presence ("3 people in the room now"), not completion ratios ("6 of 8 done"), which invite comparison.

## B. Content changes (owner: "Content building plan")

**C1. Name the trap in Exam Corner.** Touches: `chai-pack@1` → `examCorner`.
UPSC options are built to catch System 1 (fast, intuitive) answers. Teaching the trap types slows people down at the right moment.
- Add `trapType` to each wrong option: `extreme-word` ("only", "always"), `half-true`, `right-fact-wrong-reason`, `recent-news-bias`, `sounds-familiar`.
- Feedback line names it in both languages: "Trap: extreme word. 'Only' is almost never right in Polity."
- The Friday quiz can end with "This week you dodged 3 extreme-word traps."

**C2. Ask first, explain second.** Touches: `explainers/article-200.json` scene 1, timelines.
Pre-questions improve learning even when the guess is wrong, and an open question pulls people through (Loewenstein's information gap; Sugarman's curiosity).
- Ek Minute scene 1 asks the question ("Can a Governor sit on a Bill forever?"); the last scene answers it.
- Timeline story opens with "Guess: how long did it take to write the Constitution?" before the dates.

**C3. New type: "Spot the better answer".** Touches: Learn and play, new schema.
Sierra's perceptual exposure: experts are built by seeing many good and bad examples fast, not by reading rules.
- Two intros (or conclusions, or diagrams) to the same Mains question, side by side. Tap the better one, see one line why ("Defines the term before arguing").
- 6 pairs a round, under 3 minutes, no score shown to anyone. Hindi and English pairs written natively, not translated.
- Follows the pack's rules: no timer, a finished screen, draft review block.

**C4. Chunks that link.** Touches: `takeaway`, `lensChips`, Quiz night.
Oakley's chunking: a fact becomes usable once it's linked to others.
- Each card's `takeaway` is one complete chunk that can stand alone.
- Add a `syllabus` tag (GS paper and topic). Friday's quiz ends with a small "This week's chunks" map grouped by GS paper.

**C5. Mix topics in practice.** Touches: `quizzes/`, `games/match-the-article.json`.
Interleaving beats blocked practice for exams that mix topics.
- Quiz packs and game rounds mix subjects across the week instead of one subject per pack.

**C6. A writing guide for cards.** Touches: the card template and writing rules.
- Headline's only job is to get the summary read; the summary's job is to get "why it matters" read (Sugarman's slippery slide).
- Every card has one concrete image or example and one unexpected detail (Heath's Made to Stick: simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, story).
- Specific numbers beat adjectives ("₹12,000 crore", not "huge").

**C7. Number Sense, Rosling-style.** Touches: `numbers/` schema.
- Every figure answers "compared to what?" (the reference line already does this; make a text line mandatory too).
- Rates as natural frequencies where possible ("943 women for every 1,000 men").
- A `trap` line naming the misreading people usually make ("A rise from 940 to 943 is small, not a turnaround").

**C8. Myth vs Fact as a "truth sandwich".** Touches: `myths/forward-buster-constitution.json` and schema.
The Debunking Handbook: repeating the myth first can strengthen it.
- Order: fact first, then "A forward claims…" with a warning, then why the myth is wrong, then the fact again.
- The share image shows the fact as the headline, never the myth.

**C9. Two Sides ends with their own line.** Touches: `debates/` schema.
Self-explanation is a strong learning technique.
- Last screen: "In one line, which side would you take, and why?" Saved privately; optionally posted to the pod.

**C10. Lamp Off script.** Touches: `winddown/lamp-off.json`.
- One line that sleep is when today's study gets stored, so stopping now is part of studying.
- One self-compassion line for hard days. No study content after this screen.

**C11. Repeater stories: decisions, not outcomes.** Touches: alumni and repeater stories (also used by marketing).
Annie Duke's "resulting": judging a decision by its result breeds shame.
- Template: the decision I made, what I knew then, what happened, what I'd keep, what I'd change.

## C. Marketing changes (owner: "Marketing plan for 1M downloads")

**M1. Sell what the aspirant becomes.** Touches: Play Store screenshots, listing.
Sierra: people don't want a better app, they want to be better at the thing.
- Screenshot captions describe the person: "Explain Article 200 in a minute", "Spot an exam trap before it gets you", "Close the day without the 11 pm guilt". Not "1-minute explainers".

**M2. Honesty first, specific always.** Touches: Play listing, week-1 posts, launch kit.
Sugarman: admitting a limitation up front makes everything after it more believable. Short first sentence. Specifics build credibility.
- Open the listing with what we won't do: "No rank promises. No countdowns. No toppers' secret notes."
- Then specifics: "3 cards with your morning chai. About 4 minutes. In Hindi and English."

**M3. Curiosity that pays off in the same post.** Touches: week-1 posts, Instagram carousels.
- Post the Exam Corner question as slide 1; give the answer and the trap name on the last slide. No "answer in the app" bait.

**M4. Honest social proof and unity.** Touches: launch kit, store listing, posts.
Cialdini: people follow people like them, and "we" beats "you".
- Use real, named (with consent) beta-pod quotes and places ("from a pod in Mukherjee Nagar"). No inflated counts until they're true.
- Voice is "from aspirants, for aspirants".

**M5. Give before asking.** Touches: WhatsApp Channel, Telegram.
Reciprocity: the daily Chai card is free on the Channel with no install wall. The app is offered as "where your pod lives", not as the price of the content.

**M6. A refund window, said out loud.** Touches: sprint pricing pages (later).
Sugarman's satisfaction conviction: remove the buyer's risk and say so plainly. Already a brief guardrail; make it a headline, not fine print.

**M7. Tagline and onboarding ride the chai habit.** Touches: tagline options, onboarding copy.
Fogg's anchor, and the app already owns the chai motif.
- Tagline to test: "One Chai with your chai." (Hindi: «आपकी चाय के साथ एक Chai») — have the Hindi editor rework it.

**M8. Parent page: a role, not a scoreboard.** Touches: Parent page.
Parents often watch from a loss frame. Give them a specific, kind job.
- "Ask about the week, not the rank." "The numbers, honestly: of every 1,000 who sit Prelims, about 2 are selected. Effort is in their hands; the cut-off isn't."

**M9. Design the moments.** Touches: result-season waiting rooms, launch calendar.
Heath's Power of Moments: people remember peaks and transitions.
- Prelims eve note from the pod lead, result-day waiting room, a "fresh page" on the morning after results. No upsell in any of them (already a brief guardrail).

**M10. Time campaigns to fresh starts.** Touches: launch calendar.
Milkman: new beginnings make change easier.
- Post-Prelims restart, New Year, the Monday after results. The notification date (Jan 2027) is framed as "a fresh start", never as a deadline.

### App (owner: "App brainstorm and build")

**A13. The aspirant is the hero; Chotu is the guide.** Touches: Chotu copy, finished screens, Wins of the Week.
- Copy credits the person ("You explained Article 200"), never the app or Chotu ("Chotu helped you…").

**A14. "Know the paper, know yourself" on My Path.** Touches: My Path, Practice results.
- Cross the person's weak topics (from Exam Corner and quiz results) with how often UPSC asks about each topic in past papers, and show "where an hour pays most this week". Private, and it suggests rather than assigns.

**A15. Pod lead as consigliere, and a favours board.** Touches: Pod screen, pod lead role.
- An "Ask the lead privately" button, so a member can get calm advice without posting in the group.
- A small "Ask a favour / Offer help" strip in the pod (review my intro, explain this topic, share notes). Nothing is counted or ranked. Distress posts always go to moderators, whatever the pod says.

### Content (owner: "Content building plan")

**C12. New type: "Story Spine" for Essay and GS4.** Touches: Learn and play, new schema.
- Take one Chai news item and show it as a five-line story intro using Pixar's spine ("Every day… One day… Because of that… Until finally… And ever since…"), then ask the person to write their own for a given essay topic. Optional pod sharing.

**C13. A Kautilya lens, with sources.** Touches: `lensChips`, governance cards, Myth vs Fact.
- A "Kautilya says" chip on governance, tax and administration cards, with the *Arthashastra* book and chapter cited, which also gives GS4 and Essay answers a ready quote.
- A Myth vs Fact pack: "Did Chanakya really say this?", covering popular forwarded quotes.

**C14. "Should I guess?" with negative marking.** Touches: Number Sense or Practice.
- This is Sun Tzu's "choose your battles" made literal. GS Paper I gives +2 for a right answer and takes away 0.66 for a wrong one. A blind guess between 4 options is worth about 0 on average, a guess between 3 is worth about +0.22, and a guess between 2 is worth about +0.67. So "eliminate two options, then guess" is sound advice and "never guess" is not. Verify the marking scheme against the 2027 notice.

**C15. Memory hooks for lists.** Touches: cards (optional field), Match the Article.
- An optional `memoryHook` (acronym, image or story) for list-type facts such as the Fundamental Duties, written natively in Hindi too (Yandamuri; Oakley also recommends memory tricks). Use these for lists only, never as a stand-in for understanding.

**C16. Repeater stories with an honest shape.** Touches: C11's template.
- The hero's-journey order (the ordinary world, the call, the ordeal, what I brought back), combined with C11's decision questions. The "return" can be a new path, not only a selection.

**C17. A monthly "Strategy corner".** Touches: Chai or the weekly rhythm.
- One sourced line from Sun Tzu, Kautilya or the *Gita* (sources from the syllabus are a bonus), turned into one question about the person's own preparation: "Know yourself: which GS paper have you avoided this month?" No battle language aimed at people.

### Marketing (owner: "Marketing plan for 1M downloads")

**M11. Empowerment, never inadequacy.** Touches: all copy, ads, store listing.
- Sachs: inadequacy marketing ("Falling behind?", "Toppers know what you don't") sells by making people feel small. It is banned outright. Empowerment marketing shows the aspirant as the hero and the app as the mentor.

**M12. One honest origin story.** Touches: launch video, Play listing's long description, About.
- Told on the story spine: why the app exists, with the moral in one line: "Nobody should prepare alone."

**M13. Regional voices for regional hubs.** Touches: Hyderabad and Hindi-belt outreach.
- Yandamuri and Khera reach people through their own language and a familiar mentor voice. Partner with Telugu- and Hindi-speaking creators who teach study craft. Make it clear the app is in English and Hindi.

**M14. A wink, not a threat.** Touches: light posts.
- A text-only riff for fun posts: "An offer you can refuse: free forever, no rank promises, leave whenever you like." Don't use film stills (rights).

## E. Quoting and crediting the greats

Added 10 Oct at the user's request: quote these thinkers where it helps, encourage aspirants to read them, and show that the app stands on their shoulders, with pictures and voices where possible.

### What's safe, and what isn't

| Ask | Do | Don't |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quote them | Short, exact quotes (a sentence or two) with author, book and chapter. Check every one against the book, because many popular quotes are misattributed. The Hindi translation is done by our Hindi editor and labelled as a translation. | Long passages from copyrighted books, or paraphrases presented inside quote marks. |
| Encourage reading | A reading shelf with free or low-cost routes first. Disclose any affiliate link. | Selling the books as a shortcut to selection. |
| Pictures | Our own illustrated portraits in the app's illustration style, labelled with the name. Sun Tzu and Chanakya have no real portraits, so any picture of them is an artist's impression and should say so. | Photos copied from the web. These are owned by the photographers. Also don't put a living person's face in ads or store screenshots without their written permission. It implies they endorse us, and Indian courts protect personality rights. Kahneman (d. 2024) and Sugarman (d. 2022) are covered too, through their estates. No film stills from *The Godfather*. |
| Voices | Link to their own recorded talks and courses, for example Oakley's free *Learning How to Learn* course and Kahneman's recorded lectures, which open in the original player. Write to the living authors (Oakley, Sierra, Fogg, Khera, Yandamuri) asking for a short recorded message for aspirants; some may say yes. For Sun Tzu and Chanakya, a human narrator reads public-domain translations, labelled "Read by …". | AI voices imitating real people. That's a deepfake, Indian courts have stopped it (the Anil Kapoor and Arijit Singh cases), and it would break the trust the whole app rests on. |

## F. Indian thinkers

Added 11 Oct at the user's request ("the exam is Indian"). This round is also syllabus. GS4 asks for "contributions of moral thinkers and philosophers from India and the world", Essay answers lean on Indian voices, and polity, economy and history are full of them. The filter is the same as before, plus three Indian-specific rules:
- **Balance.** Cover many regions, languages, faiths and eras, and give no one tradition the final word. Religious texts are quoted as philosophy and literature. They never tell anyone how to worship.
- **Fake quotes are everywhere.** WhatsApp is full of misattributed lines from Kalam, Vivekananda, Buddha and Chanakya. Every quote needs a checked source, and the fakes become Myth vs Fact material.
- **No political flashpoints.** These are thinkers, not mascots for a party. Cards stick to what they wrote and did, and to how an aspirant can use it in an answer.
- **Older voices first (user decision, 11 Oct).** Gandhi, Ambedkar and Sen are left out as featured thinkers. Lean on older figures such as Kautilya (Chanakya), Thiruvalluvar, the Gita, Patanjali, the Buddha and the Bhakti saints. Ambedkar still appears in plain factual polity content, such as chairing the Drafting Committee.

## G. Routines and calming sound

Added 11 Oct at the user's request: calming audio such as binaural beats for stressed moments, and a routine builder an aspirant can customise like an exercise plan, drawing on the habit and routine books listed below. The filter is the same, plus one rule: **no health claims.** We say what a tool is and let people judge how it feels for them.

### Calming sound

| Option | What the evidence says | How we use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Binaural beats (two slightly different tones, one in each ear) | Mixed. Some small studies report less anxiety; reviews find the effects inconsistent. It needs headphones to work at all. | Offer it as "Calm tones (headphones)", described as something some people find relaxing. Never call it therapy, and never say it changes brainwaves. |
| Slow breathing (about 6 breaths a minute, or the double-inhale "physiological sigh") | Stronger: controlled trials show lower stress and better mood after a few minutes a day (e.g. Balban et al., 2023). Works without headphones. | The main tool: a 1-, 3- or 5-minute guided breath with a gentle visual and optional tones underneath. |
| Soundscapes (rain, waves, library) | Pleasant, helps mask noise in hostels and shared rooms; no strong claims. | Already built (6 beds). Reused here. |
| Body scan, grounding (5-4-3-2-1) | Reasonable evidence for in-the-moment calm. | Short text and voice versions, read by a human. |

**Rules**
- Calm is never a substitute for help. When the distress check fires, the helpline sheet comes first and calm tools come second ("While you wait, a slow breath can help").
- Sound is off by default and has a volume limit. Calm tones carry a short note: use headphones at low volume, and don't use while driving.
- Tones and beds are generated in code like the existing sound effects, so there are no licensing issues.

## What we're leaving out, and why

| Technique | Source | Why it's out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Streak loss ("Don't lose your 12-day streak") | Loss aversion | Weaponises the strongest bias against tired, anxious people. User already approved dropping streaks. |
| Scarcity and urgency ("Only 50 seats", countdowns) | Sugarman's urgency trigger, Cialdini's scarcity | The audience is burned by exactly this from coaching ads; brief guardrail 2. |
| Greed and exclusivity triggers ("secret notes", "toppers only") | Sugarman | Feeds the shortcut-seeking the brief says to counter. |
| Variable rewards and endless feeds | Nir Eyal's Hooked | Builds dependence; the brief says to encourage offline life. |
| Leaderboards, completion ratios, "X% finished" | Social proof | Turns into shame and comparison. |
| Decoy pricing | Anchoring | Manipulative; sprint prices should stand on their own. |
| Willpower meters | Ego depletion | The research failed to replicate. |
| War framing of the exam or of other aspirants | Sun Tzu | Feeds isolation and stress; the paper is a puzzle, not an enemy. |
| Saam-daam-dand-bhed, unsourced quotes | Chanakya | Manipulation as a tactic; fake quotes erode trust. |
| Winners vs losers | Shiv Khera | False and cruel when about 2 in 1,000 are selected. |
| Offers you can't refuse, secrecy | The Godfather | Coercion; secrecy breaks the distress protocol. |
| Inadequacy marketing | Story Wars (named there as the thing to avoid) | Sells by making people feel small. |
| AI voice clones, web photos, implied endorsement | Request to use pictures and voices | Deepfakes and personality-rights violations; use illustrations, real recordings and permission instead. |
| Thinkers as party or faith mascots | Indian thinkers round | Divides a mixed audience; we stick to what they wrote and did. |
| Gandhi, Ambedkar and Sen as featured thinkers | User decision, 11 Oct | The user asked for older thinkers instead; Ambedkar stays only in factual polity content. |
| Unsourced Kalam, Vivekananda or Buddha quotes | Indian thinkers round | Most viral ones are fake; use them as myth packs instead. |
| Health claims for binaural beats | Routines round | Evidence is mixed; we describe, not promise. |
| "Never miss twice" as a rule, topper timetables as prescriptions | Clear, Nguyen | Turns one bad day into guilt; routines are menus, not rules. |
| Growth-mindset slogans | Dweck | Effects are small and mixed; we use concrete process feedback (A1, A7) instead. |
