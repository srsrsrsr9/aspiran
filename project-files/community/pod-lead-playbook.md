# Pod lead playbook

> **DRAFT for human review. Not approved. Do not use with members until the safety lead and a qualified mental-health professional have signed off.**

Version 0.1 · drafted 11 Oct 2026

Hey, and thank you. You've said yes to looking after a small group of 6 to 8 aspirants, on top of your own preparation or your own job. That's a big deal. This playbook is here so you don't have to figure it all out alone.

Read it once properly. After that, dip in when you need a template or you're not sure what to do.

---

## 1. Your role, and its limits

**You are:** the friendly host of your pod. You welcome people, keep the weekly rhythm going, notice when someone's gone quiet, and make sure nobody feels like the odd one out.

**You are not:** a counsellor, a coach, a topper on a pedestal, or on call 24/7. You don't need to know every answer. You don't need to fix anyone's life.

What you do:

- Welcome new members like a human, not a bot.
- Post the weekly prompts (or let the app post them and add a line of your own).
- Run the Monday check-in.
- Encourage answer-writing and kind, useful feedback.
- Notice who's struggling or quiet, and check in privately.
- **Hand anything serious to a wellbeing moderator. Every time.**
- Keep the pod a place without bragging, selling or shaming.

What you don't do:

- Handle a crisis on your own.
- Give medical, legal or "professional" advice.
- Promise results.
- Sell anything. (More on this in section 11.)

## 2. The weekly rhythm

This is the default week. Bend it to fit your pod. A working-professional pod might do everything at weekends, and that's fine.

| Day | What happens | Your bit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mon** | Weekly goals post and pod check-in | Post the check-in. Reply to everyone who answers, even with a thumbs-up. |
| **Tue / Thu** | Answer-writing hour: submit, peer review, mentor comments | Nudge people to post one answer. Model good feedback. |
| **Wed** | Resource Court: keep or skip decisions on books and sources | Keep it honest. Watch for sneaky selling. |
| **Fri** | Fun night: quiz, meme roast | Join in. Keep jokes kind. Nobody's attempt number is a punchline. |
| **Sat** | Live session: topic strategy, or a repeater panel | Share the link. Encourage quiet members to just listen. |
| **Sun** | Wins of the week, "close the week", and a rest prompt | Name small wins. Remind people rest counts. |
| **Daily** | Morning target, one Mains question, evening "close the day" | You don't have to post daily. Just glance in once. |

Rough time: about **[__ hours a week, team to confirm]**. If it's taking much more, tell your moderator. That's a sign to change something, not to push harder.

## 3. Welcoming new members

The first 72 hours decide whether someone becomes a member or a lurker. A human hello beats any automated message. So please write your welcome yourself, even if you start from these.

### Within the first few hours

> Hi [name], welcome to [pod name]! I'm [your name], the pod lead here. I'm on my [nth] attempt / I cleared in [year] / I'm preparing alongside work, so I get how this goes.
>
> No pressure to post anything yet. If you'd like a tiny first step, try today's 5-minute answer or the 5 quick questions. And if you ever want to talk privately, just tap "Ask the lead privately".
>
> Glad you're here.

**For the pod (optional, with their OK)**

> Everyone, please say hi to [name], who's joined us. [One line they're happy to share, e.g. "2nd attempt, Hindi medium, Political Science optional".]

Don't share anything about a new member they haven't put on their profile themselves.

### Day 3 check-in

> Hey [name], just checking in. How's the first few days been? Is the pod's pace OK for you? If anything's confusing in the app or you'd like a different time for check-ins, tell me.

If they haven't posted at all, that's fine. Lurking is allowed. Keep it light:

> No need to reply. Just wanted you to know the door's open. Monday's check-in is a nice easy way to join in.

### Day 14 "first win"

Find something real, however small. Did they post one answer? Turn up to a room? Finish a chapter? Give feedback to someone?

> [Name], two weeks in! You [wrote your first answer on federalism / gave Aman really useful feedback on his intro / showed up to three study rooms]. That counts. Nice going.

Credit them, not the app. "You explained Article 200", not "the app helped you".

If you genuinely can't find a win, ask instead:

> Two weeks in! What's one thing that's gone OK for you, even a tiny one?

## 4. Running the Monday check-in

Keep it short and safe. People should be able to answer in 30 seconds.

**Sample check-in post**

> Good morning, pod! Monday check-in:
> 1. One thing you want to get done this week.
> 2. How's your energy, from 1 to 5? (Skip this if you'd rather not say.)
> 3. Anything getting in the way?
>
> I'll go first: [your answer].

Tips:

- **Go first.** It shows it's safe to be honest, and it's fine if your own week was rubbish.
- **Reply to everyone,** even with one line.
- **Don't compare.** If one person says "30 hours this week" and another says "5", celebrate both for showing up.
- **Low energy scores aren't an emergency.** A 1 or 2 deserves a private "how are you doing?" later. See section 7.
- **Never post anyone's mood, energy or private answers publicly.** Their mood tap is private unless they choose otherwise.

## 5. Giving peer feedback on answers

Feedback in pods should make people want to write again. The rule is: **one fix first, one specific strength last.**

Why that order? The fix is the useful part, so lead with it while they're paying attention. Ending on a real strength means they finish reading feeling able, not crushed.

**The shape**

1. **One fix** (the most important one, not five)
2. **How to do it** (a concrete suggestion)
3. **One specific strength** (not "good answer", but what exactly was good)

**Example**

> **One fix:** your intro jumps straight into points. Try a 3-line intro: define the term, give the context, take a stance.
> **For example:** "Cooperative federalism means the Centre and states work as partners. GST Council is a recent example. But fiscal tensions are testing it."
> **What worked:** your use of the Sarkaria and Punchhi Commission recommendations in the body was spot on, and well placed.

Things to avoid:

- Long lists of everything wrong.
- "This is weak" or "not UPSC level". Say what to change instead.
- Comparing to a topper copy in a way that makes them feel small.
- Comments on their English or Hindi, unless they asked for language help.
- Sharing the answer outside the pod without their consent.

Teach this shape to your pod. Pin the example.

## 6. Handling conflict

Disagreements happen, especially about current affairs, politics and "which book is best". Most are healthy. Here's when and how to step in.

**Step in when:**

- It gets personal ("you're clearly a [group]", insults, mockery).
- It's about caste, religion, region, gender or language in a hurtful way.
- It's party campaigning rather than policy discussion.
- Someone's being shamed for their attempt number, age or medium.

**How:**

1. **Cool it publicly, briefly.** "Let's keep this to the policy, not each other. Both sides have a point worth putting in an answer."
2. **Talk privately.** Message the people involved separately. Be curious first: "Hey, that thread got heated. Everything OK?"
3. **Point to the rules kindly** if needed. "Just a reminder we keep politics to both-sides discussion, no campaigning."
4. **Close the thread** if it won't calm down. That's not taking sides.
5. **Bring in a moderator** for anything involving harassment, hate, threats, sexual comments, doxxing or repeated rule-breaking. Don't try to judge serious stuff yourself.

You don't apply the bigger steps of the consequences ladder (mutes, removals). Moderators do.

## 7. Spotting distress and handing over

This is the most important section. Please read it twice.

### What to look out for

- Hopeless words: "sab khatam", "antha aipoyindi", "what's the point", "I can't do this anymore".
- Feeling like a burden: "everyone would be better off without me", "main bojh hoon".
- Goodbye-sounding messages, or apologising to family out of nowhere.
- Giving away books or notes with "won't need them any more".
- Someone who posted every day going silent, especially after a result.
- Posting at 3 a.m. night after night with low messages.

The full warning signs are in `crisis-protocol.md`, section 1. Your moderator will train you.

### What to do

**If anything sounds like danger, or you're unsure:**

1. **Reply kindly right away**, so they're not left hanging:
   > I'm really glad you told us. I'm here. I'm also bringing in [moderator name] from our wellbeing team, who's good at this.
2. **Hand over to your wellbeing moderator immediately.** Use "Get help for this post", and message them directly too.
3. **If you think they're in danger right now,** share the helplines: 112 for emergencies, Tele-MANAS on 14416. (Numbers are pinned in the app. Use the pinned ones, as they're the checked version.)
4. **Stay kind and present** until the moderator has picked it up. Then let them lead.

**For a low patch (quiet, tired, low mood, nothing alarming):**

> Hey, I noticed you've been a bit quiet. No pressure at all. Just wanted to say I'm around if you want to talk, about UPSC or anything else.

If it carries on for more than a week, or gets heavier, tell your moderator.

### Two rules you never break

1. **Never handle it alone.** Even if you're experienced. Even if they ask you not to tell anyone. Even if it's 2 a.m.
2. **Never promise secrecy when safety is at risk.** You can say:
   > I care about you, so I can't keep this just between us. I'm bringing in someone from our wellbeing team so you've got more support. You're not in trouble.

Other things to avoid:

- Don't argue, lecture or say "stay strong" or "think of your parents".
- Don't investigate or ask for details of how they'd hurt themselves.
- Don't tell the rest of the pod what's going on. If the pod asks, say "they're being looked after".
- Don't try to be their therapist. You're their pod lead, and that's enough.

## 8. Result day and failed attempts

Result days are the hardest days in a pod. Here's the plan.

**The week before**

- Re-read this section and section 7.
- Check who your moderator is on the day.

**The morning of the result**

> Morning, everyone. Whatever today brings, this pod isn't going anywhere, and neither am I. No predictions or cut-off talk in here today please. If you want to talk privately, I'm a message away.

**By the evening, message every member privately, whatever their result.**

For someone who didn't clear:

> I'm really sorry about today. It's one paper on one day, and it doesn't define you. You don't have to decide anything about next year tonight. Can I check on you tomorrow?

For someone who did clear:

> Congratulations, that's huge! Celebrate properly. The wins space is open whenever you want to share. In the pod, a few people are having a rough day, so maybe let's keep it gentle in here.

For someone who's gone quiet:

> No need to reply. Just thinking of you today.

**What not to do on result day**

- No "how many cleared from our pod?" posts.
- No posting roll numbers, marks or ranks in the pod.
- No "next attempt plan" pressure for at least a week.
- No links to courses or sprints. There are no upsells in the app for 72 hours after a result, and that goes for pod leads too.

**The next morning**

> Fresh page today. Nothing to do except eat, sleep and be kind to yourself. We'll pick things up when you're ready.

Watch for anyone who goes silent after the result. Check in privately by day 2. If they post anything hopeless, hand over to your moderator (section 7).

## 9. Repeaters and dignity

Repeaters are the most experienced people in UPSC prep. In our pods, they're the experts, not the cautionary tale.

- **Attempt tags are optional** and always respected. "4th attempt, here to rebuild" is a badge of experience.
- **Never joke about attempt numbers or age.** Not even gently. Not even if they joke about it first.
- **Invite repeaters to share what they've learned,** if they want to, like on the Saturday repeater panel.
- **Don't push anyone about "how many attempts left"** or Plan B. That's their call, in their time. If they bring it up, the Plan B resources and My Path tools are there.
- **Watch for shame.** Someone hiding their attempt number or lying about years is often carrying a lot. Make the pod a place they don't have to.

## 10. Boundaries and burnout

You're a volunteer, and very likely an aspirant too. Look after yourself.

- **Set your hours.** Put them in your pod profile, like "Usually around 7 to 9 p.m." You don't have to reply at midnight.
- **You're not the emergency service.** For anything urgent outside your hours, the app shows helplines and the wellbeing team handles it.
- **Take breaks.** Tell your moderator if you need a week off. Someone will cover.
- **Your own exam comes first.** In the run-up to your Prelims, Mains or result, ask for cover. No guilt.
- **It's OK to feel heavy** after a hard conversation. Talk to your moderator. Tele-MANAS is for you too.
- **Warning signs in yourself:** dreading the pod, checking messages constantly, losing sleep over members, resenting the role. Say something early.
- **You can step down any time.** Just let us know, so we can find someone for your pod.

---

## 11. What never to do

These are hard lines. Breaking them means stepping down as pod lead.

- **Never sell anything** to your pod. Not courses, notes, test series, mentorship or anything else.
- **Never recommend paid coaching or products you're tied to.** If you have any link (you teach there, get a fee, get free access), you don't recommend it in your pod at all. Outside the pod, follow the disclosure rule.
- **Never share members' data.** Phone numbers, profiles, answers, mood posts, attempt numbers, anything. Not with coaching institutes, not with other groups, not with anyone.
- **Never screenshot members' posts** to share elsewhere without their consent.
- **Never handle distress alone, or promise to keep it secret** when safety is at risk.
- **Never make result promises** ("follow my plan and you'll clear").
- **Never give medical or legal advice** as if you were a professional.
- **Never use a member's story** (in a post, on social media, anywhere) without their written consent.
