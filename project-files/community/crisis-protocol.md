# Distress and crisis protocol

> **DRAFT for human review. Not approved. Do not use with members until the safety lead and a qualified mental-health professional have signed off.**

Version 0.1 · drafted 11 Oct 2026 · owner: safety lead **[name, team to set]**

## Read this first

We are a peer community. We are not a clinic, a hospital or a helpline.

Our pod leads and wellbeing moderators are volunteers or small-team staff. Some may have peer-support training. None of them are acting as counsellors, doctors or therapists when they work in UPSC Pods, even if they have that training elsewhere. Our job is simple and serious: **notice, reply warmly as a human, connect the person to professional help, and never leave them alone in a public thread.**

This protocol covers what to do when a member seems to be struggling, from a low week to immediate danger. It is written for wellbeing moderators, the safety lead and on-call staff. Pod leads have a shorter version in `pod-lead-playbook.md`. The short version for pod leads is: **spot it, stay kind, hand it to a moderator. Never handle it alone.**

The protocol has nine parts:

1. Recognise
2. Respond
3. Connect
4. Escalate
5. Follow up
6. Records and privacy
7. Review
8. What Chotu and automated checks may and may not do
9. Result-day playbook

Then moderator self-care, and the review block.

## 1. Recognise

### 1.1 Three tiers

| Tier | What it looks like | Who acts | Target first human reply |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Immediate danger** | Talk of ending their life with a plan, a time, a method or a farewell. A message that sounds like goodbye. Saying they have pills, a rope or similar ready. | On-call wellbeing moderator, with the safety lead told at once | **[T1 minutes, team to set]** |
| **2. High concern** | Hopelessness ("what's the point of living"), feeling like a burden, "everyone would be better off without me", giving things away, sudden withdrawal after a result with a hopeless last post | Wellbeing moderator on shift | **[T2 minutes, team to set]** |
| **3. Low mood** | "Mann nahi lag raha", can't sleep, lonely, tired of everything, a run of low moods, going quiet | Pod lead, with a private check-in | Within 24 hours |

When unsure between two tiers, choose the higher one. Any tier 2 or 3 sign together with a farewell, a method, a time ("tonight", "aaj raat", "ee rathri") or giving things away counts as tier 1.

### 1.2 Warning signs to know

Direct statements are the clearest. Many people do not say it directly. Watch for these too.

**Indirect and coded phrasing**

- Farewell messages that do not fit the chat: "Thank you all for everything", "Sabko alvida", "Andariki bye, ika kanipinchanu".
- Apologies to family: "Maaf karna mummy papa", "Amma naanna, nannu kshaminchandi".
- "Sab khatam ho gaya" (Hindi) and "Antha aipoyindi" (Telugu). After a result these often mean "this attempt is over". Sometimes they mean much more. **Always a human read.**
- Burden: "Main gharwalon par bojh hoon", "Nenu andariki bhaaram", "I'm a burden on my parents".
- Shame: "Gharwalon ko munh nahi dikha sakta", "Intlo vaallaki moham choopinchalenu".
- Internet euphemisms: "unalive", "kms". Often jokes. A human still reads them.

**Behaviour**

- Giving away books, notes or test-series logins with "ab zaroorat nahi" or "ika avasaram ledu".
- Sudden silence after result day from someone who posted most days.
- Leaving the pod or deleting posts soon after a result.
- Posting between 2 and 5 a.m. several nights running with hopeless words.
- A run of low moods on Close the Day. (This is private by default. Only the person the member chose hears about it.)
- A sudden calm or cheerful goodbye after weeks of distress. This can be a warning sign, not relief.

**Context that raises concern**

- Result days, especially Prelims and final results.
- Last attempt or age limit reached.
- Family pressure, money running out, a breakup, a death in the family.
- Living alone in a PG or hostel in a hub city, far from home.

The full phrase lists, with native script, romanised forms, misspellings and false positives, are in `distress-phrases-hi.md`, `distress-phrases-te.md` and `distress-phrases.json`.

### 1.3 Things that look alarming but usually are not

- "Mar gaya, paper bahut tough tha." "CSAT ne maar dala." "Chachipoyaa, paper chaala tough."
- "Let's kill the syllabus." "Syllabus ni champeddam."
- "Syllabus khatam kar diya." (Good news.)
- GS discussions of farmer suicides, Kota news, capital punishment, GS4 case studies.

Slang lowers priority. It never closes a case by itself. A member can move from a GS topic to their own life in the next line. Read the whole post.

## 2. Respond

### 2.1 How to reply

- **Be quick and be human.** A short warm reply now beats a perfect one in an hour.
- **Use your own name.** "It's Neha from the wellbeing team."
- **Acknowledge the pain without judging it.** You do not need to fix anything.
- **Move it private.** Never leave a member who may be in danger in a public thread. Post one short line in public ("I've messaged you") and continue in a private message.
- **Ask directly when you are worried.** Many mental-health guides advise asking plainly about suicide, because it does not put the idea in someone's head and it gives them permission to talk. *(A mental-health professional must confirm this guidance and train moderators in it before use.)*
- **Listen more than you type.** Short replies. Let them lead.
- **Bring in help.** Helplines, a trusted person nearby, and a second moderator.

### 2.2 What not to say

| Don't say | Why |
| --- | --- |
| "Stay strong." "Be positive." | Toxic positivity. It tells them their pain is a failure of attitude. |
| "Others have it worse." | Minimises. It ends the conversation. |
| "It's just an exam." | To them, today, it is not. |
| "Think of your parents." | Adds guilt and burden, which are often the problem. |
| "Don't do anything stupid." | Judges and shames. |
| "I know exactly how you feel." | You don't. Say "I can hear how hard this is." |
| "You'll clear it next time, for sure." | A promise you cannot keep. |
| "I promise I won't tell anyone." | You cannot promise secrecy when safety is at risk. |
| Any diagnosis ("sounds like depression") | Volunteers do not diagnose. |
| Debating whether their reasons are good | Do not argue. Do not investigate. |
| Topper stories, motivational quotes, course links | Wrong moment. Never in a distress thread. |

Do not bring up religion or spirituality unless they do. If they do, follow their lead respectfully.

### 2.3 Sample replies

Use your own words where you can. These are starting points. The Hindi uses a standard, warm register with "aap".

Masculine and feminine forms are shown as "raha/rahi". Use the one that fits you.

**Opening reply**

> Thank you for telling us. It takes courage to write that. I'm here and I'm reading. Do you want to tell me what's happening right now?

> हमें बताने के लिए शुक्रिया। ऐसा लिखना हिम्मत की बात है। मैं यहाँ हूँ और आपकी बात पढ़ रहा/रही हूँ। क्या आप बताना चाहेंगे कि अभी क्या चल रहा है?

**Moving to private (public thread, one line)**

> I've sent you a message. I'm here.

> मैंने आपको मैसेज भेजा है। मैं यहीं हूँ।

**Moving to private (first private message)**

> Hi, it's [name] from the wellbeing team. I wanted to talk with you here so it's just us. How are you doing right now?

> नमस्ते, मैं [नाम], वेलबीइंग टीम से। मैं आपसे यहाँ अलग से बात करना चाहूँगा/चाहूँगी, ताकि बस हम दोनों हों। अभी आप कैसा महसूस कर रहे/रही हैं?

**Asking directly (wellbeing moderators only, after training)**

> Sometimes when people feel this low, they think about ending their life. Are you thinking about that?

> कभी-कभी जब कोई इतना टूटा हुआ महसूस करता है, तो उसके मन में अपनी जान लेने का ख़याल आता है। क्या आपके मन में ऐसा ख़याल आ रहा है?

**If they say yes, or you think they are in danger now**

> Thank you for being honest with me. Your safety matters most right now. Please call 112, or Tele-MANAS on 14416, right now. Is there someone near you, at home or in your PG, who can come and sit with you?

> मुझसे सच बताने के लिए शुक्रिया। अभी सबसे ज़रूरी आपकी सुरक्षा है। कृपया अभी 112 पर, या टेली-मानस को 14416 पर कॉल कीजिए। क्या आपके पास कोई है, घर में या पीजी में, जो आकर आपके साथ बैठ सके?

**Encouraging a call**

> Talking to someone trained can help a lot. Tele-MANAS is free. Would you like to call them now while I stay here with you in this chat?

> किसी प्रशिक्षित व्यक्ति से बात करने से बहुत मदद मिल सकती है। टेली-मानस मुफ़्त है। क्या आप अभी उन्हें कॉल करना चाहेंगे/चाहेंगी? मैं इस चैट में आपके साथ रहूँगा/रहूँगी।

**When you can't keep it secret**

> I care about you, so I can't keep this just between us. I'm going to bring in [name] from our wellbeing team, so you have more support. You're not in trouble.

> मुझे आपकी फ़िक्र है, इसलिए मैं यह बात सिर्फ़ अपने तक नहीं रख सकता/सकती। मैं हमारी वेलबीइंग टीम से [नाम] को जोड़ रहा/रही हूँ, ताकि आपको और सहारा मिले। आपने कुछ ग़लत नहीं किया है।

**After a bad result**

> Today's result is about one paper on one day. It is not a verdict on you. You don't need to decide anything tonight. Can we talk again tomorrow?

> आज का रिज़ल्ट एक दिन के एक पेपर के बारे में है। यह आपके बारे में कोई फ़ैसला नहीं है। आज रात आपको कुछ भी तय करने की ज़रूरत नहीं है। क्या हम कल फिर बात कर सकते हैं?

**Low mood check-in (pod lead)**

> Hey, I noticed you've been quiet this week. No pressure at all. Just wanted to say I'm around if you want to talk, about UPSC or anything else.

> नमस्ते, इस हफ़्ते आप थोड़े चुप-चुप लगे। कोई दबाव नहीं है। बस इतना कहना था कि अगर आप बात करना चाहें, UPSC के बारे में या किसी और चीज़ के बारे में, तो मैं यहाँ हूँ।

**Follow-up (24 to 48 hours later)**

> Checking in, like I said I would. How are you today? No need for a long reply.

> जैसा कहा था, आपका हाल पूछने आया/आई हूँ। आज आप कैसा महसूस कर रहे/रही हैं? लंबा जवाब देने की ज़रूरत नहीं है।

**Closing a conversation safely**

> I'm glad you talked to me. If it gets heavy again, the helpline numbers are pinned in the app, and you can message the wellbeing team any time. I'll check in on [day].

> मुझे अच्छा लगा कि आपने मुझसे बात की। अगर फिर से भारी लगे, तो हेल्पलाइन नंबर ऐप में पिन किए हुए हैं, और आप कभी भी वेलबीइंग टीम को मैसेज कर सकते/सकती हैं। मैं [दिन] को आपका हाल पूछूँगा/पूछूँगी।

Telugu sample replies are not included. A native Telugu writer with peer-support training must write them. Do not machine-translate these.

## 3. Connect

Every number below must be **verified before publishing**. Test-call each one, note the date, the hours and the languages offered, and record who checked it. Helplines change.

| Service | Number | What it is | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency (police, ambulance, fire) | **112** | National emergency number | **Verify before publishing** |
| Tele-MANAS | **14416** | National tele-mental-health service, free | **Verify before publishing** (hours, languages) |
| Tele-MANAS | **1-800-891-4416** | Same service, toll-free number | **Verify before publishing** |
| Regional services for Telangana and Andhra Pradesh | **[to add after verification]** | | Not yet checked |
| Campus or city services in Delhi hubs | **[to add after verification]** | | Not yet checked |

Do not add a number from memory or a blog. Only add a number after someone on the team has called it and recorded the result.

### 3.2 Where helplines appear

- Pinned in every pod and in the Chotu tab.
- Shown first, before anything else, when the distress check fires.
- In the overflow menu on every screen ("Need to talk to someone now?").
- In result-day mode, on the home screen.
- Free forever. Distress support and helpline visibility are on the never-monetise list.

### 3.3 A trusted person nearby

A phone line is good. A person in the room is often better. Ask:

- "Is there someone at home, in your PG or hostel, or a friend nearby who can be with you?"
- "Would it help if you messaged them now? I'll wait."

Respect their answer. Some members cannot tell family. Do not push them to.

## 4. Escalate

### 4.1 The ladder

| Step | Who | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pod lead | Spots it, replies kindly, hands to a moderator | Any sign of tier 1 or 2. Tier 3 they can check in on, and hand over if it continues past a week or gets worse. |
| Wellbeing moderator on shift | Takes the case, replies, moves to private, shares helplines | Every tier 1 and 2 case |
| Second moderator | Joins any tier 1 case so no one handles it alone | Tier 1, always |
| Safety lead | Told about every tier 1 case at once, by phone if after hours | Tier 1, and any case where the moderator is unsure |
| Emergency services (112) | Called by the member, or by the team as set out in 4.4 | Immediate danger |

### 4.2 Who is on call

| Slot | Who | Backup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekday daytime | **[name]** | **[name]** |
| Weekday evening | **[name]** | **[name]** |
| Night (11 p.m. to 7 a.m.) | **[name, or "no human cover", stated honestly]** | **[name]** |
| Weekends | **[name]** | **[name]** |
| Result-day surge | **[names]** | **[names]** |

Publish real moderator response hours in the app. **Do not publish a time-to-reply promise the roster cannot keep.** A two-person team cannot cover every night. If there is no night cover, say so in the app and show helplines.

Targets the team must set before launch:

- **T1**, first human reply to tier 1: **[__ minutes]**
- **T2**, first human reply to tier 2: **[__ minutes]**
- **T3**, pod lead check-in on tier 3: **24 hours** (suggested)

Measure time-to-first-human-reply on every flagged post. It is the main safety metric.

### 4.3 When no human is online

If a flag fires and no moderator is on shift, the app must be honest. It shows something like:

> Our moderators are not online right now. If you are in danger, please call 112. Tele-MANAS is free on 14416. We will read your message when we're back at [time].

It must not say "someone will reply soon" if no one will. It must not let Chotu fill the gap as if it were a person.

### 4.4 Immediate danger

If a member says they are about to act, or have already acted:

1. **Reply at once** with the "danger now" message in 2.3. Urge 112 and Tele-MANAS.
2. **Bring in a second moderator and call the safety lead.** Do not handle it alone.
3. **Ask if someone can be with them.** Ask where they are only if it helps get them help.
4. **Stay in the chat** while they call, if they want you to.
5. **If they cannot or will not call, and you believe their life is at risk**, the safety lead decides whether the team calls 112 with what we know. We usually do not have an address. Do not guess. **[Lawyer to confirm how the DPDP Act's provision for medical emergencies that threaten life applies, what we may share, and with whom. Write that answer here before launch.]**
6. **Never travel to a member yourself**, and never ask a pod lead to.
7. **Write a short record** afterwards (section 6) and debrief within 24 hours.

### 4.5 2 a.m. in result season

This is the hardest hour. Plan for it, not around it.

- Staff a surge rota for result night and the two nights after. Two people minimum, in pairs, with the safety lead reachable by phone.
- If you cannot staff it, say so in the app. Show helplines on the home screen in result-day mode.
- Night shifts are short (**[__ hours, team to set]**) and end with a hand-over note.
- Chotu's night rules apply: no more study prompts after 11 p.m., the distress check runs first.

## 5. Follow up

- Check in **24 to 48 hours** after any tier 1 or tier 2 conversation. Use the follow-up message in 2.3.
- Ask once. If there is no reply, try again after another 48 hours, then leave the door open. Do not chase.
- The moderator who handled the case does the follow-up when they can, so the member hears a familiar name.
- With the member's agreement, tell their pod lead only what they need to know ("X is having a hard week, go gently"). Never the details.
- Close the case in the record only after a follow-up has happened or two attempts have gone unanswered.

## 6. Records and privacy

Distress information is some of the most sensitive data we will ever hold. Collect as little as possible, keep it for as short a time as possible, and let very few people see it. This section is written with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its rules in mind. A lawyer must check it.

### 6.1 What we write down

A case note has only these fields:

| Field | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Case ID | Random, not the member's name |
| Member ID | Internal ID only |
| Tier | 1, 2 or 3 |
| Time flagged and how | "Pre-filter, pod post" or "Pod lead handover" |
| Time of first human reply | 23:14 |
| Actions taken | Helplines shared; moved to private; second moderator joined; 112 suggested |
| Follow-up | Date and done or not |
| Outcome | One of: "Connected to help", "Feeling safer", "No reply", "Emergency services involved", "Other" |
| Moderator | Who handled it |

### 6.2 What we do not write down

- No copy of the member's messages, beyond a one-line summary if the safety lead needs it.
- No diagnosis, guesses about their mental health, or opinions about them.
- No details of methods.
- No information about third parties (family, partners) beyond what safety needs.

### 6.3 Who can see records

- Wellbeing moderators: cases they handled, and open cases during their shift.
- The safety lead: all cases.
- No one else. Not pod leads, not subject mentors, not marketing, not analytics.
- Every view is logged in the admin console audit log.

### 6.4 Keeping and deleting

- Keep case notes for **[__ days, lawyer to set]**, then delete.
- Aggregate numbers only (count of cases, median time-to-first-reply) go into monthly reports. No names, no IDs.
- A member can ask what we hold and ask us to delete it, through the DPDP request flow in the admin console. **[Lawyer to confirm any exceptions.]**
- Case data never goes to an AI model in the cloud, never trains a model without explicit, separate consent, and is never used for marketing.

### 6.5 Where the data lives

The current design says mood, journal, distress and pod chat never leave the phone. This protocol assumes:

- **Private Chotu chats** stay on the phone. The distress check runs on the phone. Nothing is sent to a moderator unless the member taps "Talk to a person".
- **Pod posts** are already seen by the pod. A flag on a pod post sends the post's ID and tier to the safety queue so a moderator can read it.

## 7. Review

Once a month, the safety lead runs a short review with the wellbeing moderators and, when possible, the mental-health professional who advises us.

Look at:

- Number of flagged posts by tier.
- Time-to-first-human-reply: median and worst case, against T1 and T2.
- Any case where a reply was late, a step was missed, or a member was left in a public thread.
- False positives and misses. New phrases to add to the lists (without member names).
- Roster gaps, especially nights and result days.
- How moderators are doing (section 10).
- Whether helpline numbers were re-verified this month.

Write down what changes. Update this protocol, the phrase lists and the training. Any tier 1 case gets its own review within 7 days, as well as the monthly one. The aim is learning, not blame.

## 8. What Chotu and automated checks may and may not do

Chotu is the on-device AI helper, labelled "Chotu · AI". The automated distress check runs on the phone. Neither is a person and neither may act like one.

**The automated distress check may:**

- Match a post or message against the phrase lists and, later, a classifier.
- Route a pod post to the safety queue for a human.
- Show the helpline sheet first, before anything else.
- Offer a "Talk to a person" button.
- Offer a calm tool second ("While you wait, a slow breath can help some people").
- Pause upsells, quote cards and celebrations for that member while a flag is open.

**The automated distress check may not:**

- Counsel, advise or reassure as if it understands.
- Reply as if it were a human, or sign with a human name.
- Block, hide, delete or shadow-ban the post.
- Tell the pod that the post was flagged.
- Send private chat text to anyone without the member's tap.
- Make any decision about calling emergency services.

**Chotu may:**

- Say clearly that it is an AI and that it is not the right help for this.
- Show the helplines and the "Talk to a person" button.
- Stay calm and short, and stop its normal chat until the member chooses to go on.

**Chotu may not:**

- Try to talk the member through a crisis.
- Ask risk-assessment questions ("do you have a plan?"). That is for trained humans.
- Diagnose, give medical advice or suggest medicines.
- Say "someone is coming" unless a human has actually picked up the case.
- Use quotes, jokes, motivational lines or study prompts in a distress flow.
- Promise to keep a secret.

A suggested Chotu message when the check fires:

> I'm an AI, so I'm not the right help for this, but I don't want you to be alone with it. If you are in danger, please call 112. Tele-MANAS is free on 14416. You can also talk to a person from our team.
> [Talk to a person] [See helplines]

> मैं एक AI हूँ, इसलिए इसमें सही मदद नहीं कर सकता, पर मैं नहीं चाहता कि आप इसके साथ अकेले रहें। अगर आप ख़तरे में हैं, तो कृपया 112 पर कॉल कीजिए। टेली-मानस 14416 पर मुफ़्त है। आप हमारी टीम के किसी व्यक्ति से भी बात कर सकते/सकती हैं।
> [किसी व्यक्ति से बात करें] [हेल्पलाइन देखें]

## 9. Result-day playbook

Results are the most predictable high-risk days of the year. Check the dates on upsc.gov.in and put them in the calendar as soon as UPSC publishes them.

### Before (one week out)

- Confirm the surge rota for result day and the two nights after.
- Re-verify every helpline number.
- Brief pod leads: re-read the result-day section of `pod-lead-playbook.md`.
- Turn on result-day mode in the app for the day. Helplines on the home screen.
- Check that the 72-hour no-upsell window is set in feature flags. No upsells, sales or sprint offers to anyone for 72 hours after a result. No countdowns.

### On the day

- Pod leads post a calm waiting-room message in the morning. No predictions, no "cut-off" speculation threads.
- Celebrations are welcome, but in their own opt-in space, not in mixed pods. No rank or roll-number bragging in pods.
- Moderators watch for "sab khatam", "antha aipoyindi", farewell posts, and members who go silent.
- Pod leads send a private message to every member of their pod by the evening, whatever the result.

### The night after

- Pairs on shift. Safety lead on the phone.
- Chotu's night rules in force. Lamp Off is the only prompt.

### The next morning

- A "fresh page" post from the pod lead. No plans, no pressure.
- Moderators check on everyone flagged overnight.

### Days 2 to 7

- Pod leads check on anyone who has gone quiet.
- Repeater panels and Plan B resources are offered gently, not pushed.
- No one is asked to "decide about next attempt" this week.

## 10. Moderator self-care

This work is heavy. Helping someone through a frightening night can stay with you. That is normal. It is not a weakness.

- **Never alone on tier 1.** Always a second moderator, always the safety lead told.
- **Short shifts.** Night shifts are short and no one does two in a row.
- **Debrief within 24 hours** after any tier 1 case, with the safety lead or the advising professional. Talk about how you are, not just what happened.
- **You can step back.** Any moderator can say "I can't take this one" or "I need a break this month", with no explanation needed.
- **You are not responsible for the outcome.** You did your part by noticing, replying and connecting. What a person does next is not on you.
- **Access to support.** **[Team to arrange: access to a qualified professional for moderators, and how to book it.]**
- **Tele-MANAS is for you too.** Moderators can call it.
- **Watch for signs in yourself:** dreading shifts, trouble sleeping, thinking about cases all the time, feeling numb. Tell the safety lead.
