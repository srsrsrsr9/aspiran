SAMPLE · AN ANSWER WE WROTE, MARKED THE WAY YOURS WILL BE

This is what you get back, in minutes

UPSC CSE Mains 2025 · GS2 · 10 marks, 150 words

e-governance projects have a built-in bias towards technology and back-end integration than user-centric designs. Examine.

Read the answer (126 words)

E-governance means using ICT to deliver government services. India has launched many projects like Digital India, DigiLocker, UMANG and CoWIN. These projects have improved efficiency. Aadhaar-based DBT has reduced leakages… However, the focus is often on technology. Many portals are only in English and are hard to use on cheap phones. Biometric failures in ration shops have denied people their rations… In conclusion, e-governance should be citizen-centric for good governance.

At the level of a typical well-scoring answer on all five. To go above it: sharper analysis, sub-headings, one piece of data.

  1. What the question askedAt the level

    Mentions the bias but doesn't fully examine why the tech-first approach exists or weigh its pros and cons.

    Fix: Say why technology gets the focus (cost, scale) and weigh both sides before you conclude.

  2. StructureAt the level

    A basic intro, body and conclusion, but no sub-headings or points.

    Fix: Add sub-headings: "Technology first", "Gaps for users", "What to change".

  3. ContentAt the level

    Covers language, phones and literacy, but leaves out back-end integration, data security and institutional limits.

    Fix: Add a point each on back-end interoperability, data privacy and the capacity of departments.

  4. ExamplesAt the level

    Names big schemes and failures, but no data or official report.

    Fix: Quote one figure or an official assessment, such as the e-Governance Service Delivery Assessment.

  5. ConclusionAt the level

    A generic "citizen-centric" line, with no summing-up or next steps.

    Fix: Sum up what you found and propose concrete steps: user testing, interfaces in many languages.

Then you rewrite the weakest part, and the rewrite is marked too. Free: 3 answers a week. Plus: as many as you write.

Questions people ask

I already go to coaching, or watch YouTube. Why this too?

Coaching teaches it once. Aspiran makes it stay: a 10 to 20 minute review a day of what you have already studied, and answer writing that gets checked in minutes. It works alongside coaching, not instead of it.

How are my answers marked?

By you first, part by part. Then Aspiran's marker compares it with a typical well-scoring UPSC answer and tells you, on five parts, whether you are below, at or above it, with one fix for each. It can be wrong, which is why you mark your own first.

Is this a full course?

No. You learn from your own books or coaching (Laxmikanth, the NCERTs, Spectrum). Aspiran makes sure what you learn stays, works in an answer, and that you are not doing it alone.

Is it for Prelims or Mains?

Both. Prelims: the daily review, real past questions, "how sure are you?" so negative marking stops surprising you, and mocks. Mains: answers on paper, marked by you first, then by Aspiran, then a rewrite.

Is it really in Hindi?

The app, notes, practice and answer marking are in Hindi. The videos are in English for now.

How do I pay?

Open Plus in the app: UPI, card or net banking. One payment, no auto-renewal, refund within 7 days.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. The web app runs in the browser on any phone or laptop. The Android app is in testing on Google Play.
