What your seven samples share

I could see the thumbnails and three frames from each video; YouTube blocks full playback from where I work, so the NDTV moment at 6:50 I could not watch. The motion-design six have the same grammar:

  • One idea per beatEach shot says one thing, then moves on in 3 to 6 seconds.
  • Flat, minimal shapesFew colours, lots of empty space, simple icons (Asana, Francis J, SOGA).
  • The camera movesPush-ins, pans and pull-backs over a still world, so nothing feels static.
  • Match cutsA shape from one shot becomes the next shot (the Vox short is about exactly this).
  • Eased motionThings overshoot and settle; nothing moves at constant speed.
  • Sound on every hitWhoosh on cuts, tick on counts, stamp on reveals, over a quiet bed.

The Claude motion-graphics short is the most relevant one: it was made from prompts and code alone. That is the approach below.

Recommendation: a pipeline with a shot library, not hand-made templates

Writers fill a short story file. Code turns it into video. Motion quality lives in a library of reusable shots, so it improves for every story at once.

1 · Story file

Beats and lines

Each beat names a shot, the voice line and its data. Our Hidden Why packs convert almost one to one.

2 · Voice

Audio plus word times

Each line is voiced and every word gets a timestamp. Motion and captions are cued to spoken words.

3 · Motion engine

HTML that can seek

Every frame is a pure function of time, so the same code previews live in a browser and renders exactly.

4 · Render

MP4 with sound mix

Headless Chrome captures 30 fps; ffmpeg adds voice, ducked music, effects and loudness to −16 LUFS.

Rendering takes about 90 seconds per video on this machine, with no paid tools. The same files work as YouTube Shorts, Reels and in-app video.

Why not the other routes

After Effects templates

The best look, but every video needs an editor's hours. That doesn't scale to a story a day in two languages.

Remotion (React to video)

Same idea as ours and a good upgrade path, but it needs a paid company licence for teams above three people.

Lottie in the app

Good for small looping icons inside screens. It can't carry voice, captions and a 40-second edit on its own.
