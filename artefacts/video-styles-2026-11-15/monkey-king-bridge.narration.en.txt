Eighty thousand monkeys. One way out.

A Buddhist Jataka tells of a monkey king, and his troop in a great mango tree by the Ganges.

He had one rule: let no mango fall into the river.

One fruit, hidden by an ant nest, fell anyway.

It drifted down to the king of Benares. He tasted it, and wanted the tree.

At night, his archers surrounded it.

The monkey king leapt the river, tied a bamboo cane to his waist, and leapt back.

The cane fell short. So he held on, and made his own body the bridge.

The whole troop crossed over him. One, who bore him ill will, jumped hard, and wounded him.

The king had him lowered gently, and asked why a king would suffer for his subjects.

"Their safety was my duty," he said. "A ruler's first care is the people he leads."

The monkey king died. The king honoured him, and ruled by that lesson.

For G-S-four: this is leadership as service. For G-S-one: the story is carved at Bharhut and Sanchi, over two thousand years ago.

Remember the bridge.
