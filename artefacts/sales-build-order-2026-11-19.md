# What to build, in what order, to sell (19 Nov 2026)

Replaces the ranking in `volume-plan-2026-11-19.md`. That file is still the reference for *how* to make volume
when we get to it.

## The honest answer first: does volume help us sell?

**Only a little, and only in two ways.**

1. **It removes an objection.** A buyer comparing us with a library pass asks "do they have enough?" We need
   *enough* (we already have 2,000+ questions, 250+ videos, 150 reads, 20 hours of audio), not *the most*.
2. **Free, public volume brings people in.** Past-paper pages people search for, daily cards on Telegram, shorts on
   YouTube, a workbook on Amazon. Volume locked behind the paywall brings nobody.

**What actually blocks sales today:** too few people find us; those who do don't *feel* the method work before
we ask for money (spaced recall pays off on day 3, and the demo ends on day 0); and there are no faces or proof.
The last counts we have (11 Nov) were 24 accounts and 0 payments. A bigger library fixes none of this.

**Free ChatGPT, Gemini, Kimi and Claude change the picture.** They write notes, make quizzes, explain any topic
and mark a pasted answer, in Hindi too, for free. So generic notes, quizzes, explanations and one-off answer
marking are **not a reason to pay**, from us *or* from McGraw Hill. Their library pass loses to free AI too.

What those chatbots don't do well, and what we should sell:

| A free chatbot… | Aspiran… |
|---|---|
| Gets facts and answer keys wrong with confidence, and gives no source | Uses UPSC's official keys and primary sources, shown on every piece |
| Waits to be asked | Turns up every day by itself: three Chai cards, the questions due today |
| Forgets what you studied in October (memory features help only a little) | Knows all 297 syllabus lines, what you've covered, and brings back what you're about to forget |
| Marks one answer, and marks it differently next time | Marks against one UPSC rubric, keeps every answer, and tracks your rewrites and repeated mistakes |
| Is you alone with a chat box | Gives you a small pod, and named people at live sessions |
| Exists only on a screen | Puts a workbook on your desk |

**Rule for everything below:** build what a free chatbot can't do, and make it visible *before* the paywall.

## Build order

Each step lists what it is, **where** it lives, and who does it. One step at a time; the next starts when
the last is live.

### 1. The day-3 payoff (demo → email → "you kept 4 of 5")
- **What:** the free topic ends with "We'll ask you 5 of these again on Thursday. Where should we send them?"
  On Thursday, an email with 5 questions; the visitor answers in the browser and privately sees "You kept 4 of 5
  after 3 days". That screen asks for sign-up; a later one asks for Plus.
- **Where:** the open demo, email (Resend, already set up), the web app.
- **Why first:** it's the only way a visitor *feels* the thing a chatbot never does: come back to you. It turns
  our main claim from words into an experience. Not built yet.
- **Who:** Claude.

### 2. Every UPSC past paper, free and public, mapped to the syllabus
- **What:** Prelims GS since 1995 and CSAT since 2011, then Mains GS and Essay since 2013. Each question gets
  its own page with UPSC's official key (or a clearly marked "Aspiran key" where UPSC published none), why each
  option is right or wrong, the trap, and which syllabus line it tests. Plus "how often UPSC asks this line".
- **Where:** public pages under `app.aspiran.in/learn` for Google; "PYQs by topic" in the app; the garden
  shows how often each leaf has been asked.
- **Why:** "UPSC previous year questions" is what aspirants search for most, and it's where chatbots go wrong
  most visibly (they invent keys). It's the one kind of volume that brings people in *and* proves accuracy.
  Optional papers can wait.
- **Who:** Claude (UPSC's papers are free to use).

### 3. Daily presence where aspirants already are
- **What:** the three Chai cards posted automatically each morning to a free **Telegram channel** and
  **WhatsApp channel**, each ending with "test yourself: one tap". A **PYQ of the day** in the same post. The 254
  videos as YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels, one a day.
- **Where:** Telegram, WhatsApp, YouTube, Instagram.
- **Why:** a chatbot never turns up uninvited; a daily post does. This is our top of funnel, and nothing else
  costs as little per new person.
- **Who:** Claude builds the Telegram auto-post. You create the channels and accounts, and sign in for
  YouTube and Instagram uploads.

### 4. Plus = the things a chatbot can't keep
- **What:** rework what Plus offers so it's **memory, not more marking**:
  - every marked answer kept, with rewrites side by side;
  - "your three repeated mistakes" across all your answers;
  - a mistake log from every mock, fed back into review;
  - the full syllabus map with what's covered and what's slipping;
  - unlimited marking against the same rubric.
  The paywall appears at the three moments of highest value (third answer in a week, after a mock, day 14).
- **Where:** web app and Android; the website's Plus section.
- **Why:** "unlimited marking" alone loses to free ChatGPT. A year of *your* history in one place doesn't.
- **Who:** Claude.

### 5. Faces and proof
- **What:** your photo and "why I built this" on the home page; one real marked answer (original, marking,
  rewrite); a monthly live hour with a named guest (a retired officer, a recent ranker, a partner centre);
  with permission, the first members' own before-and-after (recall kept, rewrites made; no ranks).
- **Where:** aspiran.in, the app (live hour and recordings), YouTube.
- **Why:** every competitor sells a face; we sell none yet. A chatbot can't be a person either.
- **Who:** you (photo, words, guests, hosting). Claude builds the live-hour page and recordings shelf.

### 6. One coaching-centre pilot, with first-pass answer checking
- **What:** the white-label console (built, off) plus first-pass checking of answer copies, which the teacher
  confirms or edits. One small centre or self-study library, 30 days free; then a one-page case study.
- **Where:** `app.aspiran.in/institute`; in person in one city.
- **Why:** one centre is dozens of students at once, and it's a sale a chatbot can't make: a centre needs a
  system with their logo, batches and records, not a chat box.
- **Who:** you find the centre and decide the 11 open points in `coaching-centres-plan-2026-11-14.md`; Claude
  builds the checking.

### 7. Pods that start full
- **What:** pods open on the 1st and 15th of each month, starting only with at least 4 people. Until then the
  app is fully open, and the site says "your pod starts on the 1st".
- **Where:** web app, Android, website wording.
- **Why:** pods are the one thing no one else has, and right now an empty pod breaks the promise on day one.
- **Who:** Claude.

### 8. Workbooks: the Syllabus Companion first, then Polity
- **What:** the thin whole-syllabus workbook (no notes, only structure: syllabus map, PYQ tracker, mistake
  log, news log, revision calendar), then the Polity workbook. Each sold printed on Amazon, with a free chapter
  PDF and QR codes that open the matching screen in the app.
- **Where:** Amazon.in and Flipkart (print on demand), a free PDF on aspiran.in, the chapter screens in the app.
- **Why:** a book on a library desk in Mukherjee Nagar is advertising a chatbot can't buy, and a pirated PDF
  still carries our name. The Companion first because it's fast and useful to everyone whatever books they use.
- **Who:** you approve the content and choose the print partner; Claude builds the pages, the PDFs and the
  QR screens.

### 9. Volume packaging, only to fill the comparison table
- **What:** quizzes per syllabus line, one course per subject, a monthly Chai digest as an eBook, and a public
  "Library" counter.
- **Where:** app and website.
- **Why last:** it answers "do they have enough?" once people are already comparing us, and it costs little
  because it reuses what we have.
- **Who:** Claude.

## Not now

- Racing to 2,000 videos or 25 eBooks just to match a number.
- Optional-subject papers (step 2 covers what most buyers search for).
- An iOS app (worth it once Android sells).
- Naming McGraw Hill or other brands on our site. If we publish a comparison, use generic columns ("a book
  library pass", "a free chatbot") and only claims we've checked.
