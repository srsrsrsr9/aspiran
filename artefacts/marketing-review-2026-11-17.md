# Aspiran against the market: what's weak, what's good, and how to convert (17 Nov 2026)

Owner's ask: the website and the free app feel less impactful than they should. Compare with what prospects compare us
with (coaching centres, online players, AI players), then say what it takes to turn aspirants into paying customers and
coaching centres into partners.

**How this was done.** I walked aspiran.in and the Federalism demo live today as a visitor, and re-read the 11 Nov full
review, the 15 Nov demo review, the 22 Oct buyer review and the coaching-centres plan. I checked competitor prices on the
web (sources at the end; fees change by batch, so treat them as ranges). I couldn't read today's live member and payment
counts (the database read was blocked here); the last numbers I have are from 11 Nov: 24 accounts, 6 people in pods,
0 payments.

---

## 1. The verdict in five lines

1. **The product is better than the page makes it feel.** The teaching is sound and honest, but the site sells a
   *method* (spaced recall), and aspirants buy *outcomes* and *people*.
2. **The price and the position don't match.** We say "use it with or without coaching" (a supplement), but ₹9,999 is
   a replacement price. At ₹9,999 the prospect puts us next to PW OnlyIAS's whole online foundation course at about
   the same price, with live teachers.
3. **The "aha" is invisible.** Spaced recall pays off on day 3 and day 10. The demo ends on day 0, and nothing brings
   the visitor back on day 3. So the best part of the product is never felt before the ask for money.
4. **There are no humans and no proof.** Every competitor sells a face (faculty, a mentor, a topper). We sell AI voices,
   "written by our team" and, honestly, "we won't show you toppers". That is principled, but it leaves a trust gap
   that nothing else on the page fills yet.
5. **Pods, the thing nobody else has, can't be felt yet.** Six people are in pods, so a new member's pod is mostly
   empty or AI friends. The most distinctive promise is the one that disappoints first.

than replacing them. The marketing and the price should say that plainly.

## 3. Website and free app: the flaws, most damaging first

1. **The hero sells a vitamin, not a painkiller.** "What you read in October is gone by March" is true and clever, but
   forgetting is felt in March, not when someone is shopping in November. The pains aspirants feel *now*: "nobody
   checks my Mains answers", "too much material, no plan", "I'm alone and losing it", "coaching costs ₹2 lakh",
   "Hindi medium gets second-class material". Lead with one of those (answer checking or Hindi), and keep forgetting
   as the *why it works*.
2. **No product on the page.** There's no screenshot, no 20-second clip of the app in use, no marked answer. The
   prospect has to imagine it. Every competitor shows the teacher or the app in the first screen.
3. **No people.** No founder, no editor, no named face. "Written by our team" reads as "nobody in particular". One face
   and one name (the owner, and each editor with a line of background) is the cheapest trust there is.
4. **The demo starts with a blank "Loading…" for about 5 to 6 seconds** (seen today on desktop wi-fi). On a phone on
   4G it will be longer. Many visitors leave in that gap.
5. **The demo feels like a quiz page.** The teaching is good, but on screen it is text, four options and a Next button,
   which looks like every mock test site. The moments that are unique (the Governor's desk, "how sure?", the cards you
   keep) need to look and feel different from a quiz.
6. **The payoff never arrives.** The demo ends with "you can now…" but never says "we'll ask you 3 of these again on
   Thursday" and never actually asks. Without that return visit, spaced recall is just a claim.
7. **Plus doesn't feel worth ₹9,999.** Plus is "unlimited AI marking, all mocks, essays, more Chotu". The free plan is
   already generous (pod, daily review, Chai, Chotu, 3 marked answers a week, a mock a month). And an aspirant can
   paste an answer into ChatGPT for free. There is nothing in Plus a person can't get elsewhere for less.
8. **Paying is hard.** "WhatsApp us to pay by UPI" is friction and feels informal for ₹999. Cashfree is still sandbox;
   Play Billing is off. An impulse to buy has nowhere to go.
9. **Where aspirants live, we aren't.** Their day runs on YouTube, Telegram and WhatsApp. Our 250 videos, the daily
   Chai and the PYQs are inside the app; the site is the only public face.
10. **Pods break their promise on day 1** (see the verdict). A new member should never find an empty pod.

## 4. What's genuinely good (protect these)

- **Honesty as a stance.** "Things you'll never see here" (no streaks, no fake countdowns, no number sold to a call
  centre) speaks to aspirants burnt out by pushy coaching sales. No competitor can say it with a straight face.
- **The method is real.** Recall before reading, spaced review across a syllabus map of 297 lines, "how sure?",
  mark-then-rewrite. This is more serious pedagogy than any app in this market.
- **Hindi parity.** Most of the library is in Hindi too. Hindi-medium aspirants are a large group poorly served by
  premium players.
- **Volume already there:** 2,000+ questions, 11 past papers on official keys, 250+ videos, 20+ hours of audio, 60
  interactives. That's a real library, not a promise.
- **A no-sign-up demo** that teaches one topic end to end. Few competitors let you judge them before giving a number.
- **The Preamble recall trick on the home page.** It's the only place a visitor *feels* the method. Do more of it.
- **Pods.** No one else gives a small, calm, non-competitive peer group. Once they're full, they're the moat.

## 5. What it takes to turn aspirants into paying customers

Be honest about the target: no edtech converts everyone. Free-to-paid in this market is usually a few per cent. The aim
is to make the free experience good enough to be talked about, and the paid step obvious at the right moment.

**1. Choose the position: "the layer that makes any prep stick".** Say it in the hero: works with your coaching, your
YouTube teacher or your books. Then compete with nobody's lectures, and complement all of them.

**2. Fix the price to match the position, or add what justifies ₹9,999.** Two honest routes; pick one:
- **Route A (recommended): price it as a supplement.** About ₹1,999 to ₹2,999 a year (or ₹299 a month), with the ₹999
  founding price kept for the first 300. That sits under SuperKalam and PW, and is an easy yes on top of any coaching.
- **Route B: keep ₹9,999 and add humans.** For example 2 answers a month marked by a named human evaluator, a monthly
  15-minute call with a mentor, and a cohort start date. That is what test series at this price sell.
Run the founding 300 at ₹999 as planned and measure who comes back after 30 days before setting the long-term price.

**3. Make the payoff happen inside the free experience.** The demo should end with: "On Thursday we'll ask you 5 of
these again. Where shall we send them?" (email now, WhatsApp later). On Thursday, the visitor answers 5 questions and
sees, privately, "You kept 4 of 5 after 3 days." That return is the moment to ask for sign-up and, later, money. This
needs no rule bent: it's private, it's you against you, nothing is ranked.

**4. Put real people and real proof on the page.**
- The owner's face and a short "why I built this"; each editor's name and background.
- One real marked answer: the original, the AI's second opinion, the rewrite.
- From the first 300: with their permission, their words and their own before-and-after (recall kept, rewrites made).
  No ranks, no "toppers". Proof that the method works for ordinary people is enough.

**5. Design the paywall moments, not a banner.** Ask at the three points of highest value:
after the third marked answer in a week ("want this every day?"), after a mock ("see your mistake types"), and on day
14 if they've come back 5+ times. Keep everything free that builds the habit (daily review, Chai, the pod).

**6. Make paying one tap.** Cashfree in production with UPI intent, and Play Billing in the app. Retire "WhatsApp us to
pay" as the main route.

**7. Fill pods before promising them.** Open pods by cohort: new pods start on the 1st and 15th of each month, and
people wait (with the full app open to them) until a pod of at least 4 can start. Or fill them through partners (§6).
Until then, the site should say "pods start on the 1st and 15th", not imply a pod is waiting.

**8. Go where aspirants already are.**
- **YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels:** the 250 syllabus videos, one a day, each ending in "test yourself on this:
  aspiran.in".
- **Telegram and WhatsApp channels:** the daily Chai, 3 cards, free, every day.
- **Search:** the `/learn` topic pages, one per syllabus line, with PYQs by topic.
- **Hindi belt on the ground:** Prayagraj, Patna, Jaipur, Indore, Lucknow, Mukherjee Nagar. A QR recall sheet on the
  desks of self-study libraries (see §6).

**9. Fix the first 10 seconds.** Show the demo's first screen within 1 to 2 seconds (load the topic before the whole
app, or show a static first question while the rest loads). Put a 20-second product clip on the home page.

## 6. What it takes to turn coaching centres into partners

**Who to target first.** Not Vajiram or Vision: they have their own apps and see us as competition. Aim at the
thousands of small and mid-sized centres in tier-2 and tier-3 cities and the Hindi belt, plus the **self-study
libraries** (paid reading rooms) where aspirants sit all day. They have students, no tech, thin margins and no app.

**What a centre owner wants, and what we offer for each:**

| They want | What we offer |
|---|---|
| More admissions | "Your own app" on day one: their logo and colour inside Aspiran, a modern look they can show prospects |
| Lower cost of checking answer copies (evaluators cost real money per copy) | AI first pass on every copy, the teacher confirms or edits. This is the offer that sells itself; it needs to be built on top of the console |
| Students who don't drop out | "Gone quiet" list, weak topics per batch, the daily review keeping students engaged between classes |
| Hindi material | The Hindi library, ready on day one |
| Not losing students to us | A written promise: we never sell to or advertise to their students, never show another centre, and the student's data in the institute stays theirs |

**What they fear, and how to answer it:** that we'll poach their students (contract clause plus no D2C ads inside
partner batches); that it replaces their faculty (it doesn't: it takes the checking load off them); cost (start free).

**Commercial model that lets almost any centre say yes:**
- **Partner (free):** console, branding, batches, chat, doubts, plans, analytics. Students get the free plan. If a
  student buys Plus, the centre gets a share (for example 30%). Costs the centre nothing; we grow through them.
- **Pro (paid per student):** Plus for every student, AI first-pass checking for teachers. The plan's ₹1,200 to ₹1,800
  per student a year fits here.
- **Own app listing:** quoted, only for large centres.

**How to sell it:**
1. Switch the `institutes` flag on for **one pilot centre** (and one library) and run 30 days free.
2. Measure what a centre owner cares about: copies checked, teacher hours saved, students active each week.
3. Turn it into a one-page case study, then a WhatsApp-able demo video and a one-page pitch in Hindi and English.
4. One person (the owner or a hire) visiting centres in two cities, with that case study.

The 11 open decisions in `coaching-centres-plan-2026-11-14.md` (pricing among them) need answers before the pilot.

## 7. If I had to pick the next five

1. **Decide the position and price route** (supplement at about ₹2,000 to ₹3,000, or ₹9,999 with humans). *Owner.*
2. **Day-3 return in the demo:** "we'll ask you again on Thursday" by email, then the private "you kept 4 of 5". *Claude, about 2 days.*
3. **Home page v4:** painkiller hero, a 20-second product clip, the owner's face and the editors, a real marked answer,
   fast demo start. *Claude, with the owner's photo and words.*
4. **One-tap payment:** Cashfree production (owner's keys), Play Billing on. *Owner, then Claude.*
