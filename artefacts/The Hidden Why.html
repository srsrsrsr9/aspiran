The Hidden Why

Short picture stories in the style of the Deepstash ads, with a Freakonomics twist. Each one starts with a claim that sounds wrong, explains it in a few captions, names the idea, and ends with a line an aspirant can say in an interview.

Tap the right side to go on, the left to go back.

All six are drafts. Facts marked “check” on the sources card wait for a subject editor, and every Hindi line waits for the Hindi editor.

What we took from Deepstash

  • One idea per story. The cobra ad never tries to teach economics. It tells one story and stops.
  • A hook that sounds wrong. “If a hospital is ranked on survival rates…” makes you need the next frame.
  • One caption per frame, over one etched drawing. The camera moves across a single sepia illustration, so a story needs one picture, not six.
  • The name comes last. “Economists call this the Cobra Effect” lands after you already understand it, which is why it sticks.

What we added for UPSC

Deepstash stops at the name. An aspirant needs to use it, and needs not to be caught out by it. So every story ends with three cards:

Say it smartA 40-word line for the interview or an answer opening, in the aspirant's own voice.
CarefulThe nuance a board member would raise. The Delhi cobra story in the ad has no archive record; Hanoi's rat bounty does.
If they ask nextThe likely follow-up question and how to handle it.
Use it inWhich paper and which kind of answer it fits, plus sources with check flags.

The Careful card is what makes someone sound smart rather than glib. Repeating the popular version of a story is easy. Knowing which version is documented, and where the evidence stops, is what a board notices.

The Freakonomics part

Freakonomics works because it asks why people do what they do, and answers with incentives and data. Four of the six samples follow that pattern with Indian evidence: Punjab's water law and stubble fires, the panchayat reservation that turned into a natural experiment, ASER's reading numbers, and topper talks as survivorship bias. Two are classics an interviewer may already know, the rat bounty and the day-care fine, told with the documented details.

What it would take to add it to the app

  1. A template, hidden-why. Schema, fill schema, brief and config, like the other 55. The six samples already use the pack shape (upsc-pods/hidden-why@1) and sit in content/hidden-why/.
  2. No new engine. It runs on the existing Step-through engine with one new look: a full-screen illustration that pans to a focus point per frame, with the caption pill on top. With motion off, each frame shows a still crop.
  3. One tall illustration per story (360×640) with focus points per frame. The preview uses our own SVG drawings in the app palette. For launch, I'd commission an illustrator for an etched house style; there are no web photos, as with the rest of the pack.
  4. Validator rules: captions of 16 words or fewer, the last frame names the idea, Careful and at least one source are required, and every number must appear in the source text.
  5. An “interview” tag. The interview is listed as “not covered on purpose” in exam-formats.json. This would be the first format aimed at it, so that file needs a new entry.
  6. People: a subject editor checks the flagged facts (the ASER figures, Punjab's free-power year, the Hanoi and Wald details), and the Hindi editor checks every Hindi line.
  7. Optional app features: a “My smart lines” list where the Say-it-smart lines are saved, and a share card for them. Neither is needed to ship the stories.

Haiku can fill this template from a source text, like the other types. The source just has to name the study or record each story stands on.
