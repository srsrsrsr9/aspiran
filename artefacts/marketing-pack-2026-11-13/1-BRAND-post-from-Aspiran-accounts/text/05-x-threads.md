## T1. The leak (image: Q1-october-march)
1/ What you read in October is gone by March. Not because you didn't study. Because nothing brought it back.
2/ Re-reading feels like learning. The page looks familiar, so you feel you know it. Familiar isn't the same as being able to pull it out in the exam hall.
3/ Try it. The Preamble has been amended once: 1976, the 42nd Amendment. Keep that in your head.
4/ What actually works is boring and well studied: close the book and say it back. Come back just before you'd forget. Then use it in an answer.
5/ Quick, without scrolling up: which year, and which amendment?
6/ That small effort is the whole method. We built Aspiran around it for UPSC, in English and Hindi. Do one topic free, no sign-up: [link]

## T2. Sure and wrong (image: Q5-sure)
1/ In UPSC Prelims a wrong answer costs a third of the marks. Three confident mistakes cancel one right answer.
2/ So "do I know this?" is the wrong question. The right one is "how sure am I, and am I usually right when I'm this sure?"
3/ Test: "The President can dissolve the Rajya Sabha." True or false? And how sure are you?
4/ False. It's a permanent House and is never dissolved. If you were sure and wrong, that's the kind of mistake worth fixing first.
5/ Aspiran asks "sure or not sure?" every time, and shows you your own record, privately. Your confident mistakes come back first. Try a topic: [link]

## T3. Mark it yourself (image: Q4-mark-it)
1/ Most answer-writing practice goes: write, get marks, feel bad, move on. The marks teach you very little.
2/ What teaches: marking your own answer first. Directive, structure, content, examples, conclusion. Five honest numbers.
3/ Then see a second opinion next to yours. The gap between the two is the skill. Over months you watch it shrink.
4/ Then rewrite only the weakest part. Five minutes. Feedback you don't act on is just reading.
5/ That loop is built into Aspiran, for English and Hindi-medium answers. The second opinion is an AI and says so. One topic, free: [link]
