Part 1

How to use this book

  1. This book holds no notes. Your main book teaches; this one helps you see where you stand and plan what comes next.
  2. Start with the syllabus map. Shade the 'start' circle on every line, honestly. It takes one sitting.
  3. Look at the past-question counts for each paper. Fill in the earlier years from UPSC's past papers as you go.
  4. Once a week, take one Mains planning page. Plan in five minutes, then write the answer in your notebook or in the app.
  5. Keep a news log for each paper. Clip one item a week per paper; a line or two is enough.
  6. Use the revision calendar to put your recall days on paper. Plan one month at a time.
  7. Halfway through, shade the 'middle' circles. In the last month, shade the third. Go back to the lines that stayed light.
  8. Nothing here is a score. The circles and logs are for you alone.
The exam in one box
  • Prelims: two objective papers of 200 marks each. Paper I (General Studies) decides who goes to Mains. Paper II (CSAT) is qualifying at 33%.
  • A wrong answer in Prelims costs one-third of that question's marks.
  • Mains: Paper A (an Indian language) and Paper B (English), 300 marks each, qualifying at 25%.
  • Mains merit papers: Essay, GS I, GS II, GS III, GS IV and two optional papers, 250 marks each, 1750 in all.
  • Personality Test: 275 marks. Written plus interview: 2025 marks.

How to shade

  • Half: I can explain it, with gaps.

Spaced recall, in two pages

Page 1: the idea

Reading a page again feels like learning. Pulling it back from memory, with the book shut, is what makes it stay.

Spaced recall means you recall a topic a few times, with longer gaps each time. Each try is a little harder, and that effort is the point.

A simple rhythm: recall a topic the next day, then after about a week, then after about a month. If a recall goes badly, start the gaps again.

Recall means producing, not re-reading: write the main points on a blank page, explain it aloud, or answer a question on it. Then open the book and check.

Mix topics in one sitting. Polity, then geography, then ethics feels slower than one subject at a time, but it is closer to the exam.

Spaced recall, in two pages

Page 2: doing it with this book

Pick the lines you shaded lightly on the syllabus map. Write them in this week's row of the revision calendar.

For each line, close the book and write what you remember in five minutes. Mark the gaps in a different colour.

Put the same line on your calendar again a week later, and again a month later.

Use the flashcards at the back for facts that slip away. Make your own on the blank ones.

Keep it small: three to five recalls a day is enough if you keep it going. A missed day is fine; pick up the next day.
