The Preamble

One sentence that says where the Constitution's power comes from, what kind of country it creates, and what that country is for.

By the end you can
  • Rebuild the Preamble's four parts from memory.
  • Explain the five words that describe India, in your own words.
  • Name its only amendment and the three words it added.
  • Say whether it is part of the Constitution, with the cases.
  • Write a 150-word Mains answer on it.
You'll need
  • Your main Polity book, or the Constitution's text
  • A pencil, a pen, your highlighters
  • About 60 to 90 minutes over two sittings, at your pace

Without opening any book, write everything you already know about the Preamble. Words, dates, half-memories, guesses: all of it counts.

Answer key

1.1 Fill-in notes (p. 2)

1 the people of India 2 Socialist 3 Secular 4 Republic 5 political 6 worship 7 status 8 dignity 9 integrity 10 26 November 1949 11 Objectives Resolution 12 Jawaharlal Nehru 13 42nd 14 1976 15 Berubari Union 16 Kesavananda Bharati 17 LIC of India 18 court

1.2 Build it (p. 3)

Who: We, the people of India. What: Sovereign, Socialist★, Secular★, Democratic, Republic. Why: Justice (social, economic, political); Liberty (thought, expression, belief, faith, worship); Equality (of status, of opportunity); Fraternity (dignity of the individual; unity and integrity★ of the Nation). When: 26 Nov 1949; in force 26 Jan 1950.

Borrowing: Justice → Russian Revolution. Liberty, Equality, Fraternity → French Revolution. Having a Preamble → USA. Republic → French Revolution.

1.3 Own words (p. 4)

Your words will differ; check the idea. Socialist: a mixed economy aiming to reduce inequality, not state ownership of everything. Secular: the State has no religion and treats all equally, and may support or reform. Democratic: people rule through elected representatives. Republic: an elected head of State, not hereditary. Trap: Articles 25 to 28.

1.4 Timeline and sort (p. 5)

1946 Objectives Resolution · 1949 Preamble adopted · 1960 Berubari: not part · 1973 Kesavananda: part; amendable, not basic features · 1976 42nd Amdt · 1995 LIC: part · 2024 Balram Singh: upholds 1976 additions.

Can: a, c, e. Can't: b, d, f.

1.5 Practise (p. 6)

1. Thought, expression, belief, faith, worship. 2. Socialist, Secular, Integrity. 3. Berubari: not part; Kesavananda: part. UPSC style: (b). Statement 2 is false: the Preamble neither gives nor takes away the legislature's power.

1.6 Mains (p. 7): a good answer touches

Berubari (1960) → Kesavananda (1973) → LIC (1995); non-justiciable, so not a source or limit of power; courts use it to read unclear Articles and to find basic features; the 42nd Amendment and its 2024 upholding; conclusion: the Constitution's guiding statement of purpose.

2.2 Map (p. 12)

Equality 14–18 · Freedom 19–22 · against Exploitation 23–24 · Religion 25–28 · Culture and education 29–30 · Constitutional remedies 32. 12–13: definition of State, and laws against rights are void. 31: right to property, removed 1978. 33–35: armed forces, martial law, laws to give effect. 21A: education, ages 6 to 14.

2.3 One line (p. 13)

16 equal opportunity in public jobs · 17 untouchability abolished · 18 titles abolished (military, academic allowed) · 20 no retrospective criminal law, double punishment or forced self-incrimination · 21 life and personal liberty · 22 safeguards on arrest and detention · 23 no trafficking or forced labour · 24 no child under 14 in factories, mines, hazardous work · 25–28 conscience and religion · 29–30 culture, language; minorities' institutions · 32 go straight to the SC. Trap: Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009.

2.4 Article 19 and sort (p. 14)

(a) sovereignty and integrity, security of State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence. (b) sovereignty and integrity, public order. (c) sovereignty and integrity, public order, morality. (d), (e) general public interest, protection of Scheduled Tribes. (g) public interest, professional qualifications, State monopolies. No 19(1)(f): property, removed 1978.

Citizens only: 15, 16, 19, 29, 30. Every person: 14, 20, 21, 21A, 22 (not all of it for enemy aliens), 23, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28.

2.5–2.6 Emergency and writs (p. 15)

358: Article 19 suspended automatically, only if the ground is war or external aggression. 359: President's order suspends going to court for named rights. Never: 20 and 21, 44th Amendment.

Habeas corpus, “have the body”: produce a detained person; public and private persons. Mandamus, “we command”: public duty; not private persons, President, Governors. Prohibition, “to forbid”: stops a lower court before it exceeds powers; judicial and quasi-judicial only. Certiorari, “to be informed”: quashes an order already passed; judicial, quasi-judicial, administrative. Quo warranto, “by what authority”: challenges a claim to a substantive public office. Hook: prevents, cures, habeas corpus.

2.7 Cases (p. 16)

1950 Gopalan: narrow Art 21 · 1967 Golaknath: Parliament can't amend rights · 1973 Kesavananda: basic structure · 1978 Maneka Gandhi: fair, just, reasonable (arrow: Gopalan → Maneka) · 1992 Indra Sawhney: 50% cap, creamy layer · 2017 Puttaswamy: privacy is a right.

Art 32: itself a Fundamental Right; rights only; all India. Art 226: constitutional right; rights and any other purpose; within the state.

2.8 Practise (p. 17)

1. 15, 16, 19, 29, 30. 2. Certiorari. 3. Procedure must be fair, just and reasonable. 4. Art 300A; a constitutional (legal) right. UPSC style: (b). Art 15 and Art 30 are for citizens only.

2.9 Mains (p. 18): a good answer touches

Gopalan (1950) → Maneka (1978); rights read into Art 21: speedy trial (Hussainara Khatoon, 1979), livelihood (Olga Tellis, 1985), clean environment (Subhash Kumar, 1991), education (Unni Krishnan, 1993, later Art 21A), privacy (Puttaswamy, 2017); conclusion: a living Constitution, with the caution that courts should not overreach.
