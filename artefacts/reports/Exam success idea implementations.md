# Twenty-six ideas, built a hundred ways

*Part 3 of 4 in a research project on what helps people do well in exams. Neutral test-prep view: school, university entrance, standardised tests, professional and civil-service exams and language tests, in every country. Written 1 November 2026.*

Every one of the 26 central ideas from Part 2 reaches learners in a handful of recurring forms, usually four to eight, and the forms differ on a small number of design choices that repeat across the whole market. The choices that matter most are: who makes the material (the learner, a publisher, a teacher, a community or an AI), when the answer or feedback is shown, who controls the pace and the path (the learner, an algorithm or an institution), how visible the learner's effort is to others, and how hard a commitment is to undo. Product type predicts the form closely. Apps automate timing, scoring and nudges. Books carry worked examples, problem-type maps and exam targeting because these need no software. Courses and coaching sell a calendar, a teacher and a test series. Places sell silence and company. Region shapes the rest: East Asia turns study habits into products and places (mistake-notebook printers, paid silent desks, ranked national mocks, star-teacher video passes), India runs exam prep through coaching hubs, mock-test series, Telegram communities and a large free state layer, and the US and UK lean on school-licensed mastery software, learning-science books and productivity subscriptions. This report describes how each idea is built. It does not judge whether any of it works; that is Part 4.

## Headline

**The same handful of design dials recur across all 26 ideas.** Who makes the material decides most of the shortcuts family: AI-made cards, bought notes and photo solvers all move authorship away from the learner, and the products that push back do so by holding something back (the answer, the steps, the full card set). Visibility is the master dial for self-management: the same study timer is private planning, a habit streak, co-presence or a public ranking depending on who can see it. Commitment strength sorts the focus tools, from a dismissable pause to a lockout that cannot be undone.

**Algorithms are open and shared; content and authorship are where products now differ.** The FSRS scheduler is open source and runs in apps for medicine, chess, coding and civil-service exams. Socratic AI tutoring became a free feature of the three big chatbots between April and August 2025. Paid products therefore compete on exam-specific content, language, human backup and who writes the cards.

**Several ideas live almost only in books, methods or public services.** No catalogue app is built around interleaving, worked examples or learning how to learn. Exam targeting lives inside past-paper books and coaching advice. Exam-nerves support is led by public bodies in the UK and India, with commercial apps alongside.

**Regional forms are mostly the same idea under a local name and price.** A monthly silent desk costs roughly ₹1,500–5,000 in Indian cities, 100,000–200,000 won in Korea and about 400–500 yuan in China. A mistake notebook is 错题本, 오답노트 or an error log. National ranked mocks exist in Japan, China and India, but only Japan turns the rank into a pass-likelihood grade for each target university.

**Known pitfalls repeat across ideas.** The activity feels like learning while the learner's own effort is skipped (passive lectures, copied answers, AI-made cards, highlighting). Public metrics based on timer minutes invite inflation. Loss-framed mechanics (dying trees, broken streaks, demotion) create pressure. Numbers carry false precision (AI grades, percentiles, unpublished "adaptive" schedulers). Several of these pitfalls are admitted by the vendors themselves.

## Family 1: Remembering — schedulers became open code, card-making moved to AI

### RET Retrieval practice: ten forms split on recall or recognition

Retrieval practice is the most common idea in the catalogue (40 primary, 176 any). It ships in about ten forms that differ on four choices: whether the learner must produce the answer or only recognise it, who writes the questions, when feedback comes, and how big the unit is.

**Patterns.** *Two-sided flashcards* ask the learner to recall, flip and grade themselves. Anki's four buttons (Again, Hard, Good, Easy) move a card back to the first step, repeat it, advance it or graduate it ([Anki manual](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)); Anki cut SuperMemo's six grades to four, with one fail button, "since failures are a small share of reviews" ([Anki FAQ](https://docs.ankiweb.net/faqs/what-spaced-repetition-algorithm)). Brainscape replaces right-or-wrong with a 1–5 confidence rating, and low ratings bring a card back within a few cards ([Brainscape Academy](https://brainscape.com/academy/science-never-forgetting-spaced-repetition)). Carriers include Anki #1, the community-vetted AnKing medical deck #2, Quizlet #18, Brainscape #19, Cram.com #20, Mnemosyne #4 and StudyStack #436. *Card-writing rules* set the unit: SuperMemo's "20 rules" ask for understanding first, very short answers, cloze deletions (fill-in-the-blank sentences), image occlusion (hide part of a picture), no long lists, and sources on each card ([SuperMemo](https://www.supermemo.com/en/blog/twenty-rules-of-formulating-knowledge)). Note-to-card tools build cloze cards inside the learner's notes: RemNote #8, Logseq #9, SiYuan #10, Traverse #13, ZKMemo #17, Mochi #5. *Multiple-choice question banks* attach a long explanation to every item: UWorld #74, AMBOSS #75, Marrow #172 and PrepLadder #171 for Indian medical postgraduate exams, Magoosh #80, Testbook #178, and free sites Examveda #446 and IndiaBIX #447. Some exam-specific banks feed missed questions into a review queue, for example UPSC Prep #48, which uses SM-2 and negative marking. *Topic-sorted drill books* sell pre-made questions: China's 5·3 #401, India's Oswaal CBSE question banks #521 and MTG's line-by-line NCERT MCQs #522, R.S. Aggarwal's reasoning book #466 and Arun Sharma's CAT book #467 with three difficulty levels. China's volume version is 刷题 / 题海战术 #400, drilling very large numbers of questions. *Free recall* on a blank page is "blurting" in UK GCSE culture (#327, no inventor found) and 백지복습 in Korea (named in Part 2 only; no primary source checked). The Learning Scientists list six pen-and-paper forms, ending with a check against the book and a second try "a bit later on" ([Learning Scientists](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2016/6/23-1)). *Concealment on paper* is Japan's red sheet #426: key terms in orange or pink ink vanish under red plastic; Zebra sells matched pink and green pens for red and green sheets, and apps such as i-暗記シート copy the effect on photos ([rank-king.jp](https://rank-king.jp/article/14414); [App Store JP](https://apps.apple.com/jp/app/id803026043)). Cornell's cue column #333 and SQ3R's "Recite" #330 are the same move inside a note method. *Live classroom quiz games* make retrieval timed and public: Kahoot! #438 with speed points, Wayground (formerly Quizizz) #437, Blooket #439, Gimkit #440 with in-game money and returning missed questions, Socrative #490, Plickers #491 with printed cards scanned by the teacher's phone, and Edpuzzle #492 with questions inside videos. *Adaptive study modes* inside flashcard sites, such as Quizlet Learn, build a "personalized study path" and end when every term is answered correctly twice ([Quizlet Help](https://help.quizlet.com/hc/articles/360030986971); [Purdue](https://onlineteachinghub.education.purdue.edu/quizlet)). *AI-written questions* come from Revisely #27, Wisdolia #28, Jungle AI #29, Knowt #22, Vaia #21, Gizmo #23, Retain #26 and Neural Consult #79; Part 2 counted 11 entries where AI writes the cards and the app quizzes and schedules them. *Pretesting* #308 and *generation* #309 ask for answers before or instead of reading; Orbit/Quantum Country #15 embeds 112 review prompts inside essays (Part 1).

| Variant | Recall or recognition | Who makes it | Feedback | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blank page / blurting | Free recall | Learner | Delayed, self-checked | Whole topic |
| Red sheet | Cued recall | Learner marks the textbook | Immediate (lift the sheet) | Key term |
| Flashcards | Cued recall, self-graded | Learner, community or AI | Immediate | Fact or cloze line |
| Question bank | Mostly recognition | Publisher | Immediate, with explanation | Exam-style question |
| Drill book | Mixed | Publisher | End of set | Question |
| Quiz game | Recognition, timed | Teacher | Immediate, public | MCQ |
| AI generator | Either | AI | Immediate | Fact |

**Costs.** Blank-page methods are free; the red sheet is very cheap; Kahoot! and Gimkit are freemium; question banks are subscriptions or course prices, mostly unconfirmed in Part 1; Anki is free on desktop, and its iOS price was not confirmed.

**Pitfalls.** Flashcards need a real attempt before the flip, and the material needs to be partly forgotten first ([Learning Scientists](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2016/6/23-1)). Picture-matching vocabulary apps can feel like guessing games, so learners remember the picture rather than the meaning; one user guide advises the picture mode only for first exposure ([Woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/evaluating/1373089.html); [Bilibili guide](https://www.bilibili.com/opus/874234515752484921)). Self-graded designs are only as accurate as the learner's honest rating, as a competitor's review of Brainscape notes ([Nibble blog](https://nibble-app.com/blog/brainscape), competitor source). The question-sea habit is criticised as rote (#400). Live games add speed points and public leaderboards.

**By type and region.** Apps automate grading and push card-making onto the learner, a community deck or AI. Books ship pre-made questions and leave timing to the learner. Exam-specific banks tie retrieval to the exam format, so practice doubles as rehearsal. Regional forms are mostly low-tech: Japan's red sheet, China's drill books and 刷题, India's chapter-wise and NCERT MCQ books, the UK's blurting, Korea's 백지복습. No catalogue entry turns retrieval into a place.

### SPA Spacing and scheduled review: six ways to set the next review date

Spacing is the biggest app cluster (43 primary, 91 any; more than half apps). It is built in six main ways, plus a light seventh that resurfaces material for rereading.

**Patterns.** *Fixed day ladders*: UK school guidance uses 2-3-5-7 (days after learning, or counted back from the exam; no inventor found, #328, [BCU](https://bcu.ac.uk/exams-and-revision/best-ways-to-revise/spaced-repetition)); Indian aspirants pass around 1-3-7-21 (#329, origin unknown); Save My Exams gives a 30-day example with reviews on days 1, 7, 16 and 25 and a final pass five days before the exam ([Save My Exams](https://savemyexams.com/learning-hub/revision-tips/create-a-spaced-repetition-schedule-to-boost-memory)). India's SATHEE (Ministry of Education with IIT Kanpur) publishes a ladder of 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month and 3 months, then sets later gaps by self-rated difficulty (Easy up to 6 months, Hard 1–4 weeks), with an immediate re-review after a wrong answer; its inputs include target exam, date, response time and confidence ([SATHEE](https://sathee.iitk.ac.in/pyqs/bookmarking/revision-scheduler)). *Leitner boxes* (#317): the 1972 original used five compartments of increasing depth (1, 2, 5, 8 and 14 cm), reviewed when full; a right answer moves a card forward and a wrong one sends it back to box 1. Modern versions swap depths for fixed intervals, and Duolingo's first scheduler used Leitner ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leitner_system); [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-we-learn-how-you-learn)). *SM-2 ease formulas* (#318): original SM-2 uses first gaps of 1 and 6 days; Anki uses configurable learning steps (examples `1m 10m 1d`), starting ease 250%, easy bonus 1.30, hard interval 1.20, interval reset after a lapse and a 130% ease floor; Again lowers ease by 20 points, Hard by 15, Easy raises it by 15 ([Anki manual](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html); [Anki FAQ](https://docs.ankiweb.net/faqs/what-spaced-repetition-algorithm)). A card that lapses 8 times is tagged a "leech" and suspended by default ([Anki manual, Leeches](https://docs.ankiweb.net/manual/leeches)). *Fitted memory models with a retention target*: FSRS (#320) tracks difficulty, stability and current recall probability per card; the learner sets only the desired retention (default 90%, "70% to 97%" called reasonable), and FSRS-6 fits 21 parameters to the user's own history, falling back to defaults trained on several hundred million reviews ([ABC of FSRS](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/awesome-fsrs/wiki/ABC-of-FSRS)). Anki added it in version 23.10. It also runs in RemNote, Logseq, SiYuan, Rember, Avorio, Markji, Chessbook (chess openings), LeetFlash (coding problems) and spacedrep #43. Duolingo's Half-Life Regression (#321) predicts a half-life from practice features and was trained on 13 million learning traces, with public code ([Settles & Meeder, ACL 2016](https://preview.aclanthology.org/landing_page/P16-1174)). MaiMemo's SSP-MMC (#324) minimises review cost for a target retention; FSRS grew out of this work ([fsrs4anki](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs4anki)). *Exam-date overlays*: RemNote's Exam Scheduler sits on top of SM-2 or FSRS and adds a learning period, a two-in-a-row rule for missed cards, a final review of every card before the exam, a Catch Up period that proposes a temporary higher daily goal, and a computed daily goal ([RemNote Help](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/9102040-understanding-the-exam-scheduler)). Retain, Vaia, Gizmo, Quizlet Learn and Brainscape (#325) schedule with unpublished rules; Retain says it draws on "220 million learning records" (vendor claim). Backward planning by hand (#343) does the same job. *Human revision cycles*: successive relearning (#301) means recalling items to a criterion and relearning them in about three spaced sessions ([Rawson & Dunlosky 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/09637214221100484)); the coaching form is "revise each subject 3–5 times from a few standard books" (#340, [Legacy IAS](https://www.legacyias.com/?p=174404), coaching source), carried by standard books revised repeatedly (#218, #222). *Resurfacing for rereading*: Readwise #14 shows saved highlights daily on a light schedule with no recall step, about $5.59–12.99 a month after a trial ([Readwise](https://www.Readwise.io/pricing)); Foliole #16 and SuperMemo's incremental reading schedule chunks of long texts.

**Design choices.** Who sets the interval (a fixed rule, the learner's grade, a fitted model, a coach); a retention target or a growth multiplier; whether a deadline exists; daily caps on new cards and reviews; and how lapses are handled (reset, leech suspension, "two in a row").

**Pitfalls.** Review pile-ups: Anki warns that workload rises steeply above 90% retention and advises staying below 97% ([Anki manual](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html)), and RemNote's Catch Up period exists because learners fall behind. Repeated failures can trap SM-2 cards at low intervals; Anki's FAQ describes this complaint, and "ease hell" is community vocabulary. FSRS drops the ease multiplier and its project claims fewer reviews and better handling of breaks (self-reported). A vendor guide says many people copy someone else's intervals without regard to their own exam date ([iatroX](https://iatrox.com/academy/study/spacing-backwards-from-exam-day), vendor). Unpublished claims are common: SATHEE's "300% better long-term retention", Retain's 20% time saving over Anki, Brainscape's "twice as efficient", and Duolingo's model gain framed as "a 12% boost in user engagement" ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/how-duolingo-is-using-ai-to-humanize-virtual-language-lessons)), all vendor claims.

**By type and region.** Apps carry spacing; coaching supplies the calendar by hand; books leave timing to the learner. App-based spacing is strongest in medicine (AnKing, AMBOSS) and vocabulary (MaiMemo, Baicizhan, Duolingo). The algorithm layer is global and has Chinese roots (MaiMemo). Offline forms dominate elsewhere: UK day rules, India's revision cycles and 1-3-7-21, Leitner boxes.

### MNE Memory techniques: books teach the system, apps supply the picture

Memory techniques (19 primary, 25 any) reach learners almost entirely through books and memory-sport courses, plus two apps.

**Patterns.** The *memory palace* or journey (#344) places vivid images along a familiar route; how-to books include Foer #272, O'Brien #470 and #471, Dellis #472 and Horsley #280, with Yates #474 as history. *Number codes*: the major system (#345) turns digits into consonant sounds, then words, then images, and extends from 00–99 to 00–999; the Dominic system maps digits to letters that become people's initials; PAO (#347) makes each number a person-action-object image on top of either code ([Art of Memory forum](https://forum.artofmemory.com/t/confusion-between-pao-dominic-and-major/53957), practitioner forum; [Magnetic Memory Method](https://magneticmemorymethod.com/pao-system), course vendor). *Pegs and stories* (#346, #350; *The Memory Book* #281) attach items to a pre-learned list or one vivid story. *Acronyms and acrostics* (#349) and chunking (#348) need no setup. The *keyword method* (#312) pairs a foreign word with a sound-alike keyword and then an image of its meaning; it was first used in a computer-run supplement to Stanford's Russian course ([Atkinson & Raugh 1974](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD0787843.pdf)). SuperMemo advises mnemonics mainly for the small share of hard items. *Picture-word apps*: Baicizhan #398 (China, freemium, used for CET-4/6, gaokao and postgraduate entrance English) matches each word to a picture; the learner infers meaning from an example sentence, then taps the matching image, and "chopping" a word gives a sense of achievement ([Bilibili guide](https://www.bilibili.com/opus/874234515752484921); [Woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/evaluating/4110348.html)). Memrise #24 pairs spaced review with mnemonics and native-speaker video. *Word roots*: *Word Power Made Easy* #229 (1949) teaches vocabulary through Latin and Greek roots with self-tests. Paid "memory improvement" courses such as Kwik Learning #293 and photographic-memory training #390 are coded under quick fixes, not here.

**Design choices.** Setup cost is the main dial: acronyms and keywords take minutes per item; a palace needs known routes; pegs need a learned list of 10–20; number codes need a 100- or 1,000-image code learned in advance, which pays off mainly for numbers such as dates and statistics. Who makes the image is the second dial: books teach learners to make their own, while apps supply them ready-made, which removes setup but also the learner's own elaboration.

**Pitfalls.** Supplied pictures can be remembered without the meaning (Baicizhan analyses above). Commercial packaging can drift into quick-fix claims.

**By type and region.** Books and memory-sport culture dominate; China's vocabulary apps are the main app form. No source was checked on how coaching institutes teach mnemonics.

## Family 2: Understanding, being taught and making sense — who picks, who paces, who answers

### TCH Expert teaching and curated content: star teachers, free state lectures and the standard-book canon

Teaching is the biggest single idea by primary count (68 primary, 114 any). It appears in seven patterns that differ on live or recorded delivery, who picks the content, who sets the pace, and how much practice is bundled.

**Patterns.** *Live star-teacher classes* enrol learners in a batch taught by a named teacher, with support tutors for doubts and regular tests: Gaotu #395, New Oriental #397, Vedantu #183, Unacademy #170. Gaotu's school-age tutoring stopped under China's 2021 Double Reduction policy (Part 1, BK). Korea's top lecturers, *ilta*, earn through retainers, revenue shares and textbook sales; one disclosed over ₩10bn in salary for 2020 ([Korea Herald](https://www.koreaherald.com/article/3059340); [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/lifestyle/star-instructors-teach-thousands-earn-millions/11728762)). *Recorded lectures sold as passes* let the learner set the pace: Megastudy #408 and Etoos #409 for the Suneung, Gongdangi #412 with unlimited passes for civil-service exams, Hackers #413, Study Sapuri #419, Outlier.org #292 and PhysicsWallah #173. Study Sapuri is reported at ¥2,178 a month with lessons of about 5–15 minutes, from comparison sites that may carry ads ([coeteco.jp](https://coeteco.jp/articles/12896)). Toshin High School #421 is a hybrid: recorded star lectures shown inside supervised franchise centres. Korea's competition regulator blocked Megastudy's purchase of Gongdangi's owner in March 2024 (Part 1). In India, StudyIQ #180 and exam YouTube channels #232 run free mass lectures that funnel viewers to paid courses. *Free state lectures*: Korea's EBSi #410 gains its pull from exam linkage, since setters draw part of the Suneung from EBS workbooks #411 (about 50% by question count since 2022, down from about 70%; [Herald Biz](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10776287)). India offers SATHEE #460 in 12 languages, SWAYAM #525 and NPTEL #462 with proctored certificate exams, IIT-PAL #461, DIKSHA #458 and ePathshala #459 with read-aloud textbooks. *Classroom coaching as a place*: Aakash #184, Allen #185, Vajiram & Ravi #201, Kawaijuku and Sundai #422 with full-time courses for students retaking entrance exams, SAPIX and Yotsuya Otsuka #423 with ability groups, Huatu #393 and Offcn #394 in China, whose "protocol classes" refund the fee if the student fails. Coaching districts (Kota and Delhi #235, Noryangjin #415) turn teaching into an immersive place. *The standard-book canon* has learners choose one book per subject and revise it many times: NCERT #216, Laxmikanth #217, Spectrum #218, Singhania #222, Wren & Martin #230, Lucent GK #524. *Digests and compilations* compress material: monthly current-affairs compilations #215, Pratiyogita Darpan #453, GKToday #452, the *Bharat* yearbook #527, PIB #214 and newspaper exam supplements #212, #213. *Audio delivery*: Speechify #493 and NaturalReader #494 read documents aloud (coded as rereading by ear). Teaching principles such as Rosenshine's #242 and the CAST UDL guidelines #241 shape instruction rather than ship as products.

**Design choices and costs.** Live batches set the pace for the learner; recorded passes hand pace back, along with the risk of falling behind; Toshin's centres put supervision back. Curation (the canon, digests) is teaching as selection: someone else decides what is enough, which links teaching to exam targeting. Costs run from free (EBSi, SATHEE, SWAYAM, NPTEL, YouTube) to low flat rates (Study Sapuri) to course fees (Indian coaching) and premium Korean passes; most of these prices were not verified this round.

**Pitfalls.** Students who watched a fluent lecturer predicted they would remember more than those who watched a halting one, which the authors framed as a confidence illusion ([Princeton record](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/instructor-fluency-leads-to-higher-confidence-in-learning-but-not/)). Free lectures double as marketing funnels. Text-to-speech is passive rereading. Product responses include questions inserted into videos (Edpuzzle #492) and bundled tests.

**By type and region.** Celebrity teaching is an industry where one high-stakes national exam concentrates demand: Korea (Suneung), China (gaokao), India (JEE, NEET, UPSC), Japan (university entrance). In the US, video teaching is more often a mastery platform without a star (Khan Academy #65, Up Learn #70). India uniquely combines coaching hubs, the standard-book canon, free-to-paid YouTube funnels and a large free state layer.

### TUT Tutoring and mentoring: the dial is when the answer appears

Tutoring (13 primary, 27 any) comes in six patterns. The key design choice is when, or whether, the final answer is shown, and who can switch that rule off.

**Patterns.** *Socratic modes in general chatbots*: ChatGPT Study Mode #49 (29 July 2025) runs on custom system instructions and can be toggled on and off by the user, with no admin lock for schools ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/chatgpt-just-added-a-powerful-new-study-mode-heres-how-it-can-help-you-learn-faster); [implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/openai-unveils-study-mode-to-curb-cheating-and-reclaim-trust-in-the-classroom/); OpenAI's own page could not be fetched). Gemini Guided Learning #50 (6 August 2025) asks probing questions, breaks problems into steps and adds diagrams and quizzes; Google built it at the model level (LearnLM) after finding that "better prompting alone" was not enough, and teachers can share it through Google Classroom ([Google blog](https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/education/guided-learning/)). Claude's learning mode #51 (2 April 2025) launched in Claude for Education with questions such as "How would you approach this problem?" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude-for-education)). *Purpose-built tutors that refuse answers*: Khanmigo #53 uses hints and withholds final answers by design; about $4 a month or $44 a year for US learners, free for teachers in eligible countries, district prices reported at $10–15 per student per year with sources disagreeing ([Khanmigo](https://khanmigo.ai/learners); [softwarefinder](https://softwarefinder.com/artificial-intelligence/khanmigo-ai)). India's NDLI-FERMI tutor #463 (IIT Kharagpur) gives Socratic hints with "Scan & Ask" for science subjects, free for registered users. Medical platforms add tutors: Lecturio #76, Osmosis #77, iatroX #78. *Explain-then-check AI teachers*: JEET AI #464 explains on a virtual board, then asks check questions, from ₹1,499 a month (press release); PadhAI #188 pairs an AI tutor with news summaries and questions for UPSC. *Human one-to-one tuition*: OneSmart #406, Yuanfudao #88, and tutoring bundled with mocks at Kaplan #81 and The Princeton Review #82. *On-demand doubt solving*: Filo matches a photographed question to a human tutor, which the company says takes 60 seconds using about 25 matching factors (company claims; [Google Cloud story](https://cloud.google.com/customers/filo)); Xueba Jun #405 added tutoring to photo search. *Mentorship*: Edukemy #192, CivilsDaily's mentor-led Telegram groups #194, Vaidra's "AI mentor" #456, Korea's Gongsin #432 with top-university student mentors, and Studyplus for School #431 where teachers see students' logs and message them.

**Design choices.** An answer-withholding gradient runs from "never gives the final answer" (Khanmigo, FERMI) through "guides by default but can be switched off" (the three chatbots) to "explains first, then checks" (JEET AI) to "gives the full solution" (photo solvers, under OFF). Withholding holds only where the product controls the mode. Because Socratic tutoring is now free inside chatbots, paid tutors compete on syllabus grounding, language and human backup. Human mentoring in exam markets is mostly about strategy and accountability: what to read, a weekly plan, answer reviews.

**Pitfalls.** Modes that can be switched off; OpenAI's instruction-based approach accepts some inconsistency ([Geekflare](https://geekflare.com/news/chatgpt-gets-a-study-mode-key-things-to-know)). The opposite failure is friction: Khan Academy leaders acknowledged low Khanmigo use, which one blog attributes to students not knowing what to ask ([guyonai substack](https://guyonai.substack.com/p/after-khanmigo), blog), and reviewers said Guided Learning over-questions and loses track ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/06/google-takes-on-chatgpts-study-mode-with-new-guided-learning-tool-in-gemini/)).

**By type and region.** Mostly apps plus mentoring programmes. The US has purpose-built AI tutors; India has free IIT-built tutors and mentorship sold for strategy; China and Korea have premium one-to-one tuition and photo-search companies that added human tutors.

### ELA Understanding and elaboration: named methods that products can only prompt

Elaboration (19 primary, 75 any) is an activity in the learner's own head, so it mostly exists as named methods. Products can only prompt it with questions, blank fields or things to manipulate.

**Patterns.** *Question prompts*: elaborative interrogation #303 asks "why is this true?"; the Learning Scientists add linking to experience and comparing concepts ([Learning Scientists podcast](https://www.learningscientists.org/learning-scientists-podcast/2018/3/21/episode-15-how-students-can-use-interleaving-elaboration-dual-coding-and-concrete-examples)); the Question Formulation Technique #508 has learners produce and rank their own questions. *Explaining steps*: self-explanation #304; the Feynman technique #326 (explain simply to an imagined beginner, find the gap, return to the source), named on Scott Young's blog in 2011 rather than by Feynman (Part 1); teach-back #359 and reciprocal teaching #361 move it into a group. *Concrete examples and dual coding* (#305, #306): pair words with timelines, flowcharts, cartoon strips or graphic organisers, with the learner doing the connecting. *Concept maps* #336 use labelled links between nodes, unlike radial mind maps; Miro #129, Heptabase #125, Scrintal #130 and LiquidText #132 carry both. *Reading frameworks*: SQ3R #330 (Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review), SQ4R and PQ4R #331 (add Reflect), PQRST #332 (ends in Test), THIEVES #507 (a preview checklist, 2002), KWL #506 (Know, Want to know, Learned; Ogle 1986, with variants KWL-Plus and KWHL; [Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/KWL_table), secondary), and *How to Read a Book* #497. *Interactive explorables*: GeoGebra #443 and Desmos #444 (free), Mathigon #489, Brilliant #69 (subscription, price not checked). AI study modes (#49–#51, #53, #463) are now the most widespread "why" prompt. Textbooks carry it too: H C Verma #523, G.C. Leong #221, Mrunal.org's plain-language explainers #209.

**Design choices.** Reading frameworks differ on where the question step sits (before reading in SQ3R and KWL, as a preview in THIEVES) and whether they end in a self-test (PQRST) or a reflection (PQ4R). The recite and test steps are retrieval. Methods are free.

**Pitfalls.** Supplied pictures and supplied questions skip the learner's own construction step. The Feynman label is untested as a named system. No product was found that prompts self-explanation inside a video or PDF reader other than AI chat.

**By type and region.** Mostly named methods spread by US and UK learning-science groups and university learning centres, plus a few maths sites.

### EXM Worked examples and model answers: problem-type books in maths, topper copies in essays

Worked examples (8 primary, 22 any) live in books; no catalogue app is built around them. The form follows the exam format.

**Patterns.** *Problem-type-to-method books* give a solved model for every problem type: Korea's 수학의 정석 #417 (first edition 1966; estimates of 37–45 million copies), Japan's チャート式 #424 with colour-coded levels (white, yellow, blue, red) and, from January 2026, explanation videos, and R.S. Aggarwal's *Quantitative Aptitude* #465. The learner reads the solved example, tries matching exercises, and learns to spot the type. *Faded worked examples* (#307) start fully solved and remove steps one at a time, usually from the end (backward fading), or adaptively by performance ([Renkl & Atkinson](https://faculty.engineering.asu.edu/mre/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2020/02/Exp_Rev_LI06.pdf)); the IES practice guide #243 includes worked examples. *Topper answer copies* for essay exams: theiashub #195 keeps year-by-year libraries of top candidates' UPSC Mains answers ([theiashub](https://theiashub.com/toppers/upsc/2025)); "daily answer writing modelled on topper copies" #341 combines writing, comparing and evaluation. Coaching blogs recommend attempting the question first without looking, then comparing to find gaps rather than phrases, rewriting the weakest part and logging recurring errors ([NEXT IAS](https://www.nextias.com/blog/how-to-use-upsc-toppers-copies-effectively/); [CivilsDaily](https://www.civilsdaily.com/what-i-learned-after-reading-100-topper-copies/), coaching sources). Institutes host their own topper-copy pages, which also act as marketing ([Vajirao](https://vajiraoinstitute.com/toppers-answer-copy.aspx)). *Topper strategy books and interviews*: #224, #225, #227, #228, Mrunal's interview PDFs #209, Gongsin's student mentors #432, Reddit officer AMAs #233.

**Design choices and costs.** The key choice is order: attempt then compare (which turns the model into feedback) or study then imitate. In maths the model teaches a procedure; in essays it teaches structure, demand words, diagrams and coverage, while the top candidate's reasoning stays hidden. Books are cheap paperbacks, topper copies are free online, evaluation programmes cost course fees.

**Pitfalls.** Coaching sources call copying format without the reasoning "the biggest mistake", and warn against memorising toppers' introductions as templates ([NEXT IAS](https://www.nextias.com/blog/how-to-use-upsc-toppers-copies-effectively/); [SuperKalam](https://blog.superkalam.com/blog/crack-upsc-with-toppers-notes-and-answer-copies-a-complete-guide), vendor). Whether topper copies are published with permission, and how complete they are, was not verified.

**By type and region.** Problem-type books dominate where exams are calculation-heavy (Korean and Japanese maths, Indian aptitude tests). Topper copies dominate where exams are descriptive (UPSC Mains).

### NOT Note-making and organising: is the note a product or a process?

Notes (29 primary, 51 any) are dominated by apps. Eight patterns split on one question: is the note something to keep and organise, or is writing it the learning?

**Patterns.** *Page formats*: Cornell notes #333 split the page into a cue column, a notes area and a summary strip (Walter Pauk, 1950s); review means covering the notes and reciting from the cues ([UW–Eau Claire](https://www.uwec.edu/sites/default/files/documents/Cornell-System-Overview.pdf); [Univ. of Cincinnati](https://www.uc.edu/campus-life/learning-commons/learning-resources/notetaking-resources/cornell-method-notes.html)); sources differ on whether cues are written in class or soon after. Outline, charting, boxing and other formats are #334. *Mind maps* #335 (Buzan, 1974) use radial keyword branches, ideally redrawn from memory; XMind #127, MindNode #126, MindMeister #128, Traverse #13 with cloze cards, and iCanStudy #288 for first-pass learning. *Linked atomic notes*: Zettelkasten #337 (Ahrens, 2017) uses fleeting, permanent and project notes, with fleeting notes rewritten within a day or two ([Zettelkasten forum](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/comment/14188/), forum); apps include Obsidian #121, Logseq #9, Roam and Zettlr #131, SiYuan #10, Heptabase #125. *Folder systems*: *Building a Second Brain* #283 with PARA folders and progressive summarisation in layers (capture, bold, highlight, summary), with Forte noting that not every note needs every layer ([Forte Labs](https://fortelabs.com/blog/progressive-summarization-iii-guidelines-and-principles/)); Notion #117 adds syllabus and revision databases. *Condensed revision notes*: note compression #342 cuts about 100 pages to 25–30 before the exam (Part 1, citing a coaching blog); summarisation #314; the red sheet doubles notes as a quiz. *Digital handwriting*: GoodNotes #123, Notability #124 (synced to lecture audio), OneNote #122. *Notes that become cards*: RemNote #8, Mochi #5, SuperMemo #3. *Others' notes and AI note-takers*: Clearnote #433 (Japan, free photos of handwritten notes plus Q&A), coaching notes #194 and compilations #215, and AI tools Coconote #57, Turbo AI #55, Scholarly #31, StudyFetch #54.

**Design choices.** Who writes (learner, peer, coach, AI); the unit (page, card, map, folder); whether a self-test or shrinking step is built in; whether notes link. Exam-oriented forms (Cornell, red sheet, compression, notes-to-cards) build in a self-test or a shrink; knowledge-management forms (PARA) do not.

**Pitfalls.** The "collector's fallacy": gathering material feels rewarding, but knowing *about* a text is not knowing it ([zettelkasten.de](https://zettelkasten.de/posts/collectors-fallacy/)). Highlighting is selection without reorganising. Typing allows verbatim copying (#379). Over-organising was not measured by any source found.

**By type and region.** Apps dominate globally; Japan adds shared handwritten notes and the red-sheet notebook; India adds bought coaching notes and compilations. AI note-takers move authorship away from the learner, the same shift seen in GEN and OFF.

## Family 3: Practising and feedback — the loop rarely closes on its own

### MIX Interleaving and varied practice: mostly a side effect of shuffles and mocks

Interleaving is the rarest idea in products (2 primary, 13 any), carried almost only by books.

**Patterns.** *Mixed homework sets* introduce a new problem type in a block, then spread its later practice across later assignments mixed with other types; in Rohrer and colleagues' design, the first four problems sat in one assignment and the other eight were spread across later ones ([Learning Scientists](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2021/10/28-1)). A classroom-scale version gave mixed or blocked worksheets to 54 Grade 7 maths classes over four months. Rohrer and Hartwig found 78% of textbook practice problems were blocked and 11% interleaved ([Rohrer & Hartwig](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-spaced-and-interleaved-mathematics-practice-rohrer-hartwig.pdf)). *Mixed review in apps*: Seneca Learning #66 (UK, free plus premium) is the only catalogue platform described as mixing topics and returning to them. *Random Q-bank blocks and full mocks*: UWorld lets users build mixed blocks ([UWorld](https://medical.uworld.com/usmle/features/)); full-length mocks are mixed by nature. *Problem-type atlases*: 수학의 정석 #417 and チャート式 #424 make the learner pick a method by type. China's 5·3 #401 sorts questions by topic, the opposite choice. *Books that teach the idea*: *Make It Stick* #247, *How We Learn* #249, *Range* #263, Dunlosky et al. #236.

**Design choices.** When mixing starts (after first exposure), the mix ratio, whether problem-type labels are hidden (labels skip the "choose the method" step), and whether mixing spans subjects. No catalogue product was found that explicitly blocks first and then spreads later problems across later sessions; in software, mixing is usually a "random" or "all topics" filter.

**Pitfalls.** The authors note interleaving "probably takes (a little) more time than blocking". Topic-sorted books, the dominant format in China and India, are blocked by design, so mixing has to come from the learner or a mock.

### ADP Working on weak spots: forced path, gate or report

Weak-spot work (14 primary, 34 any) has six patterns that differ on who detects the weakness and what happens next.

**Patterns.** *Knowledge-space engines*: ALEKS starts with an adaptive Knowledge Check of roughly 20–30 questions (30–90 minutes), shows a "pie" of mastered, learned and not-yet topics, lets the learner choose only from topics they are "ready to learn", and reassesses periodically ([ALEKS orientation](https://www.mheducation.com/unitas/highered/explore/schools/ancora/aleks-student-orientation-ancora.pdf); [UCSD guide](https://summer.ucsd.edu/_files/SSP/CHEM6A%20ALEKS%20Assessment%20Guide.pdf)); paid via institutions (#64). Squirrel AI #59 (China, fine-grained "knowledge points"), Embibe #182 (India, freemium) and Xueersi tablets #396 follow the same pattern; their rules and prices were not verified. *Score-based mastery gates*: Khan Academy has four levels per skill (Attempted, Familiar, Proficient, Mastered) worth 0/50/80/100 points, and a wrong answer on a quiz or unit test can drop a level ([Khan Academy help](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631)); thresholds have changed over time. IXL's SmartScore rises and falls by difficulty, recency and consistency; in the 90–100 "Challenge Zone" a right answer adds 1–2 points and a wrong one removes 3–8 ([IXL guide](https://www.ixl.com/materials/SmartScore_Guide.pdf), vendor). Sparx Maths #67 and DeltaMath #73 are school-licensed; the method behind gates is Bloom's mastery learning #363. *Adaptive exams*: the GRE chooses the second section of each measure from performance on the first ([ETS](https://www.ets.org/content/ets-org/ca/en/gre/test-takers/general-test/scores/understand-scores.html)); GMAT Focus is described by a third party as adapting after every answer ([test-ninjas](https://test-ninjas.com/gmat-adaptive-testing-explained), not GMAC); the Duolingo English Test calibrates items with explanatory IRT models and selects them by Thompson sampling ([NeurIPS 2024](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/107597)). Prep products respond with official practice on the real software (#86) and adaptive quant courses (#85). *Diagnostic then plan*: PrepScholar #445 builds an SAT, ACT or GRE plan from a diagnostic test and advertises a score guarantee. *Q-bank analytics*: UWorld reports by subject, system and topic with percentile rank and time per question; AMBOSS tags each question with a 1–5 difficulty "hammer" and a High-Yield filter ([AMBOSS](https://www.amboss.com/us/features)); Oliveboard #181 gives percentile analytics for 50+ exams; Fenbi says it builds a data report and a "predicted score" from practice records ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/%E7%B2%89%E7%AC%94%E6%95%99%E5%B8%88-%E6%95%99%E5%B8%88%E8%B5%84%E6%A0%BC%E8%AF%81%E6%95%99%E5%B8%88%E6%8B%9B%E8%81%98%E5%A4%87%E8%80%83%E7%A5%9E%E5%99%A8/id1071050365)). *Deliberate practice by humans*: *Peak* #262, *Ultralearning*'s drill principle #273, ability-grouped cram schools #423, one-to-one tutoring #406. Spacing schedulers adapt timing per item, not topic choice, and carry ADP only as a secondary.

**Design choices.** Unit of weakness (knowledge point, skill, topic tag, question type); detection signal (right or wrong, plus time, confidence or item difficulty); the adaptivity rule (prerequisite graph, point threshold, ability estimate, or none); how much the learner may choose (ALEKS restricts, UWorld leaves it all to the learner); and whether mastery can decay. Costs run from free (Khan Academy) to subscriptions (IXL), institutional licences (ALEKS, Sparx) and premium tutoring.

**Pitfalls.** Gates can feel punitive: IXL's asymmetric scoring draws user complaints and a competitor's critique ([Boddle](https://www.boddlelearning.com/article/ixl-punishes-wrong-answers), competitor). Vendor brochures carry outcome claims.

**By type and region.** School products in the US and UK force a path through gates, because teachers want completion data. Adult high-stakes Q-banks give analytics and leave the choice to the learner. National exam bodies use adaptive testing to measure, not to teach. Indian test-prep apps mostly sit at "report shows weak topics", with a few knowledge-graph claims; how they compute weak-topic flags is undocumented.

### FBK Feedback and error correction: only East Asian mistake-notebook tools close the loop automatically

Feedback (16 primary, 68 any) is carried mostly by websites, in seven patterns. The choices that matter are timing, depth, who marks, and whether the learner is made to try again.

**Patterns.** *Explanations in question banks*: UWorld's Tutor mode explains after each question, Timed mode at the end of a block; AMBOSS explains every option, links to its library and adds an "Attending Tip" with an experienced physician's reasoning; India's free official NTA Abhyas gives question-wise solutions ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/neet-jee-main-how-nta-national-test-abhyas-app-helps-prepare-for-exams)); Fenbi lists "intelligent marking" by exam rules. *Human marking of written answers*: Z-kai (Japan, since 1931) hand-marks hard problems by correspondence; Indian Mains test series advertise mentor calls, model answers and some 24-hour turnarounds ([UPSCprep](https://upscprep.com/mains-test-series-upsc-2024); [Civils Daily](https://www.civilsdaily.com/?p=143396), providers); Insights IAS #198 runs free daily answer writing with peer and mentor review; US bar courses Barbri #83 and Themis #84 grade practice essays; Studiosity #92 and Busuu #435 correct writing. *AI answer graders*: photograph a handwritten answer and receive a rubric score, comments and a model answer in about a minute. SuperKalam #187 (plan about ₹4,799, unverified), UPSC AI #455, PrepAiro #190, CollectorBabu #189 (AI plus human graders), Vaidra, AnswerWriting.com, Edukemy and PW OnlyIAS all do this for civil-service Mains. PrepAiro says it calibrated on 10,000+ human-marked answers but publishes no accuracy figure (vendor claim, [PrepAiro](https://prepairo.ai/upsc/llms/upsc-ai-mains-evaluation/)). Outside India, Save My Exams #68 marks answers with AI and Grammarly #90 and Writable #91 give writing feedback. *Self-marking with official schemes*: CBSE sample papers and marking schemes #519, Physics & Maths Tutor #71, Akahon #425 with answers. *Mistake notebooks* (错题本 / 오답노트 / error log, #141): log each wrong answer with cause and principle and re-test before the next mock; free and common in JEE, UPSC, GMAT and CAT coaching. Chinese apps photograph the wrong question, erase the handwriting, sort it by subject and push review papers that are marked online to update the error record ([错题plus](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/id1476015217)); printers erase handwriting so the page can be redone ([错题打印机](https://apps.apple.com/cn/app/%E9%94%99%E9%A2%98%E6%89%93%E5%8D%B0%E6%9C%BA-%E8%87%AA%E5%8A%A8%E6%B8%85%E9%99%A4%E6%89%8B%E5%86%99%E7%AC%94%E8%BF%B9-%E8%AF%95%E5%8D%B7%E9%94%99%E9%A2%98%E6%95%B4%E7%90%86/id1598835335)); Zuoyebang's Miaomiaoji pocket printer #391 is about ¥200–600 (not checked). Huawei's notebook suggests up to six "举一反三" variant questions per maths mistake, done on paper with answers hidden ([Huawei](https://consumer.huawei.com/cn/support/content/zh-cn16028630)). A UPSC site puts missed questions into an SM-2 queue #48. *Exam wrappers* are short forms completed when a graded exam comes back, asking students to identify strengths and weaknesses, judge their preparation and classify their errors; in one CMU physics course they were collected and handed back just before the next exam ([CMU Eberly Center](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/designteach/teach/examwrappers/)); free templates exist ([UVM](https://www.uvm.edu/ctl/exam-wrappers)). *Peer review*: Insights IAS, ForumIAS #199 and peer instruction #360.

**Design choices.** Timing (per item, end of block, days later); depth (score only, correct option, why each option is wrong, model answer, line-by-line rubric); marker (key, AI, expert, peer, self); closure (forced re-attempt or variant question, or stop at the explanation). East Asian error-notebook tools are the only common products that close the loop automatically (capture, classify, reprint or vary, re-mark). Western Q-banks mostly stop at the explanation plus a "flag question" option; Indian test series stop at explanation plus percentile. Exam wrappers are almost absent from commercial products.

**Pitfalls.** AI graders can be "consistently wrong in the same direction", as one AI vendor concedes ([AnswerWriting.com](https://answerwriting.anthroholic.com/ai-answer-evaluation-vs-human-evaluation), vendor); no independent comparison of AI and human Mains marks was found. Human markers disagree and tire, as vendors also note ([CollectorBabu](https://collectorbabu.com/upsc-mains-evaluation-platforms-comparison.html), vendor). Error logs have no built-in return trigger unless one is designed in. Error-notebook apps carry auto-renewing subscriptions. Students focus only on the score, which is why wrappers exist.

### TST Exam rehearsal and technique: realism, rank display and the review step

Rehearsal (22 primary, 54 any) spreads evenly across apps, websites, courses, books and methods. Six patterns differ on realism, frequency, how scores are shown, and whether a review step follows.

**Patterns.** *Full-length timed mocks* (#339): Kaplan #81 and Princeton Review #82 sell them with courses; Indian mass test series include Testbook (FY24 revenue ₹136.7 crore), Oliveboard, Adda247 and BYJU'S Exam Prep (#177–#181). *National ranked mocks*: Kawaijuku's mocks grade each target university A–E; secondary Japanese sources put A at about 80%+ chance of passing and E at 20% or less, computed from past results, with about 15% of A-grade students still failing and grades not comparable across providers ([bestjuku](https://bestjuku.com/shingaku/s-article/53563/); [Passnavi](https://passnavi.obunsha.co.jp/article/keisetsu_jidai/2509/02/?p=2); official definitions not verified). Fenbi runs "mock contests" about every two weeks with comparison and a predicted score. India has CAT mock series with national percentiles (IMS, TIME, Career Launcher, #186) and all-India civil-service test series (#196). *Official computer-test simulators*: NTA Abhyas offers free JEE Main and NEET mocks on the real interface since 2020, with Hindi tests added in December 2025 ([PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1625181); [Ministry of Education](https://education.gov.in/node/20438)); College Board Bluebook and GMAT Official Practice run full adaptive mocks (#86). For UPSC there is no official mock, only free past papers (#454). *Past-paper books*: Akahon #425 (one book per university since 1954), Tianli 38 Sets #402 (about 38 full papers per subject, often by province), and topic-sorted books (5·3, Oswaal, PYQ books). Only the full-paper format is rehearsal; topic-sorted books serve retrieval and targeting. *Timed Q-bank blocks*: UWorld Timed mode, with third-party advice to switch from tutor to timed mode midway ([Elite Medical Prep](https://elitemedicalprep.com/uworld-mastery-a-roadmap-for-step-1-success/)). *In-exam strategy*: read the paper, answer known items first, watch the time (#142); negative-marking rules such as UPSC Prelims' one-third deduction per wrong answer ([Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/upsc-exam/negative-marking-in-upsc/), coaching source), with coaching advice to guess only after eliminating options ([Edukemy](https://edukemy.com/blog/how-to-tackle-negative-marking-in-upsc-prelims/)); and answer structures such as PEEL #510 and essay guides #226.

**Design choices.** Realism (paper or exact computer interface; fixed or adaptive); length (sectional or full); frequency (Fenbi fortnightly; Indian series a fixed number of tests); score display (raw, percentile, all-India rank, pass-likelihood band); review support (solutions, discussion video, mentor call). Japan alone turns rank into a per-school probability band; India mostly shows rank and percentile; China shows rank and predicted score.

**Pitfalls.** Percentile and percent-correct measure different things, and tutor-mode scores run higher than timed ones ([iatroX blog](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/what-is-a-good-uworld-percentage), competitor). Pass-likelihood bands are probabilities, not promises. Vision IAS was fined twice by India's consumer regulator (₹3 lakh in January 2025, ₹11 lakh in December 2025) over misleading topper claims (Part 1, #196). One App Store user reported NTA Abhyas's backend as unresponsive. Most products put the rank first and the review second.

## Family 4: Steering your own study — plans, filters, focus and the visibility dial

### TGT Exam targeting: built into books, coaching advice and sometimes the exam itself

Targeting (3 primary, 19 any) is rarely a product of its own; it lives inside past-paper books, Q-banks, strategy books and coaching advice.

**Patterns.** *Past-paper frequency analysis*: solve past papers timed and map the syllabus by topic frequency (#338); trend analysis in *Mission IAS* #228; tags in Testbook #178 and SuperKalam #187; NEET YouTube strategies built on past questions #484. *Syllabus mapping*: the AnKing deck #2 is mapped to the medical exam syllabus; newspaper exam explainers map to the UPSC syllabus #213. *Setter-linked materials*: Korea's Suneung draws on EBS workbooks; the link was cut from 70% to 50% by item count from the 2022 exam and moved to "indirect" linkage (same theme or material rather than reused passages), with all English linked items indirect ([Daehak News](https://www.dhnews.co.kr/news/view/179522943035001)); sources differ on whether direct linkage ended entirely. EBSi lectures are free and the workbooks low-priced. Board-made model papers such as CBSE sample papers #519 do similar work. *"Few sources, many revisions"* and "foundation first": #340, NCERT #216, Laxmikanth #217. *80/20 prioritisation* by marks yield #356; AMBOSS's High-Yield filter. *Institution-specific targeting*: Akahon's one book per university and Kawaijuku's per-school grades.

**Design choices.** Tagging granularity (paper, section, syllabus line, micro-topic), years of history used, weighting by marks, and whether new topics are flagged separately. Korea shows that targeting can be built into the exam system, and that its value changes when the setters change the rule.

**Pitfalls.** Frequency analysis looks backwards; no source documented cases where it failed. How Indian apps compute frequency tags is unpublished.

### PLN Planning and self-monitoring: from a timetable to a total-control school

Planning (24 primary, 74 any) is carried mainly by methods and planner apps, in eight forms.

**Patterns.** *Backward plans from the exam date* (#343, BK) set coverage and revision rounds, as in IASbaba's 60-day Prelims plan #200 and topper books. *Capacity planners*: Shovel #112 compares hours needed with hours available (about $237 a year per one review, unconfirmed, [itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/best/study-planner-apps/)); Structured #113 is a day timeline with timer. *Vendor day-by-day plans*: Barbri and Themis bar-prep plans (BK); PrepScholar's diagnostic plan; Retain and Vaia scheduling toward the exam date. Here the learner's job shifts from planning to compliance. *Time-blocking and task methods*: timeboxing #352, Getting Things Done #355, the Eisenhower matrix #353, Ivy Lee's six tasks #354, "eat the frog" #357, carried by calendars #118, TickTick #114, Todoist #115 with "karma" points, MyStudyLife #111 and Notion #117. *WOOP*: Wish, Outcome, Obstacle (an inner obstacle), Plan as "If [obstacle], then I will…"; urgent problems take about 30 seconds; the app is free ([WOOP](https://woopmylife.org/en/practice)) and was translated into all 11 South African official languages in 2022 ([UCT](https://www.news.uct.ac.za/features/teachingandlearning/-article/2022-03-31-woop-goal-realisation-app-now-in-all-11-official-languages)). *Retrospective review*: exam wrappers #140, the Study Cycle #512, the CREAM checklist #505, metacognition programmes #311. *Study logs*: Studyplus (Japan) logs materials and time, draws graphs, shares logs with friends and can be used anonymously; it announced 10 million cumulative sign-ups in July 2025 ([ReseMom](https://resemom.jp/release/prtimes/20250701/149227.html)), and its school version lets teachers see logs ([atpress](https://www.atpress.ne.jp/news/38814)). YPT shows day, week and month records that darken with more study ([AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/ypt-study-group/1441909643?country=ca)). *Total-control timetables*: China's Hengshui model of minute-by-minute schedules and strict discipline, criticised as an "exam factory" (#404, BK).

**Design choices.** Who makes the plan (learner, vendor, diagnostic, institution); number of revision rounds; buffer days; who sees the log. A sourced description of red-amber-green revision timetables was not found.

**Pitfalls.** Over-control (Hengshui). "Planning instead of studying" is widely discussed but no source was found that measures it.

### LHL Learning how to learn: content, not software

Learning how to learn (54 primary, 56 any) is books, courses and creators; Part 2 found no app built around it. Its content mostly repackages retrieval, understanding and spacing.

**Patterns.** *MOOCs*: Oakley and Sejnowski's *Learning How to Learn* #286 (Coursera, since August 2014) covers focused and diffuse thinking, chunking, procrastination, recall, spacing, sleep and test-taking; free to audit, certificate paid. 197,000 joined the first run ([UCSD TDLC](https://tdlc.ucsd.edu/tdlc2/news_LHTL_MOOC.php)); later totals conflict across outlets, from about 1.1 million to more than 4 million ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Oakley); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/education/learning-learn-2041630)). A youth version launched in December 2018 ([ASU News](https://news.asu.edu/20190226-edplus-asu-partners-provide-universal-learning-techniques-youth)). Others include #287, OpenLearn #477, Athabasca's five-week course #478, Mindware #502, Think101x #476. *Books*: *Make It Stick* #247, *How We Learn* #249, Dehaene #250, Willingham #252, *A Mind for Numbers* #258, McGuire #266, *Ultralearning* #273, Cottrell #495, Jade Bowler #469. *Evidence digests*: Dunlosky et al. #236, IES guide #243, Deans for Impact #239, APA Top 20 #486, the EEF Toolkit #240, The Learning Scientists' free posters #237 ([learningscientists.org](https://www.learningscientists.org/books)), InnerDrive #485. *Creators*: Ali Abdaal #289 (whose US$995 academy teaches content creation, not studying), Thomas Frank #290, Elizabeth Filips #297 (BK), Mike Dee #481, Huberman's studying episode #295; paid courses from iCanStudy #288 and Scott Young #291. *School strategy teaching*: EEF metacognition (plan, monitor, evaluate) #311, McGuire's study cycle in university learning centres, Pólya's four steps #509. *Myth-busting*: *Urban Myths about Learning and Education* #268.

**Design choices.** Generic or exam-specific; free audit or paid certificate; length (a five-minute video or a five-week course); whether it ends with a personal plan or only knowledge.

**Pitfalls.** Watching study-method content instead of studying is widely discussed, but no source measuring it was found. LHL is English-first and Western-authored, though Oakley's MOOC has a Chinese version (#287).

### FOC Attention and distraction control: a ladder of commitment strength

Focus tools (21 primary, 57 any) are apps above all. They line up on one dial: how hard the commitment is to undo.

**Patterns.** *Pomodoro timers*: 25 minutes on, 5 off, a longer break after four (#351, BK); Focus To-Do #99, Pomofocus #100, Be Focused #102, Session #101 (starts with an intention, ends with a reflection), Studywithme.io #451, LifeAt #449. *Focus trees*: Forest grows a virtual tree that dies if the user leaves the app; coins unlock species or fund real trees through Trees for the Future, and a group mode lets several users plant together ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_(application))). Its price is reported both as about $59.99 a year and as $1.99 one-time, and real-tree totals conflict by an order of magnitude ([forestapp.cc](https://forestapp.cc)). Flora #94 adds group pledges; China's Tomato ToDo #429 has a "xueba mode" that locks other apps, plus online study rooms ([Woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/evaluating/3069778.html)). *Hard blockers*: Freedom #95 (about $8.99/month, $39.99/year, $199 lifetime; [toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/freedom/pricing)), Cold Turkey #96 with its "Frozen Turkey" full lockout (Pro about $39 one-time; [makerstack](https://makerstack.co/reviews/cold-turkey-blocker-review/)), Opal #98 (about $19.99/month or $99.99/year; [makerstack](https://makerstack.co/reviews/opal-review/)), LeechBlock NG #450 (free, by time or budget), and YPT's phone lock #103. *Friction apps*: one sec adds a short delay, a message and an option to dismiss before a chosen app opens; a field study co-authored by the app maker tested which parts mattered ([PNAS via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409); [one sec](https://one-sec.app/max-planck-study)). *Deep-work blocks* #358 with phone and sites blocked; phone-away rules appear inside study-room etiquette and Hengshui discipline. *Focus audio*: Brain.fm, Endel, Noisli #104 (subscriptions with vendor neuroscience claims, BK), noise colours #375, Tide #430 (China) with timer, nature sounds and breathing. *Books*: *Stolen Focus* #285.

**Design choices.** Commitment strength, from weakest to strongest: a dismissable pause (one sec), scheduled blocks (Freedom, Opal), a hard lockout (Cold Turkey), a symbolic loss (a dead tree), a social loss (a group tree, a grey avatar seen by friends). Also allow list or block list, schedule or on demand, and fixed or custom lengths.

**Pitfalls.** A longitudinal study of about 1,039 users recorded usage rising when people paused the friction ([Haliburton et al. 2024](https://researchgate.net/profile/Luke-Haliburton/publication/377774169_A_Longitudinal_In-the-Wild_Investigation_of_Design_Frictions_to_Prevent_Smartphone_Overuse/links/65b7d1311bed776ae313b600/A-Longitudinal-In-the-Wild-Investigation-of-Design-Frictions-to-Prevent-Smartphone-Overuse.pdf)). Vendor-run studies and inflated claims (one sec co-authorship, focus-audio claims, conflicting Forest tree counts).

**By region.** Western focus tools are standalone subscriptions; Chinese tools bundle lockout with online study rooms.

### HAB Motivation, habits and mindset: streaks, leagues and loss framing

Habit features (11 primary, 49 any) are mostly a secondary layer on apps, plus books.

**Patterns.** *Streaks*: the Streaks app #120 (BK) and Duolingo #25, whose streak resets at midnight in the account's time zone (from a low-quality guide, [unanswered.io](https://unanswered.io/guide/duolingo-reset-times-streaks-leagues-diamond-tournament)); streak-freeze rules were not verified. Daily check-ins appear in Shanbay #399 and Insights IAS's daily answer writing #198. *XP leagues*: Duolingo has 10 weekly leagues with Diamond at the top, starting every Sunday, grouping learners of similar habits and time zone, with promotion or demotion by effort, a three-round Diamond Tournament for the top 10, removal of illegitimate XP, and an opt-out through a private profile ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-leagues-leaderboards/)); league size (30) and cut-offs come only from conflicting third-party guides. Classroom games add speed points and in-game money (#437–#440). *Role-play and pet games*: Habitica #119 (BK, freemium) with parties; Finch #134 with a virtual pet. *Habit-design methods*: *Atomic Habits* #278 (habit stacking, two-minute rule, environment design; mechanics not re-checked), Matt D'Avella's 30-day experiments #296, *Feel-Good Productivity* #279. *Mindset modules*: "not yet" and process praise #373; the US National Study of Learning Mindsets delivered two 25-minute online sessions to more than 12,000 ninth graders, free to US and Canadian schools ([UT Austin](https://news.utexas.edu/2019/08/07/a-growth-mindset-intervention-can-change-students-grades-if-school-culture-is-supportive); [Northwestern IPR](https://ipr.northwestern.edu/news/2019/tipton-growth-mindset-study.html)); grit and success MOOCs #270, #294. *National reframing*: India's Pariksha Pe Charcha reframes exams as a festival and tells parents not to compare; 2026 registrations passed 4 crore ([News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/pariksha-pe-charcha-2026-breaks-records-with-over-4-crore-registrations/)).

**Design choices.** Reset rule (calendar day or 24 hours), forgiveness (freezes, repair), visibility; for mindset, a one-off online module or teacher-led culture change.

**Pitfalls.** Loss-framed mechanics (a broken streak, demotion, last place) are the common thread in reported pressure; forgiveness features are the design response. The mindset study's authors reported results depending on school culture, so delivery context is a design choice in its own right.

## Family 5: Other people and places — silence sold by the seat, company sold by the session

### SPC Study spaces and co-presence: the one idea whose main form is a paid place

Study spaces (16 primary, 30 any) are carried by places and websites. Seven forms differ on pricing unit, staffing, silence rules, camera use, group size and moderation.

**Patterns.** *Paid silent desks.* In China, 付费自习室 offer rows of cubicles with lockers, zones by light, noise and seat type, a bin at every seat and enforced silence, sold by hour, day or year at roughly 20–100 yuan a day; they started in Japan and Korea in the 1980s and reached mainland China in 2014, with about 5,000 by 2021 ([Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1015064/why-chinese-are-studying-solo%2C-together)). Later figures include about 400 yuan a month and an industry of "over 10 billion yuan a year", with exam candidates for postgraduate entry and the civil service as main users ([China Daily, Jan 2026](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202601/14/WS6967416da310d6866eb33b30.html)); these come from search summaries and are not comparable across cities and years. Korea's 독서실 and study cafés are almost all open 24/7, some kiosk-only (pick a seat at a touch-screen and scan a barcode); a day pass costs 10,000 won and a month 100,000 won in Gyeonggi in January 2026 ([Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/living/pacific_travel/after_hours/2026-01-15/study-cafe-south-korea-20349934.html)), and a fixed 24/7 desk about 150,000–200,000 won ([Cities Insider](https://citiesinsider.com/country/south-korea/daejeon/libraries-&-study-spaces/en)). Japan's Benkyo Café adds a chat lounge for adults, about ¥11,000 to join and from about ¥4,400 a month (Part 1). India's paid "libraries" sell a monthly seat with AC, Wi-Fi and a locker: about ₹3,500–5,000 for premium Delhi seats ([Bharatnotes](https://bharatnotes.com/faq/study-environment/study-library-options-delhi/)), ₹800–1,500 in Greater Noida ([Stanza Living](https://www.stanzaliving.com/blog/libraries-in-greater-noida-best-study-rooms-reading-halls-public-libraries-in-2026)), ₹2,000–4,500 in Gurgaon chains, and a non-profit ICAI reading room at ₹750 AC or ₹100 non-AC in 2019 ([WIRC-ICAI](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/library/ReadingRoom_FacilityNotice.pdf)); these are listings, not market data. *Exam towns*: Korea's Noryangjin and Sillim (#415, BK; shrank after the bar exam changed and lectures moved online) and India's Mukherjee Nagar, Old Rajinder Nagar, Karol Bagh, Kota, Prayagraj and Patna (#235). *Public reading rooms*: a Seoul district converted a youth reading room into a public study café ([Asia Economy](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2023061407434187157)). *One-to-one body doubling*: Focusmate #105 books 25-, 50- or 75-minute sessions with a matched partner (over 99% matched), with goals stated at the start, camera on, a check-in at the end, an optional Quiet Mode with text chat, a timeliness score that can freeze accounts for no-shows, a report button, and rules against selling; free for three sessions a week, Plus $8–12 a month ([Focusmate FAQ](https://www.focusmate.com/faq)). Its "85% feel less lonely" figure is a vendor survey with no published method. *Hosted group sessions*: Flow Club #109 (price conflicting) and Caveday #110 (facilitated "caves", price unverified). *Large virtual rooms*: StudyStream #106 (founder claimed 4M+ users in 2021; [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/06/studystream-gives-students-a-place-to-study-live-with-thousands-of-others/)), Study Together on Discord #107 (about 1 million members, leaderboards, donation-funded; [Discord](https://discord.com/servers/study-together--595999872222756885)). *Study-with-me videos*: hours of someone studying, often in Pomodoro blocks, which began in South Korea (공방) and spread to Japan, China, Vietnam and the West (#108, [SCMP](https://scmp.com/week-asia/lifestyle-culture/article/3121568/study-buddies-south-korean-youtubers-take-cram-sessions)); Bilibili's 自习室 livestreams were its top live category by hours in 2019 per its CEO (#428, [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/how-bilibili-kuaishou-douyin-are-fostering-chinese-youngsters-education)).

**Design choices.** Hourly, monthly or fixed seat; staffed or kiosk; silence rules; zoning; 24-hour access; for online forms, camera required or optional, one-to-one or thousands, scheduled or drop-in, strangers or friends, talk rules and moderation. Monthly seat prices converge on a small share of living costs across countries.

**Pitfalls.** Physical safety: on 27 July 2024 three aspirants drowned in the flooded basement library of a Delhi coaching centre; 13 centres were sealed and a CBI probe ordered ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/india/story/delhi-rains-3-upsc-aspirants-die-after-coaching-centres-basement-gets-flooded-in-rajinder-nagar-probe-ordered-439135-2024-07-28); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/basement-deaths-mcd-seals-13-illegal-coaching-centres-in-old-rajinder-nagar)); Part 1 noted many Indian reading rooms are in basements. Distress in coaching hubs is recorded (#235). Chinese coverage links study rooms to "involution", over-exhaustion and avoidance of job pressure, and over half of surveyed users found them too expensive ([Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1011458/Private%20Study%20Rooms%20Emerge%20as%20Refuge%20for%20Examinees)). Quality varies; guides advise visiting before paying a month. Online body doubling needs rules against no-shows and misuse.

### GRP Study groups and teaching others: structured methods offline, broadcast channels online

Group study (7 primary, 26 any) runs through classroom methods and messaging communities.

**Patterns.** *Structured classroom methods*: peer instruction #360 (concept question, vote, discuss, revote; BK), reciprocal teaching #361 (rotating roles to predict, question, clarify, summarise; BK), jigsaw #511 (each member masters one part and teaches it). *Teach-back* #359 and the Feynman imagined beginner. *Informal groups*: Part 1 noted quizzing each other differs from chatting (BK); Sleepy Classes #193 promotes groups. *Shared material libraries*: Anki shared decks, AnKing #2, Quizlet and Cram.com sets, Clearnote #433. *Messaging and forums*: Indian Telegram channels #231 carry current affairs, daily quizzes, PDFs and mentoring, many run by coaching firms; a guide advises joining five to eight channels at most ([Bharatnotes](https://bharatnotes.com/faq/coaching/telegram-groups-and-study-communities/)); r/UPSC and r/GetStudying #233; peer Q&A on IndiaBIX #447, ForumIAS #199 and Brainly #62. *Mentor-led cohorts*: CivilsDaily's free mentor-led Telegram groups #194 ([CivilsDaily](https://www.civilsdaily.com/interested-in-group-studies-with-peers-for-upsc-cse-want-to-get-an-offline-edge-in-an-online-platform-then-join-the-free-civilsdaily-mentor-led-telegram-channel-link-inside/)), Insights IAS peer review #198, Busuu corrections #435; Habitica parties and Flora pledges are game versions.

**Design choices.** Group size, role rotation, interdependence (jigsaw needs everyone), broadcast channel or group chat, who moderates, anonymity.

**Pitfalls.** Telegram study channels carry heavy piracy and "leaked paper" scams and were linked to the 2024 NEET and UGC-NET leaks ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1069714/how-a-messaging-app-that-helps-students-prepare-for-exams-enabled-paper-leaks)). Reddit is better moderated but can amplify negativity (#233). Unstructured groups drift.

**By region.** India's group layer is Telegram and Reddit; the Western equivalent is Discord servers; Japan's is shared handwritten notes.

### CMP Public comparison and rankings: almost always a feature, rarely a product

Comparison (2 primary, 11 any) is bolted onto timers, games and mock series.

**Patterns.** *Study-hour rankings*: YPT (Korea, free with ads) shows friends' live status and ranks users within a study category in real time; in group view an active user's avatar turns orange, an inactive one stays grey at 00:00:00, and avatars sweat or catch fire with longer study ([AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/ypt-study-group/1441909643?country=ca); [Raffles Press](https://rafflespress.com/2025/02/13/yawns-pains-and-tears-on-ypt/)). Studyplus timelines encourage rather than rank. Study Together has Discord leaderboards. *XP leagues*: Duolingo (above). *Live quiz leaderboards*: Kahoot! #438, Wayground #437. *National mock ranks*: Fenbi #392 (listed in Hong Kong in January 2023), CAT mock percentiles #186, Oliveboard percentiles #181 (8 million users in 2021, [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/edtech-startup-oliveboard-secures-inr-23-cr-from-ian-fund/)), Kawaijuku and Sundai pass-likelihood grades #422. *Counter-message*: Pariksha Pe Charcha tells parents not to compare (#150).

**Design choices.** What is counted (timer minutes, XP, marks, percentile); against whom (friends, category, a matched cohort, the nation); the reset period (daily, weekly, per mock); opt-out (Duolingo's private profile); anti-cheat (Duolingo removes illegitimate XP).

**Pitfalls.** Some YPT users run the timer outside study, and one group champion logged 321 hours in a month; a Singapore student publication describes obsessive leaderboard checking, fear of last place, skipped meals and lost sleep, and advises muting toxic members ([Raffles Press](https://rafflespress.com/2025/02/13/yawns-pains-and-tears-on-ypt/), student opinion). Any metric that is public and timer-based invites inflation; the known mitigations are anti-cheat removal and making it private or friends-only.

## Family 6: Body and mind — public services lead, apps follow

### LIF Body basics: advice and methods, rarely a product

Body basics (9 primary, 20 any) are carried as advice.

**Patterns.** *Pre-exam sleep rules*: stop studying at a set time and sleep 7–9 hours the night before, and sleep after a study day ([Go Ask Alice](https://goaskalice.columbia.edu/answered-questions/how-do-i-get-most-out-all-nighter); [Sleep Education, AASM](https://sleepeducation.org/final-exams-is-sleep-the-secret-to-better-grades/)). *Naps*: keep them short, since naps over about 30 minutes can cause grogginess (Go Ask Alice); Part 1 records 10–90 minutes as the range discussed (#366, BK). *Caffeine timing*: stop about six hours before bed ([Red & Black](https://www.redandblack.com/variety/snoozing-in-danger-during-finals-week/article_16ec6e86-3e68-11e2-8522-001a4bcf6878.html), student paper quoting an expert); the trial behind the figure was found only through a vendor blog and is unverified. Commercial versions are energy and "smart" drinks. *Public tip pages*: NHS trust exam-stress pages bundle sleep, exercise, breaks, timetables, less caffeine and less social media ([WY Healthier Together](https://wyhealthiertogether.nhs.uk/health-young-people/exam-pressure)); breakfast #369 and hydration #371 are BK rows. *Exercise breaks* #368 appear inside wellbeing apps rather than as products. *Sleep apps*: Sleep Cycle, BetterSleep and Rise #135, and sleep content in Headspace #153 and Calm #154; none is exam-specific. *Targeted memory reactivation* #367 is a lab technique that replays a cue from learning during sleep ([Hu et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7144680)); it is different from "sleep learning" of new material (#388, a quick fix).

**Design choices.** Timing (the night before, after learning, hours before bed) and dose (hours of sleep, nap length). Cost: free. No source was found on how Indian coaching institutes build sleep or exercise into schedules.

### ANX Exam nerves and mental wellbeing: five layers from a 10-minute script to a helpline

Exam-nerves support (30 primary, 38 any) is the most public-sector idea. It comes in five layers.

**Patterns.** *Brief pre-exam scripts.* Expressive writing #136 (Ramirez and Beilock, *Science*, 2011): just before the test, students write for 10 minutes about their thoughts and feelings on the exam; a teacher delivers it in class and no one reads the writing ([UChicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/writing-about-worries-eases-anxiety-and-improves-test-performance)); some colleges reprint it as a handout ([Normandale](https://www.normandale.edu/_files/documents/osd/WritingAboutAnxietyImprovesTestScores.pdf)). Arousal reappraisal #137 is a few sentences read just before the test saying a racing heart can help ([Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/4214916)). Values self-affirmation #138 is about 15 minutes of writing about a personal value at the start of term or before a stressful test ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5403146)). *In-the-moment calming*: box breathing #514 (in, hold, out, hold for four counts each; [Children's Health Queensland](https://www.childrens.health.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0024/336318/grounding-strategies-for-coping-with-stress-adults.pdf)), 5-4-3-2-1 grounding #515, progressive muscle relaxation #513, guided imagery #516. Versions differ: one university handout uses 4-in, 2-hold, 4-out breaths, then grounding, then a referral line to counselling ([UND](https://und.edu/academics/services/academic-support/_files/docs/destress-less-than-two-min.pdf)). Delivery runs from printable handouts to podcasts ([Warwick](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/wss/topics/grounding-technique-podcast/)), live sessions ([ARU](https://counsellingandwellbeing.anglia.ac.uk/event/4158283)), workshop toolkits ([York](https://counselling.students.yorku.ca/sites/default/files/2020-04/SCD%20Skills%20Workshop%20Toolkit.pdf)) and breathing features in apps. *Self-help apps*: Headspace #153 (student rate reported at $9.99 a year against a $69.99 list price, [SheerID](https://shop.sheerid.com/offers/headspace-discounted-subscription-for-students/)), Calm #154, Smiling Mind #160 (free, Australia), .b's nine teacher-led lessons #161 (UK), Wysa #155 (AI chatbot with CBT and DBT techniques plus optional human coach, sold to the NHS and employers), Youper #156, MindShift CBT #157 (free, Anxiety Canada; support ended 31 March 2025, [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/mindshift-cbt-anxiety-relief/id634684825)), Finch #134. *Group and one-to-one support*: university exam-anxiety workshops #159 of one to three sessions; Kooth #158 (UK, NHS-commissioned anonymous counselling and peer community, BK). *Helplines and national programmes*: India's Tele-MANAS #149 is dialled on 14416 or 1-800-891-4416, routes by language through an IVR menu to a counsellor in a state cell, and escalates to specialists in a second tier; it runs 24x7 in 20 languages depending on what each state opts for ([Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/health/mental-health/tele-manas); [PIB backgrounder](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2024/oct/doc20241013415701.pdf)). NTA promoted it before NEET 2026 and CBSE told schools to display it (Part 1). MANODARPAN #151 and CBSE seasonal counselling #152 add to it. Pariksha Pe Charcha #150 selects participants through a MyGov quiz (1 December 2025 to 11 January 2026 for the 2026 edition), with NCERT shortlisting questions for the live show and an *Exam Warriors* book kit ([PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2000440); [CBSE circular](https://www.cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2024/106_Circular_2024.pdf)). The UK has DfE, NHS, Mind, YoungMinds, UCAS and youth-written tip pages (#143–#148).

**Design choices.** Timing (immediately before the test for scripts; during the exam for breathing; ongoing for apps); who delivers (teacher, app, counsellor, state); and the escalation path from self-help to crisis care.

**Pitfalls.** In panel data on 93 Android mental-health apps, median 30-day retention was 3.3% ([Baumel et al. 2019](https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567)). Apps close (MindShift). Regulatory status can be overstated: Wysa's FDA "Breakthrough Device" designation covers chronic pain with depression and anxiety and is not an approval ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220512005084/en/Wysa-Receives-FDA-Breakthrough-Device-Designation-for-AI-led-Mental-Health-Conversational-Agent)). A published critique warned that pre-exam writing may not suit calm students ([Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/write-stuff-test-anxiety)). Helpline language coverage depends on each state, and source counts of Tele-MANAS cells differ ([Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/explained-how-tele-manas-is-shaping-tele-mental-healthcare-in-india/)). Well-designed implementations chain the layers: handout, drop-in, counselling; Tier 1, Tier 2.

**By region.** India: state-led, phone-based, multilingual, with 2024 coaching-centre guidelines requiring counselling and forbidding rank guarantees ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/cannot-enrol-students-below-16-years-no-misleading-promises-moe-guidelines-for-coaching-centres/amp)). UK: charity, NHS and government pages, NHS-commissioned online counselling and university sessions. Australia and Canada: free non-profit apps. US: commercial subscriptions with student discounts. No equivalent Japanese or Korean public programme was found.

## Family 7: Shortcuts, handovers and defaults — who does the work, and what the tool holds back

### GEN AI-made study material: fully automatic, learner-in-the-loop or instructor-checked

AI-made material (17 primary, 23 any) is apps and websites only, and the products are close to interchangeable across countries.

**Patterns.** *Upload, get cards or a quiz*: the learner uploads PDFs, slides, photos, handwriting or a video link; the AI drafts cards or MCQs, often inside a spaced schedule. Vaia/StudySmarter #21, Gizmo #23, Retain #26, Revisely #27, Jungle AI #29, StudyCards AI #30 (exports to Anki), Mindomax #32, Revprep #46 (India), Turbo AI #55 (5 million users by October 2025, [TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2025/10/23/20-year-old-dropouts-built-ai-notetaker-turbo-ai-to-5-million-users)). Retain drafts editable Q&A, cloze and image-occlusion cards with a source reference on each ([Retain](https://www.retain.cards/)). *Learner-edited AI cards inside a scheduler*: Noji #7, RemNote #8, Rember #36, Avorio #37, Knowt #22. *Source-grounded notebooks*: NotebookLM #52 builds output only from uploaded files, with citations; from September 2025 it makes flashcards and quizzes with settings for number (Fewer, Standard, More), difficulty, language and a focus prompt, an "Explain" button for wrong answers, and two-host audio overviews ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/09/08/notebooklm-flashcards-quizzes/); [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/google-labs/notebooklm-student-features/)); users can uncheck sources to narrow output ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/11/06/notebooklm-app-flashcards-quizzes/)). StudyFetch #54 is similar. *Lecture-to-notes and summaries*: Coconote #57, Mindgrasp #56, Scholarly #31. *Browser extensions*: Wisdolia #28 (status unclear). *AI-written exam questions*: Neural Consult #79 for USMLE. *Instructor-checked AI cards*: at Northeastern, an instructor generated review flashcards with Claude and teaching assistants checked them for errors, hallucinations and course framing before students saw them ([Northeastern CATLR](https://learning.northeastern.edu/ai-gallery-post-ai-generated-flashcards-for-end-of-semester-exam-review/)).

**Design choices.** The main axis is who makes and who checks: fully automatic, learner-edited, or instructor-checked. Grounding in the learner's own files is the second. Most are freemium with unpublished tiers.

**Pitfalls.** In a radiology study, 20 medical professionals reviewing 1,284 AI-made flashcards found errors that had passed an automated AI check ([Asan Medical Center](https://news-en.amc.seoul.kr/news/eng/detail.do?cntId=3283)); in a two-course study, most students reported never spotting incorrect content, which the authors treat as a risk ([arXiv 2508.11704](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.11704)); one practitioner found two errors in the first 100 AI-made Anki cards ([De Novo](https://denovo.substack.com/p/ai-generated-anki-cards-are-not-to), anecdote). Fully automatic tools also remove the card-making step. Schedulers in Retain, Vaia, Gizmo and Quizlet Learn are unpublished. No regulator action on card errors was found; quality control is left to the learner or instructor.

### OFF Answer lookup and offloading: four kinds of guardrail

Offloading (12 primary, 16 any) is apps and websites, plus the guardrails built against them.

**Patterns.** *Photo solvers*: photograph a problem, and OCR plus a solver returns the answer, often with steps. Photomath #60 (Google) gives basic steps free; Plus (about $9.99 a month or $69.99 a year) adds animated tutorials and textbook solutions ([Photomath help](https://support.google.com/photomath/answer/14330572?hl=bg)). Gauth #61's prices vary by region and source ($7.99–11.99 a month reported) and include live tutors ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1542571008)). QANDA #407 (Korea) has about 90 million registered users, 90% outside Korea ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QANDA)). Zuoyebang #87, Xueba Jun #405 (collapsed December 2020), Google Socratic #58 (folded into Lens in 2025) and Mathway #488 (steps paywalled) complete the set. *Answer engines*: Wolfram Alpha #441, Symbolab #442. *Human or crowd answers*: Chegg #63 (subscribers down 31% to 3.2 million in Q1 2025, then large staff cuts; [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/chegg-slashes-22-of-workforce-amid-ai-disruption-in-edtech/); [Tech.co](https://tech.co/news/chegg-fires-half-workforce-new-realities-ai)) and Brainly #62. *General chatbots doing the work*: not a catalogue row, but the reason answer services lost users. One randomised study of about 1,000 Turkish high-school maths students compared an unrestricted GPT-4 chat with a tutor version that had guardrails ([Bastani et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4895486); [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education)); its results are weighed in Part 4.

The guardrails come in four types. *Answer-withholding modes*: ChatGPT Study Mode, Gemini Guided Learning, Claude learning mode, Khanmigo (see TUT); the user can usually switch them off. *Process recording*: Turnitin Clarity #89 records drafting (pastes, deletions, typing), gives the teacher a playback and a typed-versus-pasted timeline, and lets the teacher enable an AI assistant with a set policy and visible chat log ([UCLA](https://dts.ucla.edu/initiatives/bruin-learn-center-excellence/academic-tech-tools/turnitin-clarity); [Turnitin guide](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/36982699236877)); sources differ on its scope ([UF](https://elearning.ufl.edu/instructor-help/teaching-and-learning-tools/tool-finder/items/turnitin-clarity.html)). Grammarly Authorship #90 and Writable #91 are similar. *Recall checks*: Sparx Maths #67 gives each question a bookwork code; at intervals it asks for the answer to a recent question by code alone, and a failed check means redoing the question; consistent passers get fewer checks, and schools can change the setting ([Sparx support](https://support.sparxmaths.com/en/articles/342329-understanding-bookwork-checks)). *Regulation*: in December 2021 China's Ministry of Education suspended filing and review of photo homework-search apps for primary and middle school, citing dependence and weakened independent thinking ([TechNode](https://technode.com/2021/12/14/chinese-regulators-suspend-homework-answer-search-apps/); [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1009216/china-blocks-apps-helping-students-with-their-homework-)); sources differ on whether it was a removal or a suspension of approvals, and no end date was found.

**Design choices.** What is withheld (answer, steps, or nothing) and who can switch the guardrail off. Only recall checks and regulation are hard for the student to bypass.

**Pitfalls.** Photo solvers are "not always correct", according to a maths specialist (Part 1, [UBC ETEC523](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec523/2023/09/24/photomath-an-ongoing-debate/)). Clarity does not monitor outside its own space, and Turnitin admits AI detectors alone are unreliable (Part 1). A Trustpilot reviewer says Sparx checks can be passed without showing working ([Trustpilot](https://de.trustpilot.com/review/maths.sparx-learning.com), anecdote).

**By region.** Offloading is strongest and most regulated in East Asia; guardrails in the UK and US are institutional (school licences, university tools); no Indian regulation was found.

### QFX Quick fixes and popular beliefs: the product is often harmless, the claim is the issue

Quick fixes (22 primary, 26 any) come through subscriptions, physical products, paid courses and free beliefs.

**Patterns.** *Brain-training subscriptions*: daily mini-games with scores marketed as improving school or work performance: Lumosity #162, Elevate #163, Peak #164, HAPPYneuron #165. *Study drugs and supplements*: off-label prescription drugs (modafinil "anti-sleep pills" #167, ADHD stimulants #168) and herbal or "smart drink" products (#169: brahmi and Shankhpushpi syrups, racetams, L-theanine with caffeine; #166), sold through pharmacies, informal sellers and online shops; modafinil spread beyond Indian medical colleges ([Caravan](https://caravanmagazine.in/reportage/modafinil-tightening-hold-over-students)). *Speed reading*: eye-movement training or apps that flash one word at a time (#386), sold as courses (Kwik Learning #293; *Limitless* #275) ([Rayner et al. 2016](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/speed_reading.html)). *"Brain" audio*: binaural beats #376, functional music #104, Mozart-effect products #389, subliminal audio #387. *Sleep learning* #388: playing new material during sleep. *Learning-style labels* #380: a questionnaire (VARK, VAK, Honey and Mumford, Kolb) labels the learner and study is "matched" to the label ([Pashler et al. 2008](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1765/)). *Brain-type beliefs*: Brain Gym #383, left/right-brain types #385, "10% of the brain" #384, photographic-memory training #390 (mostly BK). *Small tricks*: chewing gum about five minutes before a test #377 ([PubMed 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30611764/)). *Debunking*: *Urban Myths about Learning and Education* #268.

**Regulator actions.** The US FTC settled with Lumosity in January 2016 ($2 million redress, $50 million judgment suspended) over claims that the games improve school and work performance ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges)). HAPPYneuron modified or dropped claims after a US National Advertising Division inquiry ([BBB National Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media-center/dd/advertising-claims-for-happyneuron-brain-training-method-modified-or-permanently-discontinued-following-nad-inquiry)). South Africa's advertising regulator ordered "Smart Kids Brain Boost" claims withdrawn in 2012 ([IOL](https://iol.co.za/lifestyle/2012-05-16-holford-product-claims-fall-foul-of-asa/)). The UK MHRA warned freshers in 2016 against buying modafinil online ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/freshers-warned-to-be-smart-and-avoid-modafinil)) and in 2020 warned of a birth-defect risk in pregnancy ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/sleep-disorder-drug-modafinil-linked-to-increased-risk-of-birth-defects-and-also-to-reduced-effectiveness-of-contraception)). In India, a Class 10 student who took anti-sleep pills was admitted to intensive care ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/class-10-student-takes-anti-sleep-pills-to-stay-awake-lands-in-icu-doctors-issue-warning)).

**By region.** Harms look financial in the US (subscription brain games) and physical in India (unprescribed wakefulness drugs, memory syrups). No Indian regulator action on these products was searched.

### BAS Default habits: what the tool makes easy

Default habits (6 primary, 7 any) are mostly unsold; they show up as defaults inside tools and plans.

**Patterns.** *Rereading* #315 is the default "read it again" plan; text-to-speech makes it rereading by ear (Speechify #493, NaturalReader #494). *Highlighting* #316 is built into every e-reader and PDF tool; Kindle's Popular Highlights launched in 2010, on by default, built from readers' synced highlights, and a 2022 report said it had to be switched off per book ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/how-to-turn-off-popular-highlights-amazon-kindle); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/consumer/amazon-peeking-over-kindle-users-shoulders-flna6C10406354)). Readwise #14 resurfaces highlights for rereading. *Highlights as AI input*: RemNote turns PDF highlights into cards (#8), converting a default habit into a spacing input. *Cramming and all-nighters* #381, often paired with caffeine and, at the extreme, modafinil. *Multitasking* #382, addressed by focus tools rather than by products of its own.

**Pitfalls.** Rereading builds familiarity without retrieval. Default highlighting also raised privacy concerns, since Kindle compiled highlights on Amazon's servers by default ([Errata Security](https://blog.erratasec.com/2010/05/popular-highlights-on-amazon-kindle.html)).

## The same pitfalls recur across families

Five pitfalls cut across the whole map. The first is **effort that is quietly removed**. The activity feels like learning while the learner's own thinking is skipped: a fluent lecture, a recognition-only quiz, a supplied picture or question, a copied topper answer, an AI-made card, a photo solver's full steps, a highlight. Product responses mirror this. They insert questions into videos, hold back the answer, ask for an attempt before showing the model, add a recall check, or have the learner edit the AI draft. Most of these responses can be switched off by the student. Recall checks (Sparx) and regulation (China 2021) are the exceptions.

The second is **numbers with false precision**. AI answer graders publish no accuracy figures. Percentile, percent correct and tutor-mode scores get confused. Pass-likelihood bands are probabilities, not promises. Adaptive schedulers are unpublished. Vendor outcome claims are common: "300% better retention", "twice as efficient", "85% feel less lonely". In several cases the vendor itself admits the limits, such as an AI grader being "consistently wrong in the same direction" or the app maker co-authoring the study of its own app.

The third is **public, timer-based metrics invite inflation and pressure**. Study-hour rankings get gamed. Leaderboards bring fear of last place. Loss-framed mechanics (a dead tree, a broken streak, demotion) are the common thread in reported anxiety. Designers answer with anti-cheat removal, private or friends-only views, streak freezes, Quiet Mode, and mute or block.

The fourth is **loops that do not close**. Error logs have no return trigger. Mock series put the rank before the review. Q-banks stop at the explanation. Review queues pile up until a "catch up" feature is bolted on. Only East Asian mistake-notebook tools and a few teacher routines, such as handing exam wrappers back before the next exam, close the loop by design.

The fifth is **risks outside the software**. Basement reading rooms flood. Telegram channels carry piracy and paper-leak scams. Study drugs are bought online. Coaching firms market results misleadingly and have been fined. Mental-health apps close, and many users leave within a month. These risks are physical, regulatory or commercial, and app design alone does not address them.

## Region and product type predict the form an idea takes

**Product type sets the division of labour.** Apps automate timing, scoring, nudges and, increasingly, card-writing, and they dominate spacing, notes, focus, AI-made material and offloading. Books carry worked examples, problem-type maps, interleaving and targeting, because these need no software and last for decades: 수학의 정석 dates from 1966 and Akahon from 1954. Courses and coaching sell three things: a calendar (revision cycles, test series), a teacher, and the questions. School-licensed tools (ALEKS, Sparx, IXL, Khan Academy for classes) enforce a path, because teachers want completion data. Adult self-paid products (UWorld, AMBOSS, Indian test series) give analytics and leave the choice to the learner. Places sell silence, company and supervision by the seat or by the month. Named methods carry elaboration, learning how to learn and most exam-nerves techniques, and cost nothing. Public bodies are major providers only for exam nerves, body basics, free lectures and official simulators (Tele-MANAS, NHS pages, EBSi, SATHEE, NTA Abhyas).

**East Asia turns habits into products and places.** Japan has the red sheet, colour-coded problem-type books, per-university past-paper books, correspondence hand-marking, national mocks with per-school pass-likelihood grades, Studyplus's gentle social logs and Toshin's video-in-a-centre model. Korea has *ilta* star lecturers, unlimited lecture passes, the free EBS layer whose value comes from exam linkage, 24/7 kiosk study cafés, YPT's real-time rankings and the study-with-me format. China has star-teacher live classes (restricted for school-age learners since 2021), drill culture and topic-sorted books, mistake-notebook apps and printers, fortnightly ranked mock contests, paid study rooms, Bilibili study livestreams, the largest photo-search companies and the strongest regulation of them. East Asian products tend to bundle several ideas into one app (YPT combines focus, co-presence, comparison and logging).

**India runs exam prep through hubs, series and a free state layer.** Its distinct forms are coaching districts and monthly-fee reading rooms, the "few standard sources, 3–5 revisions" canon, monthly current-affairs digests, mass mock-test series with all-India ranks, free-to-paid YouTube funnels, Telegram communities, topper copies for essay exams, AI answer graders for civil-service Mains (since about 2024), and a large free state layer: SATHEE in 12 languages, SWAYAM, NPTEL, DIKSHA, NTA Abhyas (with Hindi from December 2025), Tele-MANAS in 20 languages and Pariksha Pe Charcha with its explicit message against comparison. Paid civil-service products compete on feedback and structure because the free tier is so large.

**The US and UK sell ideas separately, as subscriptions and school licences.** They have school-licensed mastery software, high-stakes medical Q-banks with rich explanations, official adaptive test simulators, purpose-built AI tutors, standalone focus subscriptions (Freedom, Opal, Cold Turkey), remote co-working (Focusmate, Flow Club), learning-science books and creators, and NHS and charity exam-stress guidance. UK schools add blurting, 2-3-5-7 and Sparx's recall checks. Western consumer apps frame comparison as a game with an opt-out (Duolingo leagues). In East Asia and India, comparison is built into the national exam system through mock ranks.

**Some layers are now global.** The FSRS scheduler, Socratic chatbot modes and upload-and-generate AI tools work the same way in every country. Competition has moved to content, language and who checks the output.

## Conclusion

Mapping implementation rather than ideas shows that the market differs less on what it believes and more on who it makes do the work. The same idea can sit at opposite ends of a few dials. On authorship, the learner can make the card or AI can. On visibility, the study log can be private or ranked against the nation. On control, the learner can choose the topic or a prerequisite graph can. On commitment, a focus tool can be dismissable or locked. On disclosure, the answer can be shown at once or held back. These dials are mostly set by product type and local exam culture rather than by any stated theory of learning. That means two products built on the "same idea" can ask very different things of the learner, so Part 4 will need to weigh evidence for specific settings of these dials, not for idea labels alone.

Three features of the map are worth carrying forward. First, the parts of the market that are cheapest to copy (open schedulers, Socratic chat modes, upload-and-generate AI) have become commodities within two years, so differentiation now sits in exam-specific content, language coverage, human checking and closing the feedback loop. Second, the loop-closing designs are rare and regional: mistake-notebook reprinting, wrappers handed back before the next exam, recall checks, two-in-a-row rules. Third, several of the most consequential implementations are not apps at all. They are public helplines, setter-linked state workbooks, official simulators and the physical safety of rooms where people study. Any account of "what exam tools do" that looks only at software misses them.
