My recommendation

Concept A, "Study with Chotu". Delete the tour guide and the flashlight. Let the visitor work in peace, with Chotu at the edge of the screen doing his real job: a hint in his voice when they get stuck, never a word about the interface. Everything they work out gets written onto their own notebook page, which they keep by joining. Plus is mentioned once, at the marking desk, where wanting it is highest.

The demo stops describing the product and becomes the product. That is where the class comes from.

The one root cause

Every guide we've built so far talks about Aspiran. A 20-year-old in Gaya or Guntur with ₹4,000 to their name isn't sold by features being pointed at. They're sold when, ten minutes in, they think: "I understood something I've been stuck on, and someone was there when I got stuck." The demo has the material for that: the Governor's desk and the GST vote are genuinely good. The guide around them is the problem.

Four different ways to guide and sell

Four ideas, none of them a tooltip tour

Each one aims at a different feeling. All of them keep the 10 chapters' content as it is; they change who speaks, when, and where the ask happens. All follow the rules: no "AI" labels, no timers, streaks or public scores, every piece ends, reduced motion respected, Chotu never comments on feelings, ranks or results.

A · Recommended

Study with Chotu

The demo becomes ten minutes of studying with the buddy they'd actually get. Chotu speaks only to help, the work fills a page that's theirs, and the ask is "keep your page".

The feeling
"I'm not doing this alone, and I'm better at this than I thought." Relief, and a little pride that they earned.
The connection
Chotu shows up only at the moment they're stuck, with a nudge, not the answer. That's what a good senior does, and it's rare in their lives. The page fills with their discoveries in handwriting, so the demo leaves them holding something.
Where the sell is
Three quiet places. (1) The hint itself is the product: Chotu is in Free. (2) At the marking desk, one line: "Want your own page marked like this? Plus does it for every answer, ₹3,999 a year." (3) At the end: "Keep your page" (Join free). No feature wall. It won't feel pushy because every ask points at something they already made or already wanted.
Chotu and voice
Chotu is the whole idea. He sits at the edge of the work, steam rising, silent. He speaks (voice on after the first click, as you decided) only for a hint after they stall or miss, plus one hello and one goodbye. About 3 lines a chapter, never about the screen, never praise or judgement.
Borrows from
Mario 1-1 and Portal (teach by doing, never by a text box). Duolingo's Duo moments (a character who shows up briefly, with personality, then leaves). Apple keynotes for the one-line title card at each chapter's end.
Cost
About 2 days. Mostly writing hints (about 30 lines, English and Hindi), recording them (under $1), a small hint engine, the notebook page, and a shorter finish.

Why A

The reasons, in plain words

  • It sells what they'll actually get. A tour describes Aspiran. In A, the visitor uses Chotu for ten minutes, and Chotu is in the free plan. Nothing is promised that the app doesn't do on day one.
  • It answers "no connect". The only voice in the demo speaks when the visitor needs help, and stays quiet when they don't. That's what makes someone feel looked after, and it can't be done by narrating features.
  • It answers "no class". Less on screen, not more. No dim-and-spotlight, no side column, no dock over the buttons on a phone. One quiet title line at each chapter's end is the only flourish.
  • The ask feels fair. "Keep your page" is about something they made. Plus is mentioned once, next to a marked page they've just seen, with the price stated straight.
  • It's the cheapest to try properly. About 2 days, and it reuses what exists: the chapters, Chotu's voice, the recording script. B's title card is folded in for free.
  • C and D can come later. Two quick questions at the start (C) and "how others answered" (D) both fit into A once there's traffic to justify them.
