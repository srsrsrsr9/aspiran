### 2d. Does our "what you do to material" vs "how you manage learning" split match?

Partly. The research standard is a **three-way** split, not two-way: **cognitive** strategies (rehearse, elaborate, organise), **metacognitive** strategies (plan, monitor, evaluate) and **resource management** (time, environment, effort, other people). MSLQ and Donker both use it. Our families line up like this:

- Remembering, Understanding (ELA, NOT, MNE), Practising (MIX, RET) → cognitive.
- Steering your own study (PLN, TGT, LHL), FBK → metacognitive.
- Other people and places, FOC, HAB, Body and mind → resource management and motivation.

What does **not** fit any framework: TCH, TUT, EXM (as a product), GEN, OFF, QFX, CMP and TST. These are *sources* of help or *conditions*, not learner strategies. The frameworks classify what a learner does; our list classifies what products are built around. That difference is expected and should be stated in Part 2, not "fixed".

## 5. Recommended changes to the Part 2 taxonomy

**Keep the 26 codes as they are for now.** The evidence supports adding named variants and sharpening definitions, not adding or merging codes. The catalogue has no entries yet for most of the proposed new ideas, and Part 2 is built on the catalogue.

1. **Add named variants (no new codes):**
   - **HAB** += *commitment devices*: putting money or reputation at stake on a study goal (Beeminder, stickK).
   - **TST** += two sub-strands, reported and graded separately in Parts 3–4: (a) *rehearsal under exam conditions*, including practical and oral exams (OSCE, Geeky Medics) and *matching study conditions to test conditions*; (b) *test-taking strategy*: answer elimination (POE), test-wiseness, guessing under negative marking, time triage (three-pass).
   - **PLN** += *confidence calibration*: judging how well you know each item (certainty-based marking, confidence ratings). Cross-reference FBK. This fills the "monitoring / self-judgment" category every framework has.
   - **TGT** += *examiner reports*: reading what examiners say candidates got wrong.
   - **RET / BAS** += note *rehearsal aloud* (早读) and *overlearning* (drilling past first mastery) as variants, not ideas.
2. **Sharpen two definitions:**
   - **LHL**: "learning which strategies exist and when to use them (metacognitive knowledge)". This matches Donker and the EEF and answers the "circular" objection.
   - **HAB**: say it includes effort regulation, self-efficacy and task value (MSLQ, Zimmerman).
3. **Add a short "how this maps to research taxonomies" note** to Part 2, using Section 2b, and state plainly that our list classifies *what products are built around*, so it includes sources of help (TCH, TUT, GEN, OFF) that learner-strategy taxonomies leave out. List help seeking and transfer as research categories with no product built around them.
4. **Do not adopt:** the ELA split, the BAS+QFX merge, the LHL reclassification. **Keep CMP** separate for now; revisit after Part 4.
5. **Add 16 catalogue entries** (Section 4a: 7 from H1 and 9 from G), each with a primary code, and the 6 link improvements in 4b.
6. **Promotion rule for later:** if a variant gets five or more primary entries in a future catalogue round (commitment devices and test-taking strategy are the likeliest), promote it to its own code.

## 6. Research findings for the newly accepted ideas (for Part 4 grading)

Grades use the scale in `research_notes/Exam success evidence ranking/grading_scale.md`. They are **provisional**: most numbers below were read from abstracts or from papers citing the study.

### Commitment devices (HAB variant)

- **Giné, Karlan & Zinman (2010)**, "Put your money where your butt is", *American Economic Journal: Applied Economics*. Randomised field trial with about 2,000 smokers in the Philippines. 11% of those offered the deposit contract took it up; those offered it were 3 percentage points more likely to pass a 6-month urine test, and the effect held at a surprise 12-month test. Not a study outcome. Authors disclose ties to stickK. ([IPA PDF](https://poverty-action.org/sites/default/files/publications/put_your_money_where_your_butt_is.pdf); [J-PAL summary](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/print/pdf/node/1468))
- **Himmler, Jäckle & Weinschenk (2019)**, "Soft commitments, reminders, and academic performance", *AEJ: Applied Economics* 11(2), 114–42. Field experiment with university students: offering a non-binding commitment made students more likely to register for, sit and pass exams; reminders alone did nothing; procrastinators gained most. ([AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/app.20170288))
- **Ariely & Wertenbroch (2002)**, *Psychological Science* 13(3), 219–224: students chose costly self-set deadlines, which helped but less than imposed ones. **Replication warning:** later replications, including one in *Psychological Science* (2026), did not reproduce key results. ([original](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1467-9280.00441); [2026 replication](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/09567976261460772))
- No meta-analysis of commitment devices for studying was found. Take-up is typically low.
- **Provisional grade:** research **3**, effect **+** (modest; helps sitting and passing exams more than grades), band **C**.

### Test-taking strategy and test-wiseness (TST sub-strand)

- **Samson (1985)**, "Effects of training in test-taking skills on achievement test performance: A quantitative synthesis", *Journal of Educational Research* 78(5), 261–266. Citation **V** (from reference lists); effect size, study count and a DOI link **not found**. Find the journal page before grading.
- **Kulik, Bangert-Drowns & Kulik (1984)**, "Effectiveness of coaching for aptitude tests", *Psychological Bulletin* 95(2), 179–188; and Bangert-Drowns, Kulik & Kulik (1983) on achievement tests, *Review of Educational Research* 53, 571–585. Secondhand: longer drill programmes slightly more effective than short orientation; SAT coaching effects small. ([Briggs review of test-prep meta-analyses](https://www.colorado.edu/education/sites/default/files/attached-files/Briggs_Meta-Analysis.pdf))
- **Hattie, *Visible Learning for Teachers* (2012):** "teaching test-taking and coaching" about **d = 0.27** (secondhand, from a university-hosted copy). ([PDF](https://su.se/polopoly_fs/1.431459.1552987755!/menu/standard/file/Visible%20Learning%20for%20Teachers%20Maximizing%20Impact%20on%20Learning%20by%20John%20Hattie%20%28Routledge%2C%202012%29.pdf))
- No independent evidence was found for specific heuristics (three-pass, negative-marking rules).
- **Provisional grade:** research **3**, effect **+** (small), band **C**. Check Samson's numbers before finalising.

### Confidence calibration / certainty-based marking (PLN variant)

- **Moodle and UCL LAPT documentation:** CBM is a proper scoring rule; the certainty thresholds are set at 67% and 80% probability of being right. ([MoodleDocs](https://docs.moodle.org/36/en/Using_certainty-based_marking); [UCL LAPT](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/lapt/REAP_CBM.htm))
- **BMC Medical Education (2019)**, two-cohort comparison, 389 biomedical students: certainty-based formative tests raised course appreciation but **did not change** summative exam scores. ([PDF](https://bmcmededuc.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s12909-019-1610-2))
- **Slovenia (2022)**, *IJERPH*: most medical students found CBM fair (78%) and useful (94%); a reliability and attitudes study, not a learning trial. ([PMC8834968](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8834968/))
- No randomised trial or meta-analysis on learning gains found.
- **Provisional grade:** research **2**, effect **± / n/a**, band **D** (close to C).

### Context matching (TST variant)

- **Smith & Vela (2001)**, "Environmental context-dependent memory: A review and meta-analysis", *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* 8(2), 203–220. Mean effect about **d = 0.28** (secondhand); the effect shrinks when learners mentally reinstate the context or other cues are present, and is weaker for recognition than recall. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03196157))
- **Godden & Baddeley (1975)**, *British Journal of Psychology* 66, 325–331: divers recalled words better in the place they learned them. **Murre (2021)**, *Royal Society Open Science*, a direct replication, **did not** find the effect; a 2022 addendum found a significant interaction on re-analysis but the author still judged it not a successful replication. ([Murre 2021 atlas entry](https://forrt.org/flora-replication-atlas/doi/10.1098/rsos.200724/); [addendum](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8728159/))
- **Grant et al. (1998)**, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied*: students reading an article in silence or noise did better when tested in the matching condition (secondhand only).
- **Provisional grade:** research **3**, effect **+** (small, conditional; little real-exam evidence), band **C**.

### Overlearning (noted as a RET/ADP variant, graded for completeness)

- **Driskell, Willis & Copper (1992)**, "Effect of overlearning on retention", *Journal of Applied Psychology* 77(5), 615–622: overlearning has a "moderate" effect on retention that depends on the amount of overlearning, task type and retention interval. Reported effect sizes and study counts differ between secondary sources (one cites d = 0.75 for cognitive tasks). Note: the brief's "Driscoll et al. 1994" should read **Driskell, Willis & Copper 1992**. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.77.5.615))
- **Rohrer, Taylor, Pashler, Wixted & Cepeda (2005)**, *Applied Cognitive Psychology*: gains clear at 1 week were almost undetectable at 4 weeks; most earlier studies tested after one week or less. ([USF record](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1772/))
- **Provisional grade:** research **3**, effect **±** (short-term gain that fades; inefficient against spacing), band **C**.

### Lead only: reading aloud (production effect)

- MacLeod et al. (2010) named the production effect; **Fawcett (2013)** meta-analysis found a small between-participants effect, about g = 0.37 (secondhand). ([Forrin & MacLeod 2016, citing it](https://uwaterloo.ca/memory-attention-cognition-lab/sites/default/files/uploads/files/forrin-macleod-2016-cjep.pdf)) Lab word lists only; not graded.
