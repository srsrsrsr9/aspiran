# I am grouping study apps, books and methods by the central learning idea behind them. I want to

compare my grouping with how researchers classify learning and study strategies.

List the main published classifications (taxonomies) of learning or study strategies, for example
Weinstein and Mayer (1986), Pintrich's MSLQ (1991), Zimmerman's self-regulated learning model,
Dunlosky et al. (2013), Hattie and Donoghue (2016), Donker et al. (2014), the EEF Teaching and
Learning Toolkit, and any others that are widely cited.

For each one give:

1. Authors, year, and title
2. A link to the paper or official page
3. The categories it uses, listed exactly
4. One line on what it was built for (for example teachers, students, a questionnaire)

Then give one table that lines up the categories across all the classifications, so I can see which
ideas appear in all of them and which appear in only one. Only include papers you can link to.

Below are seven widely cited, linkable classifications. They differ in purpose: some are **taxonomies of strategies**, some are **questionnaires**, some are **models of self-regulation**, and some are **evidence syntheses for teachers**. I have kept each framework’s own labels rather than forcing them into one vocabulary.

## What this means for your grouping

- **Nearly universal ideas:** rehearsal/memorisation, organisation, elaboration, monitoring, planning/goal-setting, and effort or self-regulation appear in most frameworks, though under different names.
- **Mostly cognitive-psychology ideas:** spaced practice, interleaving, retrieval practice/practice testing, and specific techniques such as highlighting are central in Dunlosky et al. and Hattie \& Donoghue, but are not top-level categories in older taxonomies.
- **Mostly self-regulation ideas:** forethought, performance monitoring, self-reflection, help seeking, peer learning, and time/environment management are strongest in Zimmerman, MSLQ, Donker et al., and the EEF.
- **Useful distinction for your grouping:** separate **what the learner does to material** (rehearse, organise, elaborate, test, space, interleave) from **how the learner manages learning** (plan, monitor, evaluate, seek help, manage time and environment). That split matches the recurring cognitive-versus-metacognitive/resource-management divide.[^1_3][^1_8][^1_10]
