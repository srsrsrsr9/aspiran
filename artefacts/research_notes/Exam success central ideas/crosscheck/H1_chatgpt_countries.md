# International Examples of the 26 Central Ideas Behind Exam Success

**Research date:** 1 November 2026\
**Countries considered:** India, China, Japan, South Korea, UK, US,
Germany, Brazil and Nigeria

## Research brief

For each central idea used by study tools to help people pass exams,
identify up to three real examples from different countries or exam
cultures. Each example should include its name, country, type, link
where available, and a one-line explanation of how it applies the idea.
Prefer locally well-known but internationally less familiar examples. Do
not invent examples; leave a row empty when evidence is insufficient.

The 26 central ideas and codes below are taken from the uploaded
document *Exam success central ideas.md*.

## Research notes and confidence

A few distinctions matter when using this as a comparative research
dataset.

1.  **A product can represent multiple ideas.** For example, YPT
    combines focus control, planning, motivation, study groups and
    public rankings. It is placed under the ideas most directly
    supported by its features.

2.  **Exam culture matters.** The Chinese mistake notebook, Japanese
    red-sheet revision, Korean study-hour rankings and Indian UPSC
    topper answer copies are particularly interesting because they
    represent different ways of turning study behaviours into
    recognisable products or practices.

3.  **Some examples are stronger than others.** Government programmes,
    established publishers and official platforms have stronger
    documentation than individual commercial products. Some examples are
    identified in the source catalogue but would benefit from additional
    independent verification.

4.  **An empty row is intentional.** In particular, LIF, QFX and BAS
    need further research rather than forced examples.

## Five practices worth investigating more deeply

1.  **China's paid study-room economy:** Study space itself becomes a
    purchasable examination-preparation service.
2.  **Japan's red-sheet system:** A simple physical transformation of
    textbooks into retrieval-practice tools.
3.  **Korea's study-hour rankings:** Study effort becomes measurable,
    social and competitive.
4.  **India's UPSC topper-copy culture:** Students learn examination
    technique by studying actual high-performing answer scripts.
5.  **China's 错题本 culture:** Mistakes become a separate, organised
    study resource rather than disappearing after a test.

**Source framework:** The uploaded document *Exam success central
ideas.md*, particularly its central-ideas table and representative
examples.

**Suggested next research step:** Investigate the underrepresented ideas
(MIX, LIF, QFX and BAS) more deeply, particularly in countries whose
examination cultures are not well represented in the original 527-entry
catalogue.
