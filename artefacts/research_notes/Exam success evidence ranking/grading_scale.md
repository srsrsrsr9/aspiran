# Evidence grading scale for Part 4

Every idea gets two separate judgements, so "well researched" and "works" are not confused.

## 1. Amount and quality of research (how much do we know?)
- **5 — Extensive.** Several meta-analyses (or one very large one) plus pre-registered or large classroom /
  field trials; findings replicated across ages, subjects and countries.
- **4 — Strong.** At least one good meta-analysis plus some classroom or real-exam studies.
- **3 — Moderate.** Many studies but mostly lab-based, short-term or small; or meta-analyses with high
  heterogeneity or quality concerns.
- **2 — Limited.** A handful of trials, small samples, or evidence only for a related idea.
- **1 — Minimal.** Case reports, surveys, vendor studies or theory only.
- **0 — None found.**

## 2. Direction and size of effect on learning / exam performance (what does it show?)
- **++ Clearly positive** — consistent benefit, typically g/d ≥ 0.4 or robust practical gains.
- **+ Positive but modest or conditional** — small effects (≈0.1–0.4) or benefit only under certain conditions.
- **± Mixed / unclear** — conflicting results, failed replications, or benefits that vanish in better studies.
- **0 No effect** — good studies find no benefit.
- **− Harmful or worse than alternatives** — hurts learning or wellbeing, or is clearly worse than the
  alternative it replaces.
- **n/a** — not enough research to say.

## 3. Overall rank band (used for the final ranking)
- **A. Proven** (research 4–5, effect ++)
- **B. Well supported, with conditions** (research 3–5, effect + or ++ with important limits)
- **C. Promising but thin or mixed** (research 2–3, effect + or ±)
- **D. Little or no research** (research 0–2, effect n/a)
- **E. Researched and found not to work, or harmful** (research 3–5, effect 0 or −)

Within a band, rank by research amount, then effect size, then relevance to real exams
(classroom or real-test outcomes beat lab word lists).

## Rules
- Prefer meta-analyses and systematic reviews; give authors, year, journal, number of studies (k),
  participants (N) and effect size with its measure (g, d, OR) where available.
- Note who was studied (school, university, adults), what outcome (lab recall vs course exam vs
  standardised test), and how long after learning.
- Separate the IDEA's evidence from any BRAND's evidence (vendor studies are noted but weigh little).
- Flag replication failures, publication bias and conflicts of interest.
- Give a link (DOI or journal page) for every study cited.
