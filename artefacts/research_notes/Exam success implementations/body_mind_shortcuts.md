# Implementation patterns: body and mind (LIF, ANX) and shortcuts, handovers and defaults (GEN, OFF, QFX, BAS)

Part 3 research notes, 1 November 2026. Scope: how six of the 26 Part 2 ideas are put into practice as app features, products, programmes, public services and personal habits. Effectiveness is left to Part 4. Catalogue numbers (#) refer to the Part 1 master list (`artefacts/reports/Exam success tools and methods.md`), and codes follow `research_notes/Exam success central ideas/taxonomy_v1.md`. Where a fact comes from the Part 1 catalogue, the catalogue's own source link is given. About 15 web searches were run this session; the rest is from the catalogue.

## Q1. What distinct implementation patterns does each idea have, and how does each work (steps, design parameters, entries, cost)?

### Takeaway
Across the six ideas, the same thing comes in three forms. One is a free method or habit with no product. One is a public or institutional service. One is a paid app that packages the method and adds an engagement layer. The design choices that separate variants are: **when** it is used (weeks before, the night before, 10 minutes before, during the exam), **who makes the output** (the learner, an AI, a counsellor, a peer), **how output is checked** (not at all, by the learner, by a teacher or editor, by recall checks), and **what the tool holds back** (answers, steps, the full card set).

### Cited Findings

#### LIF: body basics (9 primary, 20 any)
Mostly carried as advice and named methods, rarely as a product. Part 1 entries: #365 sleep before and after study, #366 naps, #367 targeted memory reactivation (TMR), #368 exercise, #369 breakfast, #370 caffeine, #371 hydration, #271 *Why We Sleep*, #135 Sleep Cycle, BetterSleep and Rise (apps), and LIF as a secondary in NHS exam-stress pages (#144), Headspace and Calm (#153, #154), Tide 潮汐 (#430, China) and Huberman's study protocol (#295).

Patterns:
1. **Pre-exam sleep rules ("no all-nighter", "sleep after studying").** A personal habit, pushed by university health pages and sleep societies. Steps: stop studying at a set time, sleep 7–9 hours the night before, sleep again after a study day to consolidate. Design parameters: hours (7–9), timing (the night before and after learning). Columbia's Go Ask Alice says students who pull an all-nighter or lose a few hours are less likely to do well on a next-day exam than peers who slept 7–9 hours — [Go Ask Alice](https://goaskalice.columbia.edu/answered-questions/how-do-i-get-most-out-all-nighter). The American Academy of Sleep Medicine's patient site says a full night after studying may help the brain consolidate new information — [Sleep Education (AASM)](https://sleepeducation.org/final-exams-is-sleep-the-secret-to-better-grades/). Cost: free.
2. **Naps.** Keep them short. Go Ask Alice notes naps longer than about 30 minutes can cause temporary grogginess — [Go Ask Alice](https://goaskalice.columbia.edu/answered-questions/how-do-i-get-most-out-all-nighter). Part 1 records nap lengths of about 10–90 minutes as the range discussed (#366, BK tag in catalogue).
3. **Caffeine timing.** A rule of thumb, mostly from student and health pages: stop caffeine about six hours before bed — [Red & Black (UGA student paper) quoting an expert](https://www.redandblack.com/variety/snoozing-in-danger-during-finals-week/article_16ec6e86-3e68-11e2-8522-001a4bcf6878.html). The figure traces to a 2013 trial reported to show 400 mg six hours before bed cut sleep by over an hour, but this session only found it via a vendor blog (n=12), so it is unverified — [Takeroon blog (vendor)](https://www.takeroon.com/blog/best-energy-drinks-for-studying). Commercial versions are energy drinks and "smart drinks" sold for study; caffeine plus L-theanine also appears in the nootropic row (#169) — Part 1 catalogue.
4. **Exam-day food, water and breaks in public guidance.** NHS trust exam-stress pages bundle sleep, exercise, breaks, timetables, less caffeine and less social media into one list (#144) — [WY Healthier Together (NHS)](https://wyhealthiertogether.nhs.uk/health-young-people/exam-pressure). Breakfast (#369) and hydration (#371) are BK-tagged in Part 1 with "mixed" and "small, confounded" evidence notes.
5. **Exercise breaks.** A habit (#368), not a product; it appears as an element inside focus and wellbeing apps rather than as a standalone exam tool (Part 1 catalogue, BK).
6. **Sleep apps.** Smart alarms, sleep logs and soundscapes (#135 Sleep Cycle, BetterSleep, Rise) and sleep content inside meditation subscriptions (#153 Headspace, #154 Calm) — Part 1 catalogue. None is exam-specific; the exam link is supplied by the user.
7. **Targeted memory reactivation (TMR).** A lab technique: play a sound or smell cue during learning, replay it during sleep. A 2020 meta-analysis of 91 experiments found a small-to-moderate benefit — [Hu et al. 2020, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7144680). It is distinct from "sleep learning" of new material (QFX #388).

#### ANX: exam nerves and mental wellbeing (30 primary, 38 any)
The largest and most public-sector of the six ideas. Patterns fall into five layers: brief pre-exam scripts, in-the-moment calming, self-help apps, group and one-to-one support, and helplines and national programmes.

1. **Expressive writing before the exam (#136).** Protocol from Ramirez and Beilock (*Science*, 2011). Steps: just before the test, the student writes for 10 minutes about their thoughts and feelings about the upcoming exam; controls wrote about an unrelated topic or sat quietly. Classroom study: 106 ninth-graders before biology finals; lab study: 47 college students on a low- then high-pressure maths test — [UChicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/writing-about-worries-eases-anxiety-and-improves-test-performance); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/01/110113141605.htm). Design parameters: dose 10 minutes, timing immediately before, delivered by a teacher in class, no one reads the writing. Exact prompt wording is in the paper's supplement (not reached this session). A published critique warned it may not help, or may harm, calm students — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/write-stuff-test-anxiety). Cost: free. Some colleges reprint it as a student handout — [Normandale Community College PDF](https://www.normandale.edu/_files/documents/osd/WritingAboutAnxietyImprovesTestScores.pdf).
2. **Arousal reappraisal script (#137).** A short message before the test telling students a racing heart can help performance (Jamieson et al. 2010; "get excited", Brooks 2014). Parameters: a few sentences, read just before; delivered by researcher, teacher or app. Later results mixed — Part 1 citing [Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/4214916) and [Columbia EPIC](https://epic.tc.columbia.edu/research/academic-alert/blog-posts/2023/can-stress-actually-be-beneficial-to-achievement/).
3. **Values self-affirmation writing (#138).** About 15 minutes writing about a personal value, usually at the start of term or before a stressful test (Cohen et al. 2006, 2009); replications mixed — Part 1 citing [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5403146).
4. **In-the-moment calming: box breathing (#514), 5-4-3-2-1 grounding (#515), PMR (#513), guided imagery (#516).** Box breathing: inhale, hold, exhale, hold for 4 counts each, repeat — [Children's Health Queensland](https://www.childrens.health.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0024/336318/grounding-strategies-for-coping-with-stress-adults.pdf). Versions differ: a University of North Dakota test-anxiety handout uses 4-in, 2-hold, 4-out breaths followed by 5-4-3-2-1 and a referral line to the counselling centre — [UND handout](https://und.edu/academics/services/academic-support/_files/docs/destress-less-than-two-min.pdf). Delivery forms: printable handouts, podcasts (Warwick's grounding podcast linked to exam-anxiety drop-ins — [Warwick](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/wss/topics/grounding-technique-podcast/)), live guided sessions (Anglia Ruskin Zoom session — [ARU](https://counsellingandwellbeing.anglia.ac.uk/event/4158283)), workshop toolkits (York University — [York SCD toolkit](https://counselling.students.yorku.ca/sites/default/files/2020-04/SCD%20Skills%20Workshop%20Toolkit.pdf)), and breathing features inside apps (Calm #154, Tide #430). Cost: free.
5. **University counselling exam-anxiety workshops (#159).** One to three group sessions mixing CBT and relaxation (Part 1 mechanism); the York, Warwick and Anglia Ruskin pages above are examples of the format.
6. **Mindfulness apps and school courses.** Headspace (#153, subscription; student rate reported at $9.99/yr vs $69.99 list — [SheerID](https://shop.sheerid.com/offers/headspace-discounted-subscription-for-students/)), Calm (#154), Smiling Mind (#160, free non-profit, Australia), .b by the Mindfulness in Schools Project (#161, nine teacher-led lessons, UK), and mindfulness as a method (#372) — Part 1 catalogue.
7. **CBT self-help apps and AI chatbots.** Wysa (#155, AI chatbot delivering CBT/DBT techniques plus optional human coach; freemium plus B2B contracts with the NHS and employers; FDA Breakthrough Device designation in 2022 for chronic pain with depression and anxiety, not an approval — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220512005084/en/Wysa-Receives-FDA-Breakthrough-Device-Designation-for-AI-led-Mental-Health-Conversational-Agent)); Youper (#156); MindShift CBT (#157, free, Anxiety Canada; support ended 31 Mar 2025 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/mindshift-cbt-anxiety-relief/id634684825)); Finch (#134, self-care pet game).
8. **Anonymous online counselling plus moderated peer community.** Kooth / Student Space (#158, UK, NHS-commissioned, free; BK in Part 1).
9. **Helplines: Tele-MANAS (#149), MANODARPAN (#151), CBSE seasonal counselling (#152).** Tele-MANAS steps: dial 14416 or 1-800-891-4416, choose a language on an IVR menu, get routed to a trained counsellor in a state cell (Tier 1), who can escalate to a specialist at a District Mental Health Programme or medical college (Tier 2) — [Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/health/mental-health/tele-manas); [PIB backgrounder (Oct 2024)](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2024/oct/doc20241013415701.pdf). Services in 20 languages, depending on what each state opts for; the app has English, Hindi and 10 regional languages — [Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/health/mental-health/tele-manas). Cell counts differ by source (53 state cells vs 51 cells plus 5 regional centres) — [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/explained-how-tele-manas-is-shaping-tele-mental-healthcare-in-india/). Exam link: NTA promoted it before NEET 2026; CBSE told schools to display it (Aug 2025); Haryana logged 9,106 calls Jan–Sep 2026, over 80% about exam stress, anxiety, sleep and substance use — Part 1 catalogue (#149). Cost: free, 24x7.
10. **National programme: Pariksha Pe Charcha (#150).** An annual town hall where the Prime Minister answers student questions (Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, 2024 and 2025). Participants are picked through a MyGov competition — creative writing in 2021–22, an MCQ quiz from 2023; for the 2026 edition the MCQ ran 1 Dec 2025 to 11 Jan 2026 and NCERT shortlisted questions for the live show; selected participants get a kit with the *Exam Warriors* book — [PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2000440); [CBSE circular 2024](https://www.cbseacademic.nic.in/web_material/Circulars/2024/106_Circular_2024.pdf). The book's "mantras" are spread through the NaMo app's Exam Warriors module and school painting contests — same PIB source. 2026 registrations passed 4 crore — Part 1 citing [News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/pariksha-pe-charcha-2026-breaks-records-with-over-4-crore-registrations/).
11. **Government and charity tip pages.** UK DfE test-anxiety resources for teachers and parents (#143), NHS (#144), Mind (#145), YoungMinds (#146), UCAS (#147), peer-written Kent Youth Voice tips (#148) — Part 1 catalogue.

#### GEN: AI-made study material (17 primary, 23 any)
Apps and websites only. Part 1 found these tools close to interchangeable.

1. **"Upload a PDF, get flashcards or a quiz" (fully automatic).** Steps: upload PDF, slides, photo, handwriting or a video link; the AI drafts cards or MCQs; the learner studies them, often inside a spaced-review schedule. Entries: Vaia/StudySmarter (#21), Gizmo (#23), Retain (#26), Revisely (#27), Jungle AI (#29), StudyCards AI (#30, exports to Anki), Mindomax (#32), Revprep (#46, India), Turbo AI (#55, 5M users by Oct 2025 — [TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2025/10/23/20-year-old-dropouts-built-ai-notetaker-turbo-ai-to-5-million-users)). Retain drafts editable Q&A, cloze and image-occlusion cards with a source reference on each card and schedules them against the exam date — Part 1 citing [Retain](https://www.retain.cards/). Cost: mostly freemium; tiers often unpublished.
2. **Learner-edited, AI-assisted card writing inside a scheduler.** The AI drafts, the learner edits or accepts, and the scheduler (often FSRS) runs reviews. Entries: Noji (#7), RemNote (#8, notes and PDF highlights become cards), Rember (#36), Avorio (#37), Knowt (#22) — Part 1 catalogue. This is the main design axis inside GEN: fully automatic vs learner-in-the-loop.
3. **Source-grounded notebook (NotebookLM #52, StudyFetch #54).** Output is built only from the files the user uploads, with citations. NotebookLM added flashcards and quizzes in Sep 2025; users can set number of cards (Fewer/Standard/More), difficulty (Easy/Medium/Hard), language, and a focus prompt; an "Explain" button gives a cited explanation of a card or a wrong answer; audio overviews are a two-host podcast-style summary — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/09/08/notebooklm-flashcards-quizzes/); [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/google-labs/notebooklm-student-features/). Users can uncheck sources so Chat and Studio outputs draw only on the selected ones — [9to5Google (app, Nov 2025)](https://9to5google.com/2025/11/06/notebooklm-app-flashcards-quizzes/). Cost: free tier with daily caps; paid tiers (Part 1).
4. **Lecture-to-notes and AI summaries.** Record a lecture, get notes (Coconote #57), or summaries and quizzes from documents and videos (Mindgrasp #56, Scholarly #31) — Part 1 catalogue.
5. **Browser-extension card makers.** Make cards from any web page or PDF while reading (Wisdolia #28; status unclear in Part 1).
6. **AI-written exam-style questions.** AI-generated practice MCQs and tutoring tied to an exam (Neural Consult #79, USMLE) — Part 1 catalogue.
7. **Instructor-checked AI cards (institutional pattern).** At Northeastern, an instructor generated end-of-semester review flashcards with Claude, then teaching assistants checked them and flagged errors, hallucinations or content not matching the course framing before students used them — [Northeastern CATLR AI gallery](https://learning.northeastern.edu/ai-gallery-post-ai-generated-flashcards-for-end-of-semester-exam-review/).

#### OFF: answer lookup and offloading (12 primary, 16 any)
Apps and websites, plus the guardrails built against them.

1. **Photo solvers.** Steps: photograph a problem, OCR plus a solver or model returns the answer, often with steps; premium tiers add more explanation or live tutors. Photomath (#60, Google): basic steps free; Photomath Plus about $9.99/month or $69.99/year adds animated tutorials, deeper explanations, hints and textbook solutions — [Google Photomath help](https://support.google.com/photomath/answer/14330572?hl=bg); [MyEngineeringBuddy review](https://www.myengineeringbuddy.com/blog/photomath-reviews-alternatives-pricing-offerings/). Gauth (#61): Plus prices vary by source and region ($7.99–$11.99/month; $59.99–$119.99/year reported) and include live tutor access — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1542571008); [autogpt.net review](https://autogpt.net/ai-tool/gauthmath-ai/). QANDA 콴다 (#407, Korea): about 90M registered, about 8M monthly users (2024), 90% outside Korea — Part 1 citing [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QANDA). Zuoyebang 作业帮 (#87, China), Xueba Jun 学霸君 (#405, collapsed Dec 2020), Google Socratic (#58, folded into Lens 2025), Mathway (#488, free answers, steps paywalled) — Part 1 catalogue.
2. **Answer engines.** Wolfram Alpha (#441, Pro shows steps), Symbolab (#442) — Part 1.
3. **Human or crowd answer services.** Chegg (#63, expert answers and textbook solutions by subscription; subscribers down 31% to 3.2M in Q1 2025; staff cut 22% then about 45% in 2025) and Brainly (#62, peers plus AI) — Part 1 citing [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/article/chegg-slashes-22-of-workforce-amid-ai-disruption-in-edtech/) and [Tech.co](https://tech.co/news/chegg-fires-half-workforce-new-realities-ai).
4. **General chatbots doing the work.** Not a catalogue row of its own, but the reason answer services lost users — Part 1 (Chegg rows). Field evidence of the pattern: in a randomised study of about 1,000 Turkish high-school maths students, an unrestricted GPT-4 chat ("GPT Base") raised practice scores 48% but lowered later unassisted exam scores by 17% versus control; a tutor version with guardrails ("GPT Tutor") raised practice scores 127% with no exam penalty — [Bastani et al., SSRN/PNAS](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4895486); [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education).
5. **Guardrail: tutor modes that withhold answers.** ChatGPT Study Mode (~29 Jul 2025), Gemini Guided Learning (early Aug 2025), Claude learning mode, Khanmigo (#53; learners $4/month or $44/year; free for US teachers) — Part 1 citing [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/06/google-takes-on-chatgpts-study-mode-with-new-guided-learning-tool-in-gemini/) and [Khanmigo pricing](https://khanmigo.ai/pricing). Design choice: the user can usually switch the mode off.
6. **Guardrail: process recording (Turnitin Clarity #89; Grammarly Authorship #90; Writable #91).** Clarity is a writing space that records drafting (pastes, deletions, typing), gives the teacher a playback and a timeline of typed vs pasted text, and lets the teacher switch on an AI assistant with an assignment-level policy, with the chat log shown alongside — [UCLA DTS](https://dts.ucla.edu/initiatives/bruin-learn-center-excellence/academic-tech-tools/turnitin-clarity); [Turnitin guide](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/36982699236877); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/03/04/turnitin-students-teachers-ai-canvas). UCLA states it does not monitor anything outside the Clarity space and does not detect AI writing; the University of Florida page describes it as combining plagiarism detection with an AI assistant, so sources differ on scope — [UCLA](https://dts.ucla.edu/initiatives/bruin-learn-center-excellence/academic-tech-tools/turnitin-clarity); [UF](https://elearning.ufl.edu/instructor-help/teaching-and-learning-tools/tool-finder/items/turnitin-clarity.html). Paid add-on to Feedback Studio (Part 1).
7. **Guardrail: recall checks (Sparx Maths "bookwork checks", #67, UK).** Each question has a bookwork code; students copy code, working and answer into their book. At intervals the system shows a code from a question answered correctly in the last hour and asks for that answer without showing the question; fail it and the student must redo the question. Students who pass consistently get fewer checks; schools can change the setting — [Sparx support](https://support.sparxmaths.com/en/articles/342329-understanding-bookwork-checks); [St Aidan's FAQ](https://www.staidans.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/20240502-Sparx-FAQs-for-website.pdf). School licence.
8. **Guardrail: regulation (China).** In August 2021 platforms such as Afanti and Tipaipai said they would drop homework-search features; in December 2021 the Ministry of Education suspended filing and review of photo homework-search apps aimed at primary and middle school, saying they make students dependent and weaken independent thinking — [TechNode](https://technode.com/2021/12/14/chinese-regulators-suspend-homework-answer-search-apps/); [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1009216/china-blocks-apps-helping-students-with-their-homework-). Sources differ on whether this was a temporary removal or a suspension of approvals; no end date found.

#### QFX: quick fixes and popular beliefs (22 primary, 26 any)
Four carriers: subscription apps, physical products, paid courses and books, and free beliefs passed on by teachers, creators and parents.

1. **Brain-training game subscriptions.** Daily mini-games with scores, marketed as improving school or work performance. Lumosity (#162), Elevate (#163, freemium), Peak (#164), HAPPYneuron (#165) — Part 1 catalogue.
2. **"Study drugs" and nootropics.** Prescription drugs used off-label (modafinil "anti-sleep pills" #167, ADHD stimulants #168), and herbal or "smart drink" supplements (#169: brahmi and Shankhpushpi syrups, racetams, L-theanine plus caffeine; #166 "Smart Kids Brain Boost"). Sold through pharmacies, informal sellers and online shops; modafinil spread beyond Indian medical colleges — Part 1 citing [Caravan](https://caravanmagazine.in/reportage/modafinil-tightening-hold-over-students).
3. **Speed reading.** Either eye-movement training ("stop subvocalising") or RSVP apps that flash one word at a time (Spritz) (#386); sold as courses (Kwik Learning #293; *Limitless* #275) — Part 1 citing [Rayner et al. 2016, APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/speed_reading.html).
4. **Audio "brain" products.** Binaural beats (#376), "functional" music (#104 Brain.fm, Endel, Noisli, QFX secondary), Mozart-effect products (#389), subliminal and affirmation audio and apps (#387) — Part 1 catalogue.
5. **Sleep learning (#388).** Play new material during sleep; distinct from TMR (#367), which replays a cue for material learned while awake — Part 1 citing [Hu et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7144680).
6. **Learning-styles quizzes and labels (#380).** A questionnaire (VARK, VAK, Honey and Mumford, Kolb) labels the learner, then teaching or study is "matched" to the label; Pashler et al. 2008 found properly designed studies contradict matching — Part 1 citing [USF](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1765/).
7. **Movement and brain-type beliefs.** Brain Gym (#383), left/right-brain types (#385), "10% of the brain" (#384), photographic-memory training (#390) — Part 1 catalogue (mostly BK).
8. **Small physical tricks.** Chewing gum about five minutes before a test (#377): short, small arousal effect, some studies null — Part 1 citing [PubMed 2019 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30611764/).
9. **Debunking side.** *Urban Myths about Learning and Education* (#268) — Part 1.

#### BAS: default habits (6 primary, 7 any)
Mostly unsold habits; they show up as defaults built into tools and plans.

1. **Rereading (#315).** The default "read the chapter again" plan; Dunlosky et al. 2013 rated it low utility — Part 1 citing [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html). Text-to-speech revision is rereading by ear: Speechify (#493, free tier plus Premium), NaturalReader (#494) — Part 1.
2. **Highlighting and underlining (#316).** Built into every e-reader and PDF tool. Kindle's Popular Highlights launched in 2010, on by default, built from readers' synced highlights; a 2022 report said it had to be switched off per book — [the-ebook-reader blog](https://blog.the-ebook-reader.com/2022/01/10/do-you-like-popular-highlights-in-kindle-ebooks/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/how-to-turn-off-popular-highlights-amazon-kindle); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/consumer/amazon-peeking-over-kindle-users-shoulders-flna6C10406354). Readwise (#14) resurfaces saved highlights daily on a light spaced schedule (re-reading, not recall) — Part 1.
3. **Highlights as AI input.** RemNote turns PDF highlights into cards (#8) — Part 1; i.e. a tool can convert a BAS habit into a GEN/SPA input.
4. **Cramming and all-nighters (#381).** The night-before plan; coupled with caffeine, energy drinks and in extreme cases modafinil (#167). See LIF item 1 for the counter-advice.
5. **Multitasking while studying (#382).** Messages and media running during study; addressed by FOC tools (site blockers, focus timers), not by BAS products — Part 1.

### Inferences
- Within each idea, the decisive design parameter differs: **timing** for ANX scripts (immediately before the test), **who makes the material** for GEN (automatic vs learner-edited vs instructor-checked), **what is withheld** for OFF (answer, steps, or nothing), **what is claimed** for QFX (the product is often harmless; the claim is the issue), and **what is the default** for BAS (the tool makes the habit easy, e.g. a highlighter on every page).
- ANX and LIF are the only two of the six where public bodies are major providers (helplines, NHS pages, Pariksha Pe Charcha, DfE); GEN, OFF and QFX are almost entirely commercial.
- The "learner-in-the-loop" spectrum in GEN maps onto the generation effect that makes self-made cards useful; a tool that drafts but asks the learner to edit or answer before seeing the AI version sits between GEN and RET. (Inference; Part 4 should test.)
- OFF guardrails fall into four types: mode-based withholding (Study Mode, Khanmigo), process recording (Clarity, Grammarly Authorship), recall verification (Sparx bookwork checks), and regulation (China 2021). Only the last two are hard to switch off by the student.

### Gaps
- Exact Ramirez and Beilock prompt wording (in the *Science* supplement, not reached).
- No primary source fetched for the six-hour caffeine figure (Drake et al. 2013 is the likely origin; only a vendor blog cited it here).
- No published prices for most GEN apps (Retain, Revisely, Gizmo); Gauth and Photomath prices vary by region and source.
- No current confirmation of Kindle Popular Highlights' default state after 2022.
- No source found this session for how Indian coaching institutes build sleep, exercise or wellbeing into schedules (Kota guidelines were noted as unresearched in Part 1).

## Q2. What implementation pitfalls and harms are recorded (including regulator actions)?

### Takeaway
Harms cluster by idea: ANX tools suffer from low retention, closures and gaps between "app" and "crisis"; GEN tools can carry errors and remove the work that made self-made material useful; OFF tools enable copying and reduce independent practice (with measured exam losses in one field trial and a national suspension in China); QFX products attract regulator action over claims and, for drugs, safety; BAS defaults feel productive while doing little.

### Cited Findings
- **Mental-health app retention.** In panel data on 93 Android mental-health apps with 10,000+ installs, median 30-day retention was 3.3%; mindfulness and meditation apps 4.7%, peer-support 8.9%, breathing-exercise apps 0.0% — [Baumel et al. 2019, JMIR](https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567). Limits: Android only, panel data.
- **App closure.** MindShift CBT stopped updates and support on 31 Mar 2025; Mozilla had flagged its data collection — Part 1 citing [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/mindshift-cbt-anxiety-relief/id634684825).
- **Regulatory status overstated.** Wysa's FDA "Breakthrough Device" designation covers chronic pain with depression and anxiety and is not an approval — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220512005084/en/Wysa-Receives-FDA-Breakthrough-Device-Designation-for-AI-led-Mental-Health-Conversational-Agent). A reported 2026 NHS trial with no difference at 12 weeks is unverified (Part 1).
- **Crisis-line gaps.** Tele-MANAS language coverage depends on what each state opts for, and source counts of its cells differ — [Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/health/mental-health/tele-manas); [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/explained-how-tele-manas-is-shaping-tele-mental-healthcare-in-india/). Handouts that teach breathing usually end with a referral line to counselling, recognising that self-help is not enough for persistent anxiety — [UND handout](https://und.edu/academics/services/academic-support/_files/docs/destress-less-than-two-min.pdf).
- **Brief-intervention side effects.** A published critique warned that pre-exam expressive writing could harm calm students — [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/write-stuff-test-anxiety).
- **AI-made cards with errors.** A radiology study had 20 medical professionals review 1,284 AI-generated flashcards and found errors that had passed an automated AI check — [Asan Medical Center news](https://news-en.amc.seoul.kr/news/eng/detail.do?cntId=3283). In a two-course microlearning study, most students reported never spotting incorrect content, which the authors treat as a risk rather than reassurance — [arXiv 2508.11704](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.11704). A practitioner found two errors in the first 100 AI-made Anki cards and stopped using AI cards for topics they could not judge — [De Novo substack](https://denovo.substack.com/p/ai-generated-anki-cards-are-not-to) (anecdote).
- **Closed scheduling claims.** Retain, Vaia, Gizmo and Quizlet Learn use unpublished "adaptive" schedulers with no independent benchmark — Part 1 (#325).
- **Offloading harms learning without guardrails.** GPT Base users scored 17% lower on unassisted exams and did not perceive that they had learned less — [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4895486).
- **Photo solvers can be wrong.** A maths specialist said Photomath is "not always correct" — Part 1 citing [UBC ETEC523](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec523/2023/09/24/photomath-an-ongoing-debate/).
- **Guardrails can be gamed or are narrow.** Clarity does not monitor outside its own writing space — [UCLA](https://dts.ucla.edu/initiatives/bruin-learn-center-excellence/academic-tech-tools/turnitin-clarity); Turnitin admits AI detectors alone are unreliable — Part 1 citing [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/03/06/turnitin-announces-clarity-aim-to-make-student-writing-more-transparent/amp/). A Trustpilot reviewer says Sparx bookwork checks can be passed without showing working — [Trustpilot](https://de.trustpilot.com/review/maths.sparx-learning.com) (anecdotal).
- **China 2021 suspension.** Ministry of Education suspended filing and review of photo homework-search apps for primary and middle school, citing dependence and weakened independent thinking — [TechNode](https://technode.com/2021/12/14/chinese-regulators-suspend-homework-answer-search-apps/); [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1009216/china-blocks-apps-helping-students-with-their-homework-).
- **FTC v. Lumosity (Jan 2016).** $2M redress, $50M judgment suspended, over deceptive claims that the games improve school and work performance — [FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges).
- **NAD and HAPPYneuron.** Claims modified or permanently discontinued after a US National Advertising Division inquiry — [BBB National Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media-center/dd/advertising-claims-for-happyneuron-brain-training-method-modified-or-permanently-discontinued-following-nad-inquiry).
- **South Africa, 2012.** Advertising regulator ordered "Smart Kids Brain Boost" claims withdrawn — [IOL](https://iol.co.za/lifestyle/2012-05-16-holford-product-claims-fall-foul-of-asa/).
- **MHRA on modafinil (Oct 2016).** Part of the #FakeMeds campaign: warned freshers against buying "smart drugs" such as modafinil online, citing a survey in which 14% said they were likely to buy study drugs in the coming year — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/freshers-warned-to-be-smart-and-avoid-modafinil); [Pharmaceutical Journal](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/?p=23512). In Nov 2020 MHRA separately warned modafinil was linked to about 15% birth-defect risk in pregnancy vs 3% background — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/sleep-disorder-drug-modafinil-linked-to-increased-risk-of-birth-defects-and-also-to-reduced-effectiveness-of-contraception).
- **India: anti-sleep pills.** A Class 10 student took anti-sleep pills to stay awake and landed in ICU — Part 1 citing [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/health/class-10-student-takes-anti-sleep-pills-to-stay-awake-lands-in-icu-doctors-issue-warning).
- **Speed reading.** Trade-off between speed and comprehension; skimming works only for gist — Part 1 citing [Rayner et al. 2016](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/speed_reading.html).
- **Learning styles.** Matching instruction to style is contradicted by properly designed studies — Part 1 citing [Pashler et al. 2008](https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1765/).
- **BAS illusion of fluency.** Rereading creates familiarity without retrieval; Dunlosky rated rereading and highlighting low — Part 1 citing [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html).
- **Default highlighting as a privacy issue.** Kindle Popular Highlights compiled users' highlights on Amazon's servers by default; early opt-out meant disabling backup — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/consumer/amazon-peeking-over-kindle-users-shoulders-flna6C10406354); [Errata Security](https://blog.erratasec.com/2010/05/popular-highlights-on-amazon-kindle.html).

### Inferences
- Regulator action targets **claims** (FTC, NAD, South Africa ASA) for QFX, **safety and supply** for drugs (MHRA), and **dependence** for OFF (China). No regulator action was found against GEN tools for card errors; quality control there is left to the learner or instructor.
- A common pitfall in GEN and OFF is the same: the tool removes the effortful step (making the card, solving the problem) that the learning came from, and users do not notice (GPT Base users did not perceive lower learning).
- In ANX, the gap is between low-intensity self-help (high drop-off) and crisis care; good implementations chain them (handout → drop-in → counselling; Tele-MANAS Tier 1 → Tier 2).

### Gaps
- No error-rate study found for consumer "upload a PDF" apps specifically.
- No independent data found on how often students in India or elsewhere use photo solvers to copy vs check.
- No source found on whether China's 2021 suspension has been lifted or how AI photo-search features in Zuoyebang now comply.
- No data found on Pariksha Pe Charcha's effect or on Tele-MANAS call outcomes beyond call counts.

## Q3. How do implementations differ across product types and regions?

### Takeaway
LIF and ANX are delivered mostly as free public services and advice in the UK and India and as paid subscriptions in the US-led app market; GEN is a global app wave with near-identical products; OFF is strongest and most regulated in East Asia; QFX varies by local product (brahmi syrups and anti-sleep pills in India, brain-training apps in the US); BAS is universal and built into tools by default.

### Cited Findings
- **India: state-led exam wellbeing.** Tele-MANAS (Health Ministry, 24x7, multilingual, tiered referral), MANODARPAN (Education Ministry), CBSE seasonal board-exam counselling, and Pariksha Pe Charcha with 4 crore+ registrations in 2026 — Part 1 (#149–#152) and [News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/pariksha-pe-charcha-2026-breaks-records-with-over-4-crore-registrations/). India's Jan 2024 coaching-centre guidelines require counselling and forbid rank guarantees — Part 1 citing [Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/cannot-enrol-students-below-16-years-no-misleading-promises-moe-guidelines-for-coaching-centres/amp).
- **UK: charity, NHS and government guidance plus NHS-commissioned online counselling.** DfE resources, NHS trust pages, Mind, YoungMinds, UCAS, Kooth — Part 1 (#143–#148, #158). University counselling services run grounding and exam-anxiety sessions (Warwick, Anglia Ruskin) — [Warwick](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/wss/topics/grounding-technique-podcast/); [ARU](https://counsellingandwellbeing.anglia.ac.uk/event/4158283).
- **Australia and Canada: free non-profit apps.** Smiling Mind (Australia), MindShift CBT (Canada, discontinued 2025) — Part 1.
- **US-led commercial wellbeing.** Headspace and Calm subscriptions with student discounts; Wysa sold B2B to NHS and employers — Part 1.
- **GEN: global, interchangeable.** German (Retain, Vaia/StudySmarter), US (Gizmo, Turbo AI, StudyFetch, Google NotebookLM), Indian (Revprep) apps follow the same upload-and-generate flow — Part 1.
- **OFF: East Asia.** China's photo-search giants (Zuoyebang, Xueba Jun, Afanti, Tipaipai) and the 2021 regulatory suspension; Korea's QANDA with most users in Vietnam and Indonesia — [TechNode](https://technode.com/2021/12/14/chinese-regulators-suspend-homework-answer-search-apps/); Part 1 citing [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QANDA). Zuoyebang also sells a pocket printer for the wrong-answer notebook (Miaomiaoji) — Part 1.
- **OFF guardrails: UK and US schools.** Sparx bookwork checks are a UK school-licence product; Turnitin Clarity is piloted by US universities (UCLA, UF) — [Sparx support](https://support.sparxmaths.com/en/articles/342329-understanding-bookwork-checks); [UCLA](https://dts.ucla.edu/initiatives/bruin-learn-center-excellence/academic-tech-tools/turnitin-clarity).
- **QFX by region.** US: Lumosity, Elevate, HAPPYneuron (FTC/NAD actions). UK: MHRA warnings on modafinil. South Africa: supplement claims withdrawn. India: modafinil and "anti-sleep pills" spreading among students, brahmi and Shankhpushpi "memory" syrups — Part 1 (#162–#169) and sources above.
- **Product type pattern.** Apps dominate GEN, OFF and commercial ANX; methods and advice dominate LIF, BAS and research-derived ANX; physical products appear only in QFX (drugs and supplements) — [Part 2 report, "What carries it" sections](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

### Inferences
- For an Indian audience, the main public ANX channel is phone-based and multilingual (Tele-MANAS), not app-based; a product that serves Indian aspirants would naturally point to it rather than replace it.
- Regulation of OFF is national and strong in China, institutional (school licences, university tools) in the UK and US, and absent in India as far as this research found.
- QFX harms are most physical in India (unprescribed wakefulness drugs) and most financial in the US (subscription brain games).

### Gaps
- No Indian regulator action (CCPA, ASCI, CDSCO) on brain-boost syrups or study-drug sales was searched this session.
- No data on the regional mix of NotebookLM or other GEN users.
- No source on Japanese or Korean public exam-anxiety programmes equivalent to Tele-MANAS or Pariksha Pe Charcha.
