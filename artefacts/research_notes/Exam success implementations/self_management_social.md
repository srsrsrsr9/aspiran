## Key question 1: What distinct implementation variants does each idea have, how do they work, what are the design parameters, which entries use them, and what do they cost?

### Takeaway
Each idea shows up in four to eight recurring forms. PLN and LHL live mostly in methods, books and courses. FOC and HAB live in apps. SPC is unusual because its main form is a paid physical place. GRP lives in methods and messaging communities. CMP is almost never a product of its own; it is a feature (leaderboard, league, rank, percentile) bolted onto timers, games and mock-test series. The design parameters that recur across all seven are: who sees the data (private, friends, group, public), whether it is opt-in or default, whether the commitment can be undone, how the session is time-boxed, and how big the group is.

### Cited Findings

#### PLN: Planning and self-monitoring (24 primary, 74 any)

**Variant P1. Backward-planned timetable from the exam date.** Work back from the exam date to set coverage and the number of revision rounds; common in UPSC and JEE culture (#343, BK, untested as a named method) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). Its exam-specific cousin is "multiple revision cycles with limited sources": revise each subject three to five times from a few standard books (#340) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). Design parameters: number of revision rounds, fixed versus floating buffer days, subject rotation. Entries: #343, #340, IASbaba's 60-day Prelims plan (#200), Plutus IAS revision-plan guides (#208), *Mission IAS* and topper-strategy books (#224, #225, #227, #228) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). Cost: free as a method; packaged plans come inside paid courses.

**Variant P2. Capacity planners (hours needed versus hours available).** Shovel estimates study hours needed against hours available (#112); price about $237/yr per one review, unconfirmed — [Part 1, citing itechguides](https://www.itechguides.com/best/study-planner-apps/). Structured offers a drag-and-drop day timeline with timer (#113) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

**Variant P3. Vendor-sold day-by-day plans.** US bar-prep courses Barbri and Themis sell a day-by-day study plan with graded practice essays (#83, #84, BK) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). Diagnostic-generated plans: PrepScholar builds a personalised plan from a diagnostic test (#445); flashcard apps Retain and Vaia schedule toward the exam date (#21, #26) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). Design parameter: the plan is fixed by the vendor, so the learner's job shifts from planning to compliance.

**Variant P4. Time-blocking and task methods.** Timeboxing gives each task a fixed calendar slot (#352, BK); Getting Things Done captures tasks in an external system with a weekly review (#355, BK) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). Also the Eisenhower matrix (#353), Ivy Lee's six tasks each evening (#354), eat the frog (#357) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). App carriers: Google and Apple Calendar (#118), TickTick with Eisenhower matrix, habit tracker and Pomodoro (#114), Todoist with "karma" points (#115), MyStudyLife (#111), Notion student templates (#117) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

**Variant P5. WOOP / implementation intentions.** Four steps: Wish (challenging but achievable), Outcome (imagine the best result), Obstacle (the main *inner* obstacle), Plan as "If [obstacle], then I will [action]"; the site says urgent problems can take about 30 seconds; no fixed schedule ("as often as you have wishes"); app is free — [WOOP site](https://woopmylife.org/en/practice). The app was translated into all 11 South African official languages in 2022 and described as "bare-bones" — [Part 1, citing UCT](https://www.news.uct.ac.za/features/teachingandlearning/-article/2022-03-31-woop-goal-realisation-app-now-in-all-11-official-languages). Design parameter: the obstacle must be internal (a habit or feeling), not external.

**Variant P6. Retrospective review and reflection.** Exam wrappers: after results, students note how they prepared, the errors made and what they will change; free CMU templates; effects "highly variable" (#140) — [Part 1, citing CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/teaching/designteach/teach/examwrappers/). The Study Cycle (preview, attend, review, study, check) (#512) and the CREAM self-review checklist (#505) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). Error logs overlap with FBK (#141). Metacognition and self-regulation (plan, monitor, evaluate) is among the EEF's top-rated strands (#311) — [Part 1, citing EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/how-do-we-teach-metacognition).

**Variant P7. Study logs (self-monitoring, often social).** Studyplus: log materials and study time, auto-generated graphs, share logs on a timeline with friends to encourage each other; can be used anonymously; 10 million cumulative sign-ups announced July 2025 (cumulative, not active) — [ReseMom / PR Times, Jul 2025](https://resemom.jp/release/prtimes/20250701/149227.html); earlier milestones 50,000 (Dec 2012) and 500,000 (Jun 2014) — [atpress](https://www.atpress.ne.jp/news/47616). Studyplus for School lets teachers see students' logs and message them; used by about 1,000+ schools and cram schools — [atpress](https://www.atpress.ne.jp/news/38814) (via search snippet; figure date not confirmed). YPT logs hours per subject and shows day/week/month records whose colour deepens with more study — [App Store listing via AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/ypt-study-group/1441909643?country=ca). Focus To-Do and Be Focused log Pomodoro statistics (#99, #102) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

**Variant P8. Total-control institutional timetables.** The Hengshui model: minute-by-minute timetables, heavy drilling, morning recitation, strict discipline; widely criticised in Chinese media as an "exam factory"; included in Part 1 as an anti-pattern (#404, BK) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md).

Not found this round: a sourced description of the red-amber-green (RAG) retrospective revision timetable; it is not a catalogue entry. See Gaps.

MOOCs.** Learning How to Learn (Oakley and Sejnowski, Coursera, since Aug 2014): focused and diffuse thinking, chunking, procrastination, recall, spacing, sleep, test-taking; free to audit, certificate paid (#286) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). 197,000 joined the first run — [UCSD TDLC](https://tdlc.ucsd.edu/tdlc2/news_LHTL_MOOC.php); more than 4 million enrollments as of June 2025 (secondary source) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Oakley); totals conflict across years and outlets (about 1.1M to 4M) — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/education/learning-learn-2041630). A youth version launched Dec 2018 with 2,600+ youth in its first period — [ASU News](https://news.asu.edu/20190226-edplus-asu-partners-provide-universal-learning-techniques-youth). Others: related Oakley MOOCs (#287), OpenLearn study skills (#477), Athabasca's five-week Learning to Learn Online (#478), Mindware (#502), Think101x (#476) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

**Variant L2. Study-skills and science-of-learning books.** *Make It Stick* (#247), Carey's *How We Learn* (#249), Dehaene (#250), Willingham's *Outsmart Your Brain* (#252), *Ace That Test* (#256), *A Mind for Numbers* (#258), McGuire's *Teach Students How to Learn* (#266), *Ultralearning* (#273), Cottrell's *Study Skills Handbook* (#495), Jade Bowler's *The Only Study Guide You'll Ever Need* (#469) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). Part 2 found LHL content mostly repackages retrieval (27 mentions), understanding (21) and spacing (14) — [Part 2 report](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

**Variant L3. Evidence digests for teachers and students.** Dunlosky et al. 2013 (#236), IES Practice Guide (#243), Deans for Impact (#239), APA Top 20 (#486), the EEF Toolkit (#240), The Learning Scientists' free posters on six strategies (#237), InnerDrive (#485) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md); Learning Scientists books page — [learningscientists.org](https://www.learningscientists.org/books).

**Variant L4. Studytube and productivity creators.** Ali Abdaal (active recall, Anki, Feynman, Pomodoro; his Part-Time YouTuber Academy costs US$995 and teaches content creation, not studying) (#289) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md); Thomas Frank (#290), Med School Insiders, UnJaded Jade, Elizabeth Filips (#297, BK), Mike Dee (#481), Huberman Lab's studying episode (#295) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). Paid creator courses: iCanStudy (Justin Sung) (#288), Scott Young's courses (#291) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

**Variant L5. School and university strategy instruction.** Metacognition programmes (EEF: plan, monitor, evaluate) (#311) and McGuire's study cycle taught in university learning centres (#266, #512) — [Part 1, citing EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/how-do-we-teach-metacognition). General thinking-skill programmes: Pólya's four steps (#509) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

**Variant L6. Myth-busting.** *Urban Myths about Learning and Education* (#268) debunks learning styles and the learning pyramid — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

Design parameters: generic (all learners) versus exam-specific; free audit versus paid certificate; length (a 5-minute video vs a five-week course); whether it ends with a personal plan or just knowledge. Part 2 noted "no app is built around" LHL — [Part 2 report](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

#### FOC: Attention and distraction control (21 primary, 57 any)

**Variant F1. Pomodoro and interval timers.** 25 minutes work, 5 minutes break, longer break after four; named after a tomato kitchen timer (Cirillo, late 1980s) (#351, BK) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). Carriers: Focus To-Do (#99), Pomofocus (#100), Be Focused (#102), Session (starts with an intention, ends with a reflection) (#101), Studywithme.io (#451), LifeAt (#449) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). Parameters: fixed 25/5 versus custom lengths; whether a task list and stats sit alongside.

**Variant F2. Gamified commitment timers (focus trees).** Forest: a virtual tree grows while you stay off the phone and dies if you leave the app; coins can unlock species or fund real trees via Trees for the Future; a co-focus mode lets several users plant together — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_(application)). A 2018 review said real trees cost 2,500 coins each, capped at five per user, paid tier only (dated) — [RESET](https://en.reset.org/cure-your-phone-addiction-and-plant-trees-03282018/). Real-tree totals conflict across sources (109,760 on the homepage vs "over two million" on Wikipedia) — [forestapp.cc](https://forestapp.cc). Price conflicts: about $59.99/yr subscription vs $1.99 one-time — [Part 1, citing makeheadway](https://makeheadway.com/blog/forest-app-review/). Flora adds group pledges (#94); Tomato ToDo (China) has a "xueba mode" that locks other apps plus online study rooms (#429) — [Part 1, citing Woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/evaluating/3069778.html). Design parameters: penalty is symbolic (dead tree); reward converts to real-world good; group mode makes one person's exit kill everyone's tree (Flora/Forest group modes; mechanism BK).

**Variant F3. Hard blockers (precommitment).** Freedom blocks sites and apps on all devices on a schedule; about $8.99/mo, $39.99/yr, $199 lifetime (#95) — [Part 1, citing toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/freedom/pricing). Cold Turkey: hard-to-undo blocks including a full "Frozen Turkey" lockout; free basic, Pro about $39 one-time (#96) — [Part 1, citing makerstack](https://makerstack.co/reviews/cold-turkey-blocker-review/). Opal: scheduled blocks and focus reports; about $19.99/mo or $99.99/yr (#98) — [Part 1, citing makerstack](https://makerstack.co/reviews/opal-review/). LeechBlock NG (free extension): blocks at set times or after a time budget (#450) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). YPT also locks the phone during a timer (#103) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). Parameters: allow list vs block list; schedule vs on-demand; how hard to undo (Cold Turkey's lockout is the extreme).

**Variant F4. Friction apps.** one sec combines a short delay, a deliberation message and an option to dismiss before a chosen app opens. In the PNAS 2023 field study (280 participants, six weeks), 36% of opening attempts were abandoned after the intervention and attempts fell 37% by week six, combining to 57% fewer openings; in a controlled online follow-up the dismiss option had the strongest effect, the delay helped, and the message alone did not — [PNAS via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409). The app maker co-authored the study (conflict of interest) — [one sec](https://one-sec.app/max-planck-study). A longitudinal study of about 1,039 organic users found usage rises when people pause the friction and recovers when they resume — [Haliburton et al. 2024](https://researchgate.net/profile/Luke-Haliburton/publication/377774169_A_Longitudinal_In-the-Wild_Investigation_of_Design_Frictions_to_Prevent_Smartphone_Overuse/links/65b7d1311bed776ae313b600/A-Longitudinal-In-the-Wild-Investigation-of-Design-Frictions-to-Prevent-Smartphone-Overuse.pdf). Price: freemium, not verified — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md).

**Variant F5. Deep-work blocks and phone-away rules.** Deep work: long single-task blocks with phone and sites blocked (#358) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). Phone-away rules as such have no catalogue entry; they appear inside study-room etiquette (see SPC) and Hengshui-style discipline (#404).

**Variant F6. Noise masking and focus audio.** Brain.fm, Endel, Noisli (subscription; vendor neuroscience claims) (#104, BK); white/pink/brown noise (#375, BK, mixed); music while studying (#374); binaural beats coded QFX (#376) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). Tide 潮汐 (China) bundles focus timer, nature sounds and breathing (#430) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

**Variant F7. Books on systemic attention.** *Stolen Focus* (#285) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

#### HAB: Motivation, habits and mindset (11 primary, 49 any)

**Variant H1. Streaks.** Daily habits tracked as unbroken chains: Streaks app (#120, BK), Duolingo (#25) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). Duolingo's streak resets at midnight in the account's time zone, independent of the league clock; streak freezes exist but I found no reliable source on how many or how earned — [unanswered.io guide (low quality)](https://unanswered.io/guide/duolingo-reset-times-streaks-leagues-diamond-tournament). Daily check-ins: Shanbay vocabulary check-ins (#399), Insights IAS daily answer writing (#198) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). Parameters: reset rule (calendar day vs 24h), forgiveness (freezes, repair), visibility.

**Variant H2. XP, leagues and tournaments.** Duolingo: 10 leagues with Diamond at the top (expanded from 5 to make the top harder to reach); a new weekly league starts every Sunday in your time zone; you are grouped with learners of similar study habits and time zone, across all languages; promotion or demotion by effort; top 10 in Diamond eligible for a three-round Diamond Tournament; Duolingo monitors and removes illegitimate XP; leaderboards can be switched off by making the profile private; first tested in 2018 — [Duolingo blog, May 2023](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-leagues-leaderboards/). League size of 30 and exact promotion cut-offs come only from third-party guides that contradict each other (e.g., Diamond places 26–30 demoted) — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://duolingo.deconstructoroffun.com/mechanics/leagues). Classroom games: Kahoot! speed points and public leaderboards (#438), Wayground/Quizizz (#437), Gimkit in-game money (#440), Blooket (#439) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

**Variant H3. Role-play and pet games.** Habitica turns habits and to-dos into a role-playing game with parties (#119, BK, freemium) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md); Finch: care for a virtual pet by completing self-care goals (#134) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

**Variant H4. Habit-design methods (habit stacking, two-minute rule, identity, environment).** *Atomic Habits*: four laws, habit stacking, two-minute rule, environment design (#278); Matt D'Avella's 30-day experiments (#296); *Feel-Good Productivity* (#279) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). Mechanism details beyond the catalogue line were not re-checked this round.

**Variant H5. Growth-mindset interventions and "not yet".** Teach that ability grows with effort; praise process; "not yet" (#373) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). The US National Study of Learning Mindsets delivered two 25-minute online sessions at the start of high school to more than 12,000 ninth graders (65 or 76 schools; sources differ); reported +0.10 GPA points, larger where schools were lower-achieving and peer norms supportive; freely available to US and Canadian schools — [UT Austin](https://news.utexas.edu/2019/08/07/a-growth-mindset-intervention-can-change-students-grades-if-school-culture-is-supportive); [Northwestern IPR](https://ipr.northwestern.edu/news/2019/tipton-growth-mindset-study.html). Grit and Science of Success MOOCs (#270, #294) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). Design parameters: one-off short online module vs teacher-delivered culture change (grading "not yet").

**Variant H6. National reframing campaigns.** Pariksha Pe Charcha: PM's annual town hall reframing exams as a festival and telling parents not to compare; 2026 registrations over 4 crore — [Part 1, citing News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/pariksha-pe-charcha-2026-breaks-records-with-over-4-crore-registrations/).

#### SPC: Study spaces and co-presence (16 primary, 30 any)

**Variant S1. Paid silent desk (study rooms, dokseosil, study cafés, Indian "libraries").**
- China 付费自习室: rows of standard cubicles with small lockers; zones by lighting, noise and seat type; a bin at every seat so people needn't walk around; silence expected; seats by hour, day or year at roughly 20–100 yuan a day; first in Japan and Korea in the 1980s, mainland China from 2014, about 5,000 by 2021 — [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1015064/why-chinese-are-studying-solo%2C-together). Shanghai rooms about 2–20 yuan/hour (2020); Shanxi about 500 yuan/month (2024); about 400 yuan/month passes and an industry "over 10 billion yuan a year" (Jan 2026); more than 3,600 businesses, 92% founded in the previous five years; iiMedia survey: over half of users thought it too expensive; main users are 考研 and civil-service (考公) candidates — [China Daily, Jan 2026](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202601/14/WS6967416da310d6866eb33b30.html); [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1011458/Private%20Study%20Rooms%20Emerge%20as%20Refuge%20for%20Examinees); [PeopleNewsToday](https://www.peoplenewstoday.com/news/en/2024/11/23/1077674.html) (figures from search summaries; cities and years not comparable).
- Korea 독서실 / 스터디카페: almost all open 24/7, some kiosk-only (pick a seat and pay at a touch-screen, scan a barcode to enter); day pass 10,000 won, month 100,000 won, lockers 9,000 won/month (Gyeonggi, Jan 2026) — [Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/living/pacific_travel/after_hours/2026-01-15/study-cafe-south-korea-20349934.html); a fixed desk with 24/7 access about 150,000–200,000 won/month (Daejeon, undated) — [Cities Insider](https://citiesinsider.com/country/south-korea/daejeon/libraries-&-study-spaces/en). A Seoul district turned a youth reading room into a "public study café" — [Asia Economy](https://view.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2023061407434187157). Korean-style study rooms have been exported to US Korean communities — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/asian-america/traditional-korean-study-rooms-fulfill-new-mission-america-n150866).
- Japan 勉強カフェ Benkyo Café: membership spaces for adults with a lounge for chatting; joining fee about ¥11,000, monthly from about ¥4,400; 31 sites and 5,000+ members (2022) — [Part 1, citing ReseMom](https://resemom.jp/release/prtimes/20220921/88179.html).
- India paid "libraries" and reading rooms: monthly seat with AC, Wi-Fi and locker; Delhi premium about Rs 3,500–5,000/month — [Bharatnotes](https://bharatnotes.com/faq/study-environment/study-library-options-delhi/); Greater Noida about ₹800–1,500/month day-only, more for 24-hour seats — [Stanza Living](https://www.stanzaliving.com/blog/libraries-in-greater-noida-best-study-rooms-reading-halls-public-libraries-in-2026); Gurgaon chains ₹2,000–4,500/month — [magicpin](https://magicpin.in/blog/india/gurgaon/entertainment/best-libraries-gurgaon); a small-town listing ₹400/month for a 4-hour AC plan, ₹1,600 for a fixed seat — [Roushan Library](https://roushan-library.onrender.com/); ICAI non-profit reading room Rs 750/month AC vs Rs 100 non-AC (2019) — [WIRC-ICAI](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/library/ReadingRoom_FacilityNotice.pdf). All are listings, not market data.
Design parameters: hourly vs monthly vs fixed seat; staffed vs kiosk-only; silence rules; zoning (cubicle vs open desk vs lounge); 24-hour access; lockers.

Exam towns.** Noryangjin and Sillim gosichon (Korea): districts of cram schools, tiny rooms and cheap meals; shrank after the bar exam changed and lectures moved online (BK) (#415); Indian coaching hubs: Mukherjee Nagar (Hindi medium), Old Rajinder Nagar and Karol Bagh (English), Kota (JEE/NEET), Prayagraj, Patna (#235) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). **Variant S4. One-to-one body doubling by appointment.** Focusmate: book 25, 50 or 75 minutes; matched with a partner (over 99% of bookings matched; otherwise next slot 15 minutes later); greet and state goals; work with camera on; check in at the end; mic optional; Quiet Mode with text chat for libraries; favourite partners; lateness or no-shows lower a timeliness score and can freeze the account; report button for partners who goof off; talk limited to greeting and check-in, no selling or propositions; free up to three sessions a week; Plus $8/month yearly or $12 monthly — [Focusmate FAQ](https://www.focusmate.com/faq). **Variant S5. Hosted group sessions.** Flow Club: hosted group focus sessions with goal sharing and music; price conflicting (free only vs $40/mo) (#109); Caveday: facilitated group sprints ("caves") with a set ritual; paid, price unverified (#110) — [Part 1, citing Caveday](https://www.caveday.org/caveday-vs-flow-club).

**Variant S6. Tomato ToDo online study rooms (#429); LifeAt ambient co-working (#449) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

### Inferences
- Visibility is the master dial. The same study log is PLN when private (Focus To-Do stats), HAB when it shows a streak, SPC when others see you're "on" (YPT orange avatar), and CMP when ranked. Many products slide along this dial in one app.
- Commitment strength is the master dial for FOC: friction (one sec, dismissable) < scheduled blocks (Freedom, Opal) < hard lockout (Cold Turkey) < symbolic loss (Forest tree) < social loss (group tree, visible grey avatar).
- SPC is the only idea where the dominant implementation is a paid physical place, and its pricing converges across countries on "a monthly seat costs a small share of living costs": roughly ₹1,500–5,000 in Indian metros, 100,000–200,000 won in Korea, about 400–500 yuan in China.
- CMP almost never stands alone; it is attached to a timer (YPT), a game (Duolingo, Kahoot!) or a mock series (Fenbi, CAT mocks). Its parameter set is small: what is counted, against whom, over what period, and whether you can hide.
- LHL is content, not software; its implementations differ mainly by format and length, and they converge on the same handful of strategies.

## Key question 2: What are the common implementation pitfalls?

### Takeaway
The recurring pitfalls are: planning that replaces studying, counting time instead of learning (study-hour inflation), loss-framed mechanics that create anxiety (streaks, dying trees, last place), dependence on an outside prop (body double, blocker), unmoderated communities that carry piracy and scams, and physical study spaces that cut corners on safety. Many are design consequences of the visibility and commitment dials above.

### Cited Findings
- **Study-hour inflation and gaming.** Some YPT users clock minutes during non-study hours, which "erodes the tool's credibility"; one group champion logged 321 hours 12 minutes in a month (over 10 hours a day) — [Raffles Press](https://rafflespress.com/2025/02/13/yawns-pains-and-tears-on-ypt/). Duolingo monitors irregular activity and removes illegitimate XP from leaderboards — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-leagues-leaderboards/).
- **Leaderboard pressure and over-study.** Raffles Press describes "YPTunnel vision": obsessive leaderboard checking, fear of "the dreaded last place", lower energy, mood swings, skipped meals and lost sleep; it argues "effective studying is not synonymous with extensive studying"; it advises muting or blocking toxic group members — [Raffles Press](https://rafflespress.com/2025/02/13/yawns-pains-and-tears-on-ypt/) (student publication, Singapore; opinion).
- **Paid study rooms as involution and avoidance.** Sixth Tone links Chinese study rooms to rising youth stress, asocial behaviour and "involution", and says they can push people to exhaust themselves — [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1015064/why-chinese-are-studying-solo%2C-together); coverage frames some users as looking busy while avoiding job pressure, and over half of surveyed users found them too expensive — [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1011458/Private%20Study%20Rooms%20Emerge%20as%20Refuge%20for%20Examinees).
- **Physical safety in exam towns.** On 27 July 2024 three aspirants drowned in the flooded basement library of Rau's IAS, Old Rajinder Nagar; 13 centres sealed and a CBI probe — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/india/story/delhi-rains-3-upsc-aspirants-die-after-coaching-centres-basement-gets-flooded-in-rajinder-nagar-probe-ordered-439135-2024-07-28); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/basement-deaths-mcd-seals-13-illegal-coaching-centres-in-old-rajinder-nagar). Part 1 noted many Indian reading rooms are in basements (#234) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). Distress and suicides reported in coaching hubs (#235) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md).
- **Quality variance in paid spaces.** Indian reading-room reviews complain of poor Wi-Fi; guides advise visiting before paying a month — [Stanza Living](https://www.stanzaliving.com/blog/libraries-in-greater-noida-best-study-rooms-reading-halls-public-libraries-in-2026); [Yappe listing](https://yappe.in/uttar-pradesh/lucknow/self-study-space-library/2237683).
- **Unmoderated groups, piracy and scams.** Telegram study channels carry heavy piracy and "leaked paper" scams and were linked to NEET and UGC-NET 2024 leaks — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1069714/how-a-messaging-app-that-helps-students-prepare-for-exams-enabled-paper-leaks); Bharatnotes advises joining at most 5–8 channels — [Bharatnotes](https://bharatnotes.com/faq/coaching/telegram-groups-and-study-communities/). Reddit is better moderated but "can amplify negativity" (#233) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md).
- **Unstructured groups drift.** Part 1: unstructured study groups give mixed results; quizzing each other beats chatting (#360, BK) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md).
- **Body-doubling misuse and limits.** Focusmate needs rules against no-shows (timeliness score, account freezing), goofing off (report button) and selling or propositions — [Focusmate FAQ](https://www.focusmate.com/faq). Little research; a small 2025 EEG study found no effect (#362) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). I found no source measuring "dependence" on body doubling.
- **Friction and blocker relapse.** Usage rises when people pause friction and recovers on resumption — [Haliburton et al. 2024](https://researchgate.net/profile/Luke-Haliburton/publication/377774169_A_Longitudinal_In-the-Wild_Investigation_of_Design_Frictions_to_Prevent_Smartphone_Overuse/links/65b7d1311bed776ae313b600/A-Longitudinal-In-the-Wild-Investigation-of-Design-Frictions-to-Prevent-Smartphone-Overuse.pdf). Only the dismiss option and the delay worked; the message alone did not — [PNAS via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9974409).
- **Vendor-run evidence and inflated claims.** The one sec study had the app maker as co-author — [one sec](https://one-sec.app/max-planck-study); Focusmate's loneliness survey has no published method (#105); focus-audio vendors make neuroscience claims (#104, BK) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md). Forest's real-tree totals vary by an order of magnitude across sources — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_(application)); [forestapp.cc](https://forestapp.cc).
- **Over-control as a planning failure.** The Hengshui minute-by-minute model is widely criticised as an "exam factory" (#404, BK) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md).
- **Mindset messaging needs context.** The national mindset study found effects were larger only where school culture/peer norms were supportive — [UT Austin](https://news.utexas.edu/2019/08/07/a-growth-mindset-intervention-can-change-students-grades-if-school-culture-is-supportive); Sisk et al. 2018 meta-analysis found weak effects; contested (#373) — [Part 1, citing Sisk et al.](https://englelab.gatech.edu/articles/2018/Sisk,%20Burgoyne%20et%20al.%20(2018)%20-%20Mindset%20and%20Academic%20Achievement.pdf).
- **Comparison harms are recognised by the state in India.** Pariksha Pe Charcha tells parents not to compare — [Part 1, citing News on AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/pariksha-pe-charcha-2026-breaks-records-with-over-4-crore-registrations/).

### Inferences
- Any metric that is (a) public and (b) based on self-reported or timer-only time invites inflation; the two known mitigations are anti-cheat removal (Duolingo) and making the metric private or friends-only (Studyplus anonymity, Duolingo private profile).
- Loss-framed mechanics (streak break, dead tree, demotion, last place) are the common thread in reported anxiety; the forgiveness features (streak freeze, Quiet Mode, mute/block) are the design response.
- Planning-instead-of-studying and LHL-as-procrastination (watching studytube instead of studying) are widely discussed in creator culture, but I found no source that measures them this round (see Gaps).
- SPC pitfalls are physical and regulatory (fire, flooding, cost), not only psychological; they sit outside app design.

### Gaps
- "Planning instead of studying" (procrastiplanning) and "studytube as procrastination": no source fetched.
- Streak anxiety: no primary source (Duolingo help or research) fetched; only the YPT student article on comparison anxiety.
- No source measuring body-doubling dependence.
- No safety data for Chinese or Korean study rooms (fire codes, 24-hour unstaffed kiosks).

## Key question 3: How do implementations differ across product types and regions?

### Takeaway
East Asia turns self-management and co-presence into products and places (paid silent desks, YPT and Studyplus logs, Bilibili study rooms, national mock rankings with pass-likelihood grades). The West packages the same ideas as productivity apps, subscriptions and remote co-working (Freedom, Forest, Focusmate, Flow Club) plus learning-science books and MOOCs. India's distinct forms are exam towns, monthly-fee reading rooms, Telegram communities and mock-test percentiles, with a state counter-message against comparison.

### Cited Findings
- **East Asia makes habits into products.** Part 1 summarised: silence is something you pay for (Chinese study rooms, Korean study cafés, Benkyo Café), and study time is public and social (YPT rankings, Studyplus logs, Bilibili rooms) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md).
- **Paid study rooms spread from Japan and Korea (1980s) to mainland China (2014)**; about 5,000 by 2021 — [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1015064/why-chinese-are-studying-solo%2C-together). Korean cafés are mostly 24/7 and kiosk-run — [Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/living/pacific_travel/after_hours/2026-01-15/study-cafe-south-korea-20349934.html). Japan's Benkyo Café adds a social lounge and targets adults — [Part 1, citing ReseMom](https://resemom.jp/release/prtimes/20220921/88179.html). India's equivalent is the monthly-fee "library" with AC, Wi-Fi and locker — [Bharatnotes](https://bharatnotes.com/faq/study-environment/study-library-options-delhi/).
- **Study-with-me began in South Korea** and spread to Japan, China, Vietnam and the West — [Part 1, citing SCMP](https://scmp.com/week-asia/lifestyle-culture/article/3121568/study-buddies-south-korean-youtubers-take-cram-sessions); Bilibili made it a top live category in 2019 — [Part 1, citing KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/how-bilibili-kuaishou-douyin-are-fostering-chinese-youngsters-education).
- **Study logs: Japan social-but-gentle, Korea ranked.** Studyplus emphasises timeline sharing and encouragement, can be anonymous, and has a teacher-facing version — [ReseMom](https://resemom.jp/release/prtimes/20250701/149227.html); [atpress](https://www.atpress.ne.jp/news/38814). YPT adds real-time category rankings and phone lock, and has spread to Singapore — [AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/ypt-study-group/1441909643?country=ca); [Raffles Press](https://rafflespress.com/2025/02/13/yawns-pains-and-tears-on-ypt/).
- **China's FOC is bundled with co-presence.** Tomato ToDo combines app lockout with online study rooms (#429) — [Part 1, citing Woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/evaluating/3069778.html).
- **Western FOC is subscription productivity software.** Freedom ($39.99/yr), Opal ($99.99/yr), Cold Turkey Pro (about $39 one-time), Focusmate Plus ($8–12/month) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md); [Focusmate FAQ](https://www.focusmate.com/faq).
- **Rankings in national mocks.** China: Fenbi national online mocks (#392); Japan: Kawaijuku/Sundai pass-likelihood grades (#422, BK); India: CAT mock percentiles (#186) and Oliveboard percentiles (#181) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md).
- **Exam towns in Korea and India**; Korea's gosichon shrank after the bar exam changed and lectures moved online (BK) — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md).
- **India's group layer is Telegram and Reddit**, coaching-run and largely unmoderated, with documented leak and scam problems — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1069714/how-a-messaging-app-that-helps-students-prepare-for-exams-enabled-paper-leaks); Western equivalents are Discord servers (Study Together, StudyStream) — [Discord listing](https://discord.com/servers/study-together--595999872222756885).
- **LHL is English-first and Western-authored** (Oakley, Willingham, Learning Scientists, studytube), though Oakley's MOOC has a Chinese version (#287) — [Part 2 appendix](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md). WOOP is translated into all 11 South African official languages — [Part 1, citing UCT](https://www.news.uct.ac.za/features/teachingandlearning/-article/2022-03-31-woop-goal-realisation-app-now-in-all-11-official-languages).
- **Mindset interventions are delivered by schools in the US** as free short online modules — [Northwestern IPR](https://ipr.northwestern.edu/news/2019/tipton-growth-mindset-study.html); India's equivalent is a national PM town hall — [Part 1](../../reports/Exam%20success%20tools%20and%20methods.md).
- **Product-type split (Part 2 counts).** PLN mainly methods and planner apps; LHL books, courses and creators; FOC apps; HAB a secondary feature of apps plus books; SPC places and websites; GRP methods and communities; CMP a feature inside apps and test series — [Part 2 report](../../reports/Exam%20success%20central%20ideas.md).

### Inferences
- The East Asian pattern bundles FOC + SPC + CMP + PLN in one product (YPT; Tomato ToDo); Western products tend to sell each idea separately (a blocker, a timer, a co-working site).
- Cost structure differs: East Asian and Indian co-presence is mostly paid per seat in a building; Western co-presence is mostly a software subscription; study-with-me videos are free everywhere.
- Comparison is culturally normalised in East Asian and Indian exam systems (national mock ranks), while Western consumer apps frame it as a game (leagues) with an opt-out.

### Gaps
- No systematic usage data comparing regions (e.g., share of students using study rooms by country).
- Little on Latin America, Africa, Europe outside the UK, and the Middle East for any of these seven ideas.
- Hindi- and regional-language versions of LHL content: not searched this round.
