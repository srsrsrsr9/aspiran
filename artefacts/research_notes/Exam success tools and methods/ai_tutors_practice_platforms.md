# AI tutors, study assistants, and practice / question-bank platforms (worldwide, late 2026)

Scope: AI tutors and study assistants, adaptive practice platforms, mock-test and past-paper tools, essay and answer-feedback tools, and exam-specific prep. Pure flashcard apps are out of scope because another researcher covers them.

Research date: 1 Nov 2026. 16 tool calls. Sources marked **[vendor]** are company claims, and **[competitor]** marks a rival's blog. Entries marked **(not verified this session)** come from the researcher's prior knowledge and were not checked against a source in this pass. The report writer should treat them as context only, or verify them before stating them as fact.

## For each tool: name, type, URL, price model, what it does, core idea

### Takeaway
More than 50 tools fall into six groups. They rest on a small set of core ideas: (1) Socratic or guided questioning (the 2025 "study modes" from OpenAI, Google and Anthropic; Khanmigo; NotebookLM Learning Guide), (2) adaptive diagnosis plus mastery learning (ALEKS, Squirrel AI, Sparx, Khan Academy), (3) practice testing with detailed explanations (UWorld, AMBOSS, Magoosh, mock-test series), (4) retrieval practice and spacing (Seneca, NotebookLM quizzes), (5) source-grounded summarising and quiz generation (NotebookLM, StudyFetch, Turbo AI, Mindgrasp), and (6) instant solutions from a photo (Photomath, Gauth, Brainly, Google Lens). The field changed fast in 2025 and 2026. General LLMs added study modes, Quizlet Q-Chat and the standalone Socratic app were shut down, Chegg collapsed, and the medical question banks added AI copilots.

### Inferences
- Since mid-2025, Socratic questioning has become the default answer to "AI does your homework." OpenAI, Google and Anthropic all shipped study modes within about four weeks of each other, so a study mode alone does not set a product apart.
- Source-grounded generation (upload notes, get quizzes, flashcards and audio) is now a commodity. NotebookLM gives it away free, and StudyFetch, Turbo AI, Mindgrasp and Coconote compete mostly on price.
- In high-stakes professional exams, the established question banks (UWorld, AMBOSS) are adding AI copilots on top of their own vetted questions. They are not being replaced. Trusted, exam-realistic questions and explanations seem to be what keeps users with them.
- For UPSC, AI Mains answer evaluation is a crowded vendor category with no independent validation, which leaves room for an honest, editor-checked approach.

## Which tools explicitly cite learning science? (vendor-cited studies)

### Takeaway
The vendors most explicit about learning science are ALEKS (Knowledge Space Theory and mastery), Seneca (retrieval practice, spacing and interleaving, with its own 2018 study), Khanmigo and the LLM study modes (Socratic questioning), Squirrel AI (knowledge-gap mastery), and Sparx (a claimed Cambridge evaluation). Nearly every outcome claim found is vendor-generated or secondhand. The two strongest independent studies are about AI tutoring in general, not a branded product: Kestin et al. (2025, positive, purpose-built tutor) and Bastani et al. (negative for unguarded ChatGPT).

### Cited Findings
- **ALEKS** cites Knowledge Space Theory (Doignon and Falmagne, 1985) and claims a mastery rate above 90%. Its research is written by ALEKS-affiliated authors (a 2021 preprint and a 2022 EDM paper that adds a neural network to the first assessment). — [McGraw Hill KST paper](https://www.mheducation.com/content/dam/mhe/research/prek-12/essa-tier-4/aleks-practical-perspective-knowledge-space-theory.pdf); [EDM 2022](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2022/proceedings/2022.EDM-industry-track.109/index.html).
- **Seneca** cites retrieval practice and interleaving (Rohrer et al. 2015) and its own 2018 *Impact* paper ("double the marks"). — [Seneca blog](https://senecalearning.com/en-GB/blog/the-best-way-to-revise-according-to-neuroscience/).
- **Sparx Maths** cites a Cambridge University evaluation. The primary source was not found. — [School PDF](https://www.westfield.chorustrust.org/_site/data/files/documents/learning/courses/ks4-maths/BCEB49A91250FB95A5399F500B89FC50.pdf).
- **Save My Exams** claims "+2.6 grades" with no methodology given. — [Save My Exams](https://www.savemyexams.com/learning-hub/sme-articles/save-my-exams-vs-sparx-maths/).
- **Squirrel AI** cites its own comparisons, saying it beat human teachers by about 5% (2019). — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2019/08/02/131198/china-squirrel-has-started-a-grand-experiment-in-ai-education-it-could-reshape-how-the/).
- **Gemini Guided Learning** is built on LearnLM, Google's models tuned on learning-science principles. — [Maginative](https://www.maginative.com/google-launches-guided-learning-for-gemini-joining-the-ai-tutor-arms-race).
- **NotebookLM** makes retrieval practice and explanatory feedback concrete through quizzes and the "Explain why wrong" button, though no study was cited. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/google-labs/notebooklm-student-features/).
- **AnswerWriting.com** claims "+23% in 30 days." **PrepAiro** claims calibration against more than 10,000 human-graded answers. Both are vendor claims. — [AnswerWriting](https://answerwriting.anthroholic.com/upsc-answer-evaluator); [PrepAiro](https://prepairo.ai/upsc/llms/upsc-ai-mains-evaluation/).
- **Independent study, positive (Kestin et al. 2025, *Scientific Reports* 15:17458).** A randomised crossover trial with 194 Harvard physics students. A purpose-built AI tutor, designed on teaching best practice, produced median learning gains more than double those of an in-class active-learning session. Students took less time (median 49 minutes against 75) and reported higher engagement. Limits: one course at one elite university, a short-term test on two topics, and not a comparison with human tutors. — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/); [ETC Journal review](https://etcjournal.com/2025/11/10/review-of-kestin-et-al-s-june-2025-harvard-study-on-ai-tutoring/); [iatroX summary](https://www.iatrox.com/blog/harvard-ai-tutor-trial-what-it-showed-question-banks).

### Inferences
- What reliably predicts results is the technique, not the brand: retrieval practice, mastery learning, worked examples and Socratic scaffolding. The product claims themselves are mostly unvalidated, so the validation phase should look at the techniques and treat product claims with caution.
- The Kestin result depended on a tutor carefully designed around good teaching practice. It does not support the idea that "any chatbot tutors well."

### Gaps
- No independent evaluation was found for Up Learn, Save My Exams, Seneca (outside its own study), StudyFetch, Turbo AI or any UPSC AI evaluator.
- The Sparx "Cambridge" study and the Seneca 2018 *Impact* paper were not retrieved.

## Notable concerns (AI doing homework, "answer apps" that may harm learning)

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence of harm is Bastani et al.: unguarded GPT-4 made practice scores better but exam scores 17% worse once access was removed, while a tutor with guardrails avoided that harm. Answer apps (Photomath, Gauth, Brainly, Chegg) worry teachers because they skip the effort that produces learning. No controlled study of those specific apps was found. AI-authorship detection is unreliable and produces false flags.

### Cited Findings
- **Bastani et al., "Generative AI Can Harm Learning."** A field experiment with nearly 1,000 Turkish high-school maths students. With access, practice scores rose 48% for GPT Base and 127% for GPT Tutor. After access was removed, the GPT Base group scored 17% worse on exams than the control group, and the GPT Tutor group scored about the same as the control. The authors say students used the AI as a "crutch." Limits: one subject at one school. Publication status is reported both as an SSRN preprint and as PNAS 2025, and was not confirmed from PNAS. — [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education); [Stanford SCALE repository](https://scale.stanford.edu/ai/repository/generative-ai-can-harm-learning); [PDF](https://nmdprojects.net/teaching_resources/bastani_generative_ai_can_harm_learning.pdf).
- **Answer apps.** Teachers and parents worry students "skip the learning part." The step-by-step views are argued both ways, as a way to check your own work or as a way to copy. Accuracy errors have been reported. ### Inferences
- Guardrails matter more than whether a tool uses AI at all. A tutor that gives hints and makes students produce their own answers avoided the harm, while plain answer access caused it. For Aspiran this supports Chotu's Socratic approach and checks like Sparx's bookwork checks.
- AI evaluation of UPSC Mains answers carries a similar risk. Students may tune their answers to the AI's rubric. No sources on this were found, so it is an inference only.

### Gaps
- No controlled studies were found on Photomath, Gauth or Brainly specifically.
- No evidence was found on how often students switch study modes off, or on long-term retention with study modes.
- Gauth's ownership, user numbers and any regulatory issues were not verified.
