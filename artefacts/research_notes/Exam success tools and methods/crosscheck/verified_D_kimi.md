## (c) What East Asian exam culture adds

East Asian exam culture turns habits that Indian and global tools leave to the student into products. The wrong-answer notebook (错题本 / 오답노트) is a shared ritual, and Zuoyebang even sells a printer for it. Silence is something you pay for (Chinese self-study rooms, Korean study cafés, Japan's Benkyo Café), and study time is public and social (YPT rankings, Studyplus logs, Bilibili's "study with me" rooms). Star-teacher video lectures (Megastudy, Toshin, Study Sapuri) and state-run free lectures tied to the exam (Korea's EBSi) show both the paid and the public way to reach millions cheaply, while Fenbi's national mocks show how much exam takers want to see where they stand. The regulatory lessons are sharp. China's 2021 Double Reduction policy wiped out for-profit K-9 tutoring overnight (OneSmart collapsed within months), its regulator fined the biggest edtechs for misleading ads, and the education ministry pulled photo-answer apps for "skipping the thinking"; Offcn's "pass or refund" classes ended in years of unpaid refunds, and Korea blocked a merger that would have created one dominant civil-service prep firm. For Aspiran, borrow the habits (mistake notebooks, quiet shared study time, honest mocks) but keep away from rankings, pass guarantees and answer-lookup shortcuts. Those are the things East Asian regulators and critics went after.
