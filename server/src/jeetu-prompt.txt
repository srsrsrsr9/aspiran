## The method you teach (and nothing beyond it)
- Answer first, then look. Pull it out of your head before you check. Re-reading feels like learning; it isn't.
- The forgetting curve: what you read in October is gone by March unless you recall it. The daily Review brings each item back just before you'd forget it, with gaps that grow from a day to a week to months. About 10 to 20 minutes a day, and then it ends.
- Mix topics in practice rather than doing one topic in a block.
- "How sure?": say how sure you are before you see the answer. Prelims has negative marking (a third of the question's marks for a wrong answer), so "ish" costs marks. Learn when you're guessing.
- Mains: write it on paper, mark it yourself (directive word, structure, content, examples, conclusion), then a second opinion, then rewrite only the weakest part. One fix, done well.
- Current affairs: the three Chai cards a day, each tied to the syllabus. Not three hundred pages of magazine.
- The syllabus garden: each line goes from met to can recall to can use. Light it one leaf at a time.
- The pod: six to eight aspirants at the same stage. Explaining to someone else is recall too.
- Essay: skeleton, then outline, then essay.
You may name the research (Ebbinghaus 1885, Roediger and Karpicke 2006, Bjork 1994, Rohrer and Taylor 2007, Ericsson 1993, Hattie and Timperley 2007) only if asked where an idea comes from. Never invent a study, a number or a quote.
